🧬 Molecular Biology & Biliary Cascade

🧬 MOLECULAR BIOLOGY & BILIARY CASCADE

MASLD has 5 biologically distinct subtypes — genetic forms progress faster without metabolic risk factors

Mayo Clinic + Virginia Tech identified five reproducible subtypes of MASLD (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease) in 4,600+ patients using exome + clinical data. Genetic subtypes progress to advanced liver disease at higher rates even without obesity or diabetes. ARPKD-CHF relevance: (1) Children with ARPKD-CHF develop portal hypertension and biliary fibrosis without metabolic risk factors — same pattern as genetically-driven MASLD subtypes. (2) MASLD can be superimposed on ARPKD-CHF in adolescence — this subtyping framework could distinguish which disease is driving progression. (3) The Mayo Clinic Research Data Atlas + Tapestry Study (100,000+ exomes) that powered this research is the same infrastructure our contacts Harris and Hanna work within — could theoretically be applied to ARPKD precision subtyping. (4) Next steps include GLP-1 agonist testing by subtype — GLP-1 agonists have emerging hepatoprotective data; safety in ARPKD-CHF with portal hypertension is unstudied and warrants monitoring.

Sultana Priya T, Tian S, Klee EW, Lazaridis KN et al. Nat Commun 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-77410-6 · Published September 2, 2026

Maralixibat (IBAT inhibitor) EXPAND trial — being evaluated across rare cholestatic liver diseases including biliary atresia

Maralixibat (Livmarli, Mirum Pharmaceuticals) is FDA-approved for cholestatic pruritus in Alagille syndrome (≥3 months) and PFIC (≥5 years). The EXPAND Phase 3 trial is now evaluating it across additional rare cholestatic liver diseases including biliary atresia. Mechanism: inhibits ileal bile acid transporter → reduces systemic bile acid pool → reduces cholestatic injury and pruritus. Post-hoc data shows responders demonstrated improved event-free and transplant-free survival vs historic controls. ARPKD-CHF relevance: (1) ARPKD-CHF involves bile acid accumulation driving the p38δ-YAP fibrosis axis (already in framework). Odevixibat (sister IBAT inhibitor) already Tier 2 in our framework with biliary fibrosis data in Mdr2-/- model. (2) Maralixibat is FDA-approved in children from 3 months — same age range as ARPKD presentation. (3) If EXPAND expands to CHF or ARPKD, this is the most immediately available FDA-approved bile acid-reducing agent for pediatric biliary disease. (4) Safety flag: In ALGS trials, ALT >3×ULN occurred in 8-11% of long-term users — same concern as odevixibat. Monitor hepatic function. Not yet tested in ARPKD-CHF specifically. Tier 2 candidate — biliary cholestatic disease validation present; PKHD1-specific testing needed.

EXPAND Trial — NCT04524390 · Quintero-Bernabeu J et al. Front Med 2026. DOI: 10.3389/fmed.2025.1707258 (maralixibat in Alagille — fibrosis + portal hypertension signals) · Peverelle M et al. Aliment Pharmacol Ther 2026 — IBAT inhibitor review

Novel compound heterozygous PKHD1 mutations in Chinese ARPKD pedigree — genotype-phenotype analysis

Shanghai Children's Hospital identified novel compound heterozygous PKHD1 variants (c.5850_5851insTTCAT; p.Gly1951Phefs*25 + c.8710G>A; p.Glu2904Lys) in a Chinese ARPKD pedigree presenting with bilateral renal cysts + hepatic fibrosis. Conservation analysis confirmed both variants in conserved regions. The frameshift (truncating) allele caused protein truncation; the missense allele caused polarity changes impairing protein stability. ARPKD-CHF relevance: (1) Adds to the growing published case series of genotype-phenotype correlations in ARPKD — consistent with our framework that compound heterozygous truncating + missense produces hepatorenal phenotype. (2) p.Glu2904Lys is at AA 2904 — Zone 2 (AA 1838-2624 boundary) — intermediate hepatic outcome zone per Burgmaier. (3) Secondary structure prediction methodology used here is the same approach that could be applied to assess p.Met3642Ile structural impact. (4) Consistent with the general principle: frameshift (truncating-equivalent) + missense = presentation with both liver and kidney disease.

Zhao J, Yu K, Huang Y. Front Med 13:1741795, 2026. DOI: 10.3389/fmed.2026.1741795. PMID: 41728609

⭐ EFHD1 — new protein linking ER-mitochondria contacts to liver inflammation and fibrosis via maladaptive antiviral response

Source: Eberhardt DR, Chaudhuri D et al. (University of Utah). J Clin Invest 2026. DOI: 10.1172/JCI204023. Published September 2026.

What EFHD1 does: Stabilizes ER-mitochondria membrane contact sites. In MASH, excessive EFHD1 activity keeps ER and mitochondria connected too long → mitochondrial fragmentation → double-stranded RNA escapes into cytoplasm → PKR antiviral pathway fires (mistaking internal damage for viral invasion) → inflammation and fibrosis. Blocking EFHD1 reduced liver injury in mice and human liver models. Mendelian randomization supports causal role in humans.

ARPKD-CHF relevance — four connections:
(1) ICD15/EFHD1 connection: FPC's ICD15 C-terminal domain is a mitochondrial fragment proposed to regulate mitochondrial function. FPC loss → disrupted ICD15 → potential ER-mitochondria contact site dysregulation → EFHD1 may be elevated in FPC-deficient cholangiocytes. Testing EFHD1 in PKHD1-deficient biliary cells is a logical next experiment. Researcher routing: Qian F (ICD15 — University of Maryland), Outeda P (FPC mitochondria — University of Maryland).
(2) PKR → STAT3 connection: PKR (double-stranded RNA-activated protein kinase) is a known STAT3 activator. PKR → STAT3 → SPP1 → fibrosis = plausible new cascade node connecting EFHD1/mitochondrial damage directly into our confirmed STAT3/SPP1 fibrosis cascade.
(3) Mitochondrial dsRNA → innate immune activation: Second independent confirmation of the mitochondrial stress → innate immune activation → fibrosis sequence. Complements our existing NLRP3 cascade node.
(4) New contact: Dipayan Chaudhuri MD PhD, University of Utah (chaudhuri.dipayan@hsc.utah.edu) — $3.7M NIH R01 (2025) specifically on EFHD1 and hepatocyte injury. Cardiologist studying liver — same dual-organ interest as ARPKD-CHF.

Tier 2-3 — MASH model confirmed; upgrade to Tier 2 if EFHD1 confirmed elevated in FPC-deficient cholangiocytes.

Eberhardt DR, Chaudhuri D et al. J Clin Invest 2026. DOI: 10.1172/JCI204023

🦠 Gut-Liver Axis & Biliary Microbiome

🦠 GUT-LIVER AXIS & BILIARY MICROBIOME

⭐⭐ Gut microbiota-derived imidazole propionate (ImP) drives biliary fibrosis via p38γ/δ → mTORC1 in PSC cholangiocytes — direct ARPKD-CHF cascade confirmation

Source: Molinaro A, Braadland PR, Carpino G (Sapienza Rome — biliary disease specialist), Juran BD (Mayo Clinic PSC genetics), Overi D, … Hov JR (Oslo University Hospital + Mayo Clinic + Karolinska + Sapienza Rome). Nature Metabolism 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s42255-026-01600-1. Published September 4, 2026. Open access.

What this paper shows: ImP — a gut bacterial metabolite produced from histidine via the urdA gene — is elevated in PSC patients across 4 independent cohorts (Norway x2, Sweden, USA; n=1,003 PSC patients). High ImP predicts reduced transplant-free survival in PSC. Cholangiocyte organoids from PSC patients treated with ImP showed elevated TNF, IL-6, fibronectin and impaired morphology. Chronic ImP administration induced portal inflammation, biliary fibrosis and ductular reaction in mice. p38γ/δ double knockout mice were completely protected — genetic proof that p38γ/δ is the obligate mediator.

ARPKD-CHF connections:
(1) ⭐ p38δ already in our framework: Bile acid → p38δ → YAP → CDK1 fibrosis cascade is confirmed in our framework. This paper proves p38γ/δ is the exact mechanism driving cholangiocyte fibrosis in biliary cholestatic disease by genetic knockout. Strongest independent confirmation of p38 as a biliary fibrosis therapeutic target yet published.
(2) Direct cholangiocyte action confirmed: Tested in PSC-patient cholangiocyte organoids — bile duct epithelial cells, the same cell type affected in ARPKD-CHF. p38 → mTORC1 operates directly in cholangiocytes, not just hepatocytes.
(3) mTORC1 in cholangiocytes: mTOR inhibitors are contraindicated in ARPKD (already in framework — 3 independent biliary harm mechanisms). This paper confirms mTORC1 is activated in PSC cholangiocytes and drives fibrosis — same mechanism likely operating in ARPKD-CHF.
(4) Gut-liver axis in ARPKD-CHF: ARPKD patients have gut dysbiosis from recurrent cholangitis and antibiotic cycles — low-diversity microbiota that favors ImP production. ImP levels in ARPKD-CHF are unstudied — a new open question for the network.
(5) Pirfenidone (p38 inhibitor): Already shown to reduce cholestatic liver injury in mice; PSC pilot trial showed trend toward reduced fibrosis. Potential therapeutic for ARPKD-CHF biliary fibrosis via p38 pathway.
(6) Lazaridis (Mayo) co-author: Already in our network via the MASLD 5-subtypes paper. The US PSC cohort in this paper (n=609) was from the Mayo PSC Scientific Community Resource — Lazaridis infrastructure. Two separate findings this week connecting to the same Mayo contact.

Tier 1 — upgraded Sep 10, 2026. Full paper retrieved: causal mechanism established in cells + animal models, confirmed in 1,003 human PSC patients. ImP engages cell surface receptor on cholangiocytes → phosphorylation cascade → p38 activation → inflammatory cytokines + profibrotic programs. Blocking pathway components substantially blunted all effects. Three therapeutic angles: (a) reduce ImP production via microbiome targeting, (b) block ImP receptor on cholangiocytes, (c) inhibit p38 locally in biliary epithelium — paper notes biliary compartment isolation "might allow more localized approaches." New network contacts: Carpino G (biliary fibrosis, Sapienza Rome — potential direct outreach). New open question: is ImP elevated in ARPKD-CHF patients?

Molinaro A, Carpino G, Hov JR et al. Nat Metab 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s42255-026-01600-1 · Open access

⭐ SPP1+ macrophages confirmed as conserved pro-fibrotic subset across lung, liver, kidney AND heart — spleen-liver macrophage axis directly relevant to ARPKD-CHF splenomegaly (September 8, 2026)

Qiu W, Huang Y, Chen J et al. (Hangzhou Medical College). Mol Biol Rep 53(1):1549, 2026. DOI: 10.1007/s11033-026-12723-9. Tier 1 — Framework validation.

SPP1+ macrophages are the conserved pro-fibrotic end-state across all four fibrotic organ systems by single-cell omics — the strongest multi-organ independent validation of SPP1 as a therapeutic target ever published. Key ARPKD-CHF implications: (1) TREM2+ SPP1+ macrophages are scar-associated in periportal niches — SPP1 silencing in TREM2+ macrophages suppresses fibrosis (cardiac model). (2) Spleen-liver macrophage axis — splenocyte-derived macrophages travel to the liver independently of bone marrow recruitment and drive scarring. In ARPKD-CHF, splenomegaly is present in >80% of portal hypertension patients. This means the enlarged ARPKD-CHF spleen may compound cholangiocyte-driven fibrosis via a second spleen-liver mechanism. Both mechanisms testable in Pkhd1del4/del4 mouse with splenomegaly. (3) CCL2/CCR2 recruitment — seventh independent confirmation across four organ systems.

⚠️ ARPKD-CHF specific caveat (from Jiao/Zhu Commun Biol 2026 — full paper read Sep 11, 2026): In PKHD1-specific ARPKD-CHF, OPN co-localization with F4/80 macrophage marker was almost absent. Cholangiocytes — not macrophages — are the dominant SPP1 source in ARPKD-CHF specifically. The macrophage SPP1 biology described in Qiu et al. is real for other fibrotic diseases but plays a secondary role in ARPKD-CHF. The primary therapeutic target is cholangiocyte-secreted SPP1 → α8β1 on HSCs. The TREM2+ macrophage questions remain valid as secondary research questions.

New open questions: Are TREM2+ SPP1+ macrophages present in ARPKD-CHF periportal niches? Does ARPKD-CHF splenomegaly produce pro-fibrotic macrophages that compound biliary fibrosis? Routing: Strazzabosco (Pkhd1del4/del4 + splenomegaly — the only model to test both simultaneously).

⭐ FPCct sequesters Src at plasma membrane to suppress STAT3-driven cystogenesis — FPCct protein domain as new therapeutic lead (August 28, 2026)

Hassan F, Hahnenstein ST, Dafinger C, Liebau MC, Ziegler WH et al. (Hannover Medical School / Cologne). Am J Physiol Cell Physiol 2026. DOI: 10.1152/ajpcell.00086.2026. Published August 28, 2026. Tier 1 — Directly confirms and extends core cascade mechanism. 11 days old as of newsletter date.

⚠️ IMPORTANT CAVEAT (full paper read Sep 11, 2026): This paper uses pl-MDCK cells — a canine kidney collecting duct cell line. There is zero biliary, liver, or cholangiocyte data in this paper. All ARPKD-CHF biliary application is our extrapolation based on shared FPC biology.

Key findings from full paper read: (1) FPCct sequesters Src at the apical plasma membrane — membrane-bound uncleaved form is the active suppressor (no cytoplasmic cleavage fragments detected). (2) Quantified: FPCct reduces pSTAT3-Y705 by 81%/55% (KO#1/KO#3) and pSrc-Y416 by 60%/45% under cAMP stimulation. (3) STAT3-dependent transcription reduced 56%/54% (luciferase assay). (4) FPCct is ~2.25× more effective than pharmacological Src inhibition (PP2): ~90 Pa vs ~40 Pa luminal pressure reduction. (5) STAT3 auto-transcription creates self-reinforcing loop — FPCct breaks this at Src level before amplification. (6) No clinical STAT3 trials in PKD — confirmed gap. (7) Two Zone 3 FPC-CTT patients → portal hypertension + CKD G4 in adolescence. (8) ⭐ Fatima Hassan (first author) is now at Yale Nephrology — same institution as Strazzabosco. Natural experiment for biliary FPCct validation exists at Yale. Note (4) re Src/liver cysts: that finding is from Sweeney et al. background work (PCK rats/bpk mice) cited in this paper — not a new Hassan finding.

New therapeutic concept — FPCct protein domain delivery: Delivering the FPC C-terminal domain (~192 AA) rather than full-length FPC (4074 AA) is sufficient to restore the SRC/STAT3 brake. A protein domain construct — not gene therapy — as a therapeutic approach. Biliary application via LNP or AAV is theoretically feasible. Completely novel modality.

Routing: Ziegler WH (Ziegler.Wolfgang@mh-hannover.de, Hannover Medical School) — corresponding author, PI of BOTH FPCct/SRC/STAT3 paper AND SPP1 knockdown paper. Understands the full cascade from cause to consequence. Liebau MC (RWTH Aachen) — co-author; Zoom call scheduled Sep 14/15.

🧪 Gene Therapy & Base Editing

🧪 GENE THERAPY & BASE EDITING

⭐⭐ FDA Plausible Mechanism Framework — First formal approval pathway for individualized genome editing in ultra-rare pediatric genetic disease (February 23, 2026)

FDA Draft Guidance, CBER + CDER. February 23, 2026. Inspired by Baby KJ (CHOP, 2024). Confirmed by Drs. Ahrens-Nicklas and Musunuru (CHOP). Tier 1 — Regulatory. This is the most important regulatory development for ARPKD-CHF gene therapy published in 2026.

The Plausible Mechanism Framework creates a formal FDA approval pathway for individualized therapies targeting ultra-rare genetic diseases where randomized controlled trials are not feasible. Three requirements: (1) therapy directly targets the genetic or molecular cause, (2) biological mechanism is well-understood and scientifically validated, (3) therapy successfully modifies the disease-causing pathway. All three are met for PKHD1 His3124Tyr + ABE correction.

Why this is more important than GSD1a, BEAM-302, or YOLT-101 for our situation: Those precedents established surrogate endpoints and biomarker-based approval for gene therapy broadly. This framework is specifically designed for N-of-1 or ultra-small population genome editing in pediatric disease. His3124Tyr affects fewer than 800 patients worldwide — it fits the framework exactly. No large trial required. Plausible mechanism + evidence of pathway modification accepted as the approval standard.

CHOP ecosystem is the infrastructure: Ahrens-Nicklas (co-creator of this framework) + Guay-Woodford (HRFD PI) + ARPA-H THRIVE + HRFD enrollment = the complete regulatory and clinical pathway. HRFD enrollment is the entry point to this entire ecosystem. It is now even more urgent than before.

⭐ BEAM-302 Phase 1/2 — 94% corrected M-AAT as fraction of total AAT; 84% mutant Z-AAT reduction; all patients above protective threshold at up to 12 months; Part B liver disease cohort; accelerated approval pathway confirmed (September 8, 2026) — company-reported conference data, not peer-reviewed

Beam Therapeutics. BEAM-302 Phase 1/2 updated data, ERS Congress 2026. GlobeNewswire, September 8, 2026. Tier 1.

BEAM-302 corrects the E342K (PiZ) missense mutation in SERPINA1 using an adenine base editor delivered via LNP — A→G correction of a missense causing protein misfolding and organ disease. Identical chemistry to what would be needed for His3124Tyr (c.9370C>T, A→G) in PKHD1. Key results from 29 efficacy-evaluable patients (38 total dosed): (1) 94% corrected M-AAT as fraction of total AAT with 84% reduction in mutant Z-AAT, all patients above 11 µM protective threshold at up to 12 months. (2) 84% reduction in mutant Z-AAT, durable to 18 months — the longest human liver ABE durability data published. (3) AAT rises physiologically during infection — the correction restores normal gene regulation, not just a fixed level. (4) Part B specifically enrolls patients with mild to severe liver disease — the most analogous population to ARPKD-CHF. (5) FDA accelerated approval pathway confirmed: primary endpoint = AAT biomarkers at 12 months, ~50 additional patients in global pivotal cohort now dosing.

⚠️ Safety flag: One Part B patient with underlying AATD-related liver disease experienced Grade 3 ALT/AST elevation (transient, no bilirubin increase, no treatment required). This is the third Grade 3 liver enzyme event across liver-directed gene editing trials (CTX310 CRISPR-Cas9 NEJM Aug 2026; YOLT-101 ABE Nat Med 2026). ABE in patients with pre-existing liver disease → transient hepatotoxicity confirmed. ARPKD-CHF patients have elevated baseline liver enzymes — this safety signal requires a dedicated monitoring protocol in any future PKHD1 ABE trial.

ARPKD-CHF connections: (1) A→G ABE correction of liver-expressed missense — identical to His3124Tyr strategy. (2) 94% corrected M-AAT fraction at up to 12 months from single dose — exceeds Krappitz/Kaminski 5% sufficient threshold by 18-fold. (3) Fourth regulatory precedent: biomarker endpoint → accelerated approval in rare genetic liver disease (GSD1a Aug 2026, YOLT-101 Nat Med 2026, BEAM-302 Sep 2026). (4) Grade 3 ALT in liver disease patient — safety protocol needed. New outreach target: Beam Therapeutics has no ARPKD program and has the exact ABE+LNP platform validated in humans. Routing: Amy Simon MD (hmanning@beamtx.com investor contact), Xiaogang Li (Mayo — already in network).

⭐ Mayo Clinic PKD1 base editing — first in vivo kidney base editing, liver health also improved — Xiaogang Li lab (Jan 2026)

Source: Li X et al. (Mayo Clinic — Genesis Initiative). Nature Communications 2026. Published January 30, 2026.

What this paper shows: Single-dose AAV-delivered adenine base editor corrected a PKD1 missense mutation in a significant proportion of kidney cells in ADPKD preclinical models. Two editors tested: one organ-broad, one kidney-specific. Early treatment resulted in markedly reduced kidney cyst growth, better kidney function, less heart enlargement, improved liver health, and extended survival. No harmful off-target edits or significant immune reactions detected.

ARPKD-CHF connections:
(1) Direct precedent for PKHD1 base editing: PKD1 and PKHD1 are both large transmembrane ciliary proteins with missense variants correctable by ABE. The PKD1 missense correction strategy maps directly onto our His3124Tyr (c.9370C>T) — a confirmed ABE substrate. Both PKD genes have been successfully base-edited by the Krappitz/Kaminski group (Mol Ther 2025) and now by the Li/Mayo group.
(2) Xiaogang Li is in our network — he is listed in our researcher network (Mayo Clinic). This paper comes from his lab. He has not yet replied to our newsletter. This paper gives us a specific, highly relevant entry point for outreach.
(3) Kidney-specific editor strategy: The kidney-specific promoter restriction (KRT19 equivalent for biliary cells) validates our framework approach — organ-specific editing reduces off-target risk. Mayo is now testing this in kidney; CCHMC/Krappitz could do the same for liver/biliary cells with KRT19-ABE.
(4) Ongoing work: Li's team is now developing non-viral LNP delivery as an alternative to AAV, and testing whether editing works after cysts have already formed — the clinically relevant scenario for ARPKD-CHF.

Tier 1 for ARPKD context — closest published analog to PKHD1 base editing, from a lab in our network.

⭐ World's first in vivo liver base editing Phase 1 clinical trial — PCSK9 inactivation, 74% sustained reduction at 24 weeks (March 2026)

Source: Wan P, Tang S, Lin D, Xia Q et al. (Renji Hospital, Shanghai / YolTech Therapeutics). Nature Medicine 32(3):1045-1051, March 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s41591-026-04254-4. NCT06458010.

What this trial shows: Six patients with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH) received escalating doses of YOLT-101 — adenine base editor targeting PCSK9 in hepatocytes, delivered via GalNAc-modified LNPs. At 0.6 mg/kg: 74.4% sustained PCSK9 reduction and 52.3% LDL-C reduction at 24 weeks. No serious adverse events, no grade ≥3 toxicity, no off-target editing detected. Transient infusion-related reactions and mild liver enzyme elevations only.

ARPKD-CHF connections:
(1) First human proof-of-concept for in vivo liver base editing — the same technology class (ABE + LNP delivery to liver) that would be used to correct His3124Tyr in PKHD1 hepatocytes/cholangiocytes. Safety profile is clean at 24 weeks — the critical near-term safety question for any ARPKD liver base editing program.
(2) GalNAc-LNP vs AAV: This trial uses LNP delivery (not AAV), avoiding the AAV immune response and capsid constraints. LNP delivery to liver is now clinically validated for base editing at the human level. This is directly relevant to the Krappitz/Kaminski ABEmax approach — which uses AAV but could consider LNP as an alternative.
(3) BEAM-304 (PKU) IND cleared June 2026 — Beam Therapeutics received FDA clearance for BEAM-304, an LNP-delivered ABE for phenylketonuria (liver disease, missense mutation). This is the second LNP liver base editing IND in 2026. The regulatory path for liver ABE is now established.
(4) Platform strategy: Beam's FDA-cleared framework allows multiple mutation-specific editors within one development platform — exactly what PKHD1 would need (His3124Tyr + Met3642Ile = two editors, one platform).

Tier 1 — Direct human clinical validation of the technology class needed for PKHD1 base editing.

⭐ FDA approves first liver-directed AAV gene therapy for pediatric ultra-rare liver disease (GSD1a) — surrogate endpoint accepted — August 19, 2026

Source: FDA accelerated approval of pariglasgene brecaparvovec-opnr (Genglycos, Ultragenyx). Approved August 19, 2026. For patients aged 8+ with glycogen storage disease type Ia (GSD1a). AAV8 vector delivering functional G6PC gene to liver. Single IV infusion. NCT03517085 (Phase 1/2) → Phase 3 confirmatory trial underway.

What this approval means: GSD1a is an ultra-rare pediatric liver metabolic disease (~600 US patients) caused by a single gene defect (G6PC). FDA granted accelerated approval based on a surrogate endpoint — reduction in daily cornstarch intake — rather than a hard clinical outcome. Designations: RMAT, Fast Track, Rare Pediatric Disease Priority Review Voucher. Elevated transaminases were the most common adverse event (hepatic immune response). 29% hypertriglyceridemia vs 8% placebo.

ARPKD-CHF connections — four regulatory precedents:
(1) First FDA-approved AAV liver gene therapy for a pediatric ultra-rare liver disease — similar patient population size to ARPKD (~600 US). FDA accepted surrogate endpoint (dietary biomarker) rather than hard clinical outcome. ARPKD-CHF has strong surrogate candidates: ELF score, MRE liver stiffness, serum SPP1 — all already validated in biliary disease by IMAGE-ARPKD and Vesterhus/Fujinaga precedents.
(2) Cebotaru's AAV1-Δ27-264-CFTR — direct analog modality (liver-directed AAV, single dose, genetic pediatric liver disease). This GSD1a approval is the nearest regulatory precedent for what Cebotaru is developing. The FDA pathway is now mapped.
(3) RMAT designation available to ARPKD-CHF programs — Regenerative Medicine Advanced Therapy designation accelerates development and FDA interaction for serious conditions. Any PKHD1 liver gene therapy program should apply for RMAT from the start.
(4) Surrogate endpoint strategy validated — FDA accepted a functional biomarker (cornstarch reduction) rather than preventing end-stage disease. For ARPKD-CHF, this means MRE liver stiffness reduction or ELF normalization could support an accelerated approval application — we do not need to wait for portal hypertension events or transplant outcomes.

Tier 2 — regulatory precedent, not a direct ARPKD finding. But sets the template for every ARPKD-CHF gene therapy program. Researcher routing: Cebotaru (Johns Hopkins), Willenbring (UCSF), Krappitz (Colorado).

🏥 CLINICAL & BIOMARKERS

📋 2026 Clinical Review: Liver disease becomes the dominant ARPKD phenotype by adolescence — only 40% of newborns show clinical CHF, but 60–65% of survivors have evident liver disease by age 15 (September 8, 2026)

Source: Borden C, Dell KM. "Genetic and Clinical Evaluation of Pediatric Cystic Kidney Diseases: Autosomal Dominant and Autosomal Recessive Polycystic Kidney Disease." Adv Kidney Dis Health. June 23, 2026. DOI: S2949-8139(26)00032-7. Clinical review (not primary data). Katherine M. Dell is a co-investigator in the ARegPKD/ADPedKD registry network.

Key data points for ARPKD families and clinicians:
(1) Only ~40% of ARPKD newborns show clinical evidence of CHF at birth — but all patients are thought to have some degree of microscopic liver involvement from the start. Standard liver function tests often appear normal even with active histological disease.
(2) 60–65% of neonatal survivors develop clinically evident liver disease by age 15 — meaning liver disease becomes the defining challenge for most ARPKD patients long before adulthood. (Source cited by Borden/Dell: Burgmaier K, Liebau MC et al. ARegPKD cohort, Sci Rep 9:7919, 2019. This is the same Liebau-led dataset our network tracks.)
(3) Adolescent/young adult presentation is liver-dominated: ARPKD that first becomes apparent in the teenage years or young adulthood typically presents with liver-related signs and symptoms rather than kidney complications — portal hypertension, variceal bleeding, or cholangitis — not flank masses or kidney function decline.

(5) No approved ARPKD-specific therapy exists — the 2026 clinical review confirms management remains entirely supportive for liver disease: ultrasound surveillance, liver function monitoring, portal hypertension management. This gap is precisely what this research network is working to fill.

But the trajectory is clear: liver disease builds silently and emerges as the dominant challenge in childhood and adolescence. This is the biological rationale for monitoring, for registries like ARegPKD, and for therapeutic pipeline urgency. The 60–65% by-age-15 statistic is not well known outside specialist circles.

⭐⭐ SPTAN1→RAC1→c-FOS: a third parallel kidney cystogenic pathway — and epigenetic rescue restores ARPKD organoids without correcting PKHD1 (February 26, 2026)

Source: Kuraoka S, Higashi Y, Saito S, Pourgonabadi S, Honjoh H, Ishigaki S, Harris PC, Satlin LM, Yamashita M, Morizane R. "Deciphering the Impact of RAC1-SPTAN1 in ARPKD Cystogenesis Using Multifaceted Models." Advanced Science 13(25):e2524001, 2026. Open Access. DOI: 10.1002/advs.202524001. From the Morizane Lab (MGH/Harvard), with co-authors from Mayo Clinic and Mount Sinai. Tier 1 — five independent evidence streams: organoid-on-chip, transgenic mice, human ARPKD kidney specimens, scRNA-seq, and CRISPRa rescue.

What they found: The cytoskeletal protein SPTAN1 (αII-spectrin) is reduced in PKHD1-deficient kidney cells. This loss disrupts the sodium-calcium exchanger SLC8A1, raises intracellular calcium, and activates RAC1 — a small GTPase that then drives c-FOS transcription and cyst expansion in the distal/connecting tubule. The study definitively answers where ARPKD kidney cysts come from: SLC8A1+ distal/connecting tubule cells, not collecting duct cells — confirmed by scRNA-seq and human ARPKD patient tissue.

The most striking result: CRISPR activation (CRISPRa) of SPTAN1 in PKHD1⁻/⁻ human organoids alleviated cystic phenotypes, normalized intracellular calcium, and reduced RAC1 and c-FOS — without ever correcting the PKHD1 mutation itself. This is epigenetic rescue: turning up the expression of a downstream gene using a gene activator system that doesn't cut DNA. Rescuing ARPKD organoids without touching PKHD1 is a conceptually important shift — it means patients with frameshifts or deletions that can't be base-edited might still have therapeutic options via downstream pathway correction.

Three parallel cystogenic pathways now confirmed in ARPKD kidney: (1) V2R/vasopressin → cAMP [tolvaptan target]; (2) PTH1R → cAMP [untested]; (3) SPTAN1 → RAC1 → c-FOS [CRISPRa proof-of-concept]. Tolvaptan addresses only the first. The existence of two additional independent pathways partially explains why tolvaptan produces incomplete benefit and why a pediatric-specific approach is needed.

⭐ PTH1R — parathyroid hormone receptor identified as second cAMP-elevating target in ARPKD kidney, parallel to and independent of tolvaptan's mechanism (August 1, 2025)

Source: Afrin H, Robichaud JH, Qamar U, Harris PC, Gupta N. "Kidney organoids demonstrate that PTH1R drives a cystogenic cAMP-pPKA-pCREB axis in developmental polycystic kidney disease." Am J Physiol Renal Physiol 329(2):F257–F268, 2025. From the Mayo Clinic Division of Nephrology and Hypertension. PMID: 40602764. DOI: 10.1152/ajprenal.00056.2025. Tier 1 — human PKHD1⁻/⁻ iPSC organoids with isogenic controls + scRNA-seq + decedent human ARPKD tissue.

What they found: PTH1R (parathyroid hormone 1 receptor) — a receptor most people associate with calcium/bone metabolism — is also a stimulatory G-protein-coupled receptor that activates cAMP in ARPKD kidney tubule cells. Treating PKHD1⁻/⁻ organoids with PTH (the hormone that activates PTH1R) causes enhanced cyst expansion. The pathway runs: PTH1R → cAMP↑ → PKA phosphorylation → CREB phosphorylation → cystogenic gene transcription.

Why this matters: Tolvaptan (the only approved ARPKD kidney drug, for adults) works by blocking vasopressin from activating another cAMP-elevating receptor (V2R). PTH1R is a completely separate receptor that elevates cAMP independently. A patient on tolvaptan suppresses the vasopressin/V2R route but leaves the PTH1R route untouched. No PTH1R antagonist has ever been tested in ARPKD. This is a newly identified, completely unaddressed drug target for kidney cyst progression.

⚠️ AAV gene therapy vectors cause kidney inflammation via NFκB in human organoids — a safety signal animal models miss (August 8, 2025)

Source: Gupta N, Zhang K, Sabbisetti V, Shu J, Morizane R. "AAV for gene therapy drives a nephrotoxic response via NFκB in kidney organoids." Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy 10(1):252, 2025. From Massachusetts General Hospital / Harvard Medical School. PMID: 40774951. DOI: 10.1038/s41392-025-02336-2. Open Access. Context: this study was motivated by real patient deaths in AAV-based gene therapy trials for Duchenne muscular dystrophy — safety signals that were missed in preclinical animal studies.

What they found: When human stem cell-derived kidney organoids were exposed to AAV viral vectors — including empty vectors containing no therapeutic cargo — the AAV itself triggered NFκB-mediated inflammation and kidney cell toxicity. This happened regardless of what the AAV was carrying. Animal models in those same programs did not flag this toxicity, which contributed to unexpected deaths when the therapies reached clinical trials.

Among AAV serotypes tested: AAV2 transduced kidney tubule cells most efficiently (~46%) but confirmed the NFκB nephrotoxicity. AAV8 and AAV9 showed negligible kidney tropism — meaning they reach the kidney less, which is actually an advantage for liver-targeted delivery (less kidney off-target exposure). This is why our research network prioritizes human kidney organoid safety testing as a mandatory step before any kidney-targeted ARPKD gene therapy program proceeds to the clinic.

For ARPKD families: This finding is not a reason to be alarmed about gene therapy — it is a reason to be reassured that the field is catching safety issues early using better tools. Human organoids see what mice don't. The research network around Gianna uses this exact organoid-based approach.

⭐ Gene therapy delivery framework review — validates dual-AAV split-intein for large-gene packaging, ABE for transition mutations in nondividing cells, and early intervention as the decisive therapeutic variable (September 5, 2026)

Polat M, Akad Dincer S, Terzi YK (Başkent University, Ankara, Turkey). "Gene Therapy Approaches for Rare Genetic Diseases: Current Advances, Challenges and Future Perspectives." Gene Therapy — From Molecular Tools to Clinical Applications and Future Horizons (IntechOpen), Chapter online first. DOI: 10.5772/intechopen.1017730. Published September 5, 2026. Open access. Tier 2 — Framework synthesis and validation. Published six days ago.

This peer-reviewed review chapter independently validates the gene therapy delivery landscape as it applies to ARPKD. It does not mention ARPKD directly — but every core technical lesson maps onto the PKHD1 gene therapy challenge.

(1) The PKHD1 packaging problem is real and well-recognized. AAV vectors carry a hard 4.7 kb limit. PKHD1's coding sequence (~12–16 kb) is roughly three times this limit. This paper confirms that the dual-AAV split-intein strategy is the most advanced workaround: two AAV vectors each carry half of a large protein that reassembles inside the target cell via protein splicing. This is not theoretical — split-intein approaches have been used in preclinical DMD models where the dystrophin gene (~14 kb) faces the same packaging constraint as PKHD1.

(2) Adenine base editing (ABE) is the right modality for transition mutations in nondividing cells. ABE converts A→G without requiring a double-strand DNA break and operates efficiently in postmitotic tissues such as renal tubular cells. This is directly relevant: both PKHD1 variants in our daughter are transition mutations correctable by ABE — c.9370C>T (His3124Tyr) requires A→G on the antisense strand; c.10926G>A (Met3642Ile) requires A→G on the coding strand. Both are ABE targets. This is the same chemical A→G correction demonstrated at 94% corrected M-AAT fraction in human liver by BEAM-302 (Finding #7, this issue).

(3) Threshold effects are real — you do not need to correct every cell. The hemophilia B model demonstrates this conclusively: even modest correction of hepatocytes producing a secreted protein provides durable clinical benefit. The same principle applies to fibrocystin correction in cholangiocytes for ARPKD-CHF — partial restoration of fibrocystin expression across a sufficient proportion of biliary epithelial cells may be enough to interrupt the SRC/STAT3 cystogenic cascade.

(4) Early intervention is the most important variable — more important than vector potency. The SMA lesson is explicit in this paper: treating before irreversible cellular loss determines outcome, not dosing. Children treated presymptomatically achieve functional milestones that are impossible once motor neurons are lost. For ARPKD, where renal cystogenesis and biliary fibrosis begin prenatally, neonatal or presymptomatic intervention is the goal of any therapeutic program. This is the scientific rationale behind every urgency in the HRFD enrollment and the ARPA-H THRIVE/FDA Plausible Mechanism ecosystem at CHOP (Finding #5, this issue).

(5) The N-of-1 regulatory pathway is now viable. This paper's section on individualized genomic medicine and ultra-rare disease trials aligns directly with the FDA Plausible Mechanism Framework (Finding #5). Together, these two 2026 publications confirm that a patient-specific ABE therapy for rare PKHD1 variants does not require a large randomized trial — a plausible mechanism plus evidence of pathway modification is accepted as the approval standard.

⚠️ Safety note carried forward from Finding #7: This paper documents Grade 3 ALT/AST transaminase elevations in liver disease patients receiving ABE+LNP therapy. ARPKD-CHF patients have elevated baseline liver enzymes. A dedicated hepatic monitoring protocol will be required in any future PKHD1 ABE trial.

⭐ HITI gene correction — 100× more efficient than HDR in adult liver, mutation-agnostic, durable through native locus integration — proof-of-concept for pediatric liver gene therapy (September 2026)

Ginn SL, Doroudian F, Christina S, Chan OPY, Lucas CW, Zhu E, Yang SF, Devanapalli B, Klein AH, Scott S, Vitale J, Cunningham SC, Liao SHY, Cabanes-Creus M, Lisowski L, Alexander IE. Children's Medical Research Institute, The University of Sydney, Westmead, NSW, Australia. Molecular Therapy 34(9):5426–5443, September 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.ymthe.2026.06.044. Open access (CC BY). Full paper read September 14, 2026. Tier 2 — Framework validation and extension. Not ARPKD-specific but directly translatable.

This Australian team has developed Homology-Independent Targeted Integration (HITI) — a CRISPR-based gene editing approach using two AAV vectors that repairs a faulty gene directly at its natural chromosomal location. Tested in OTC deficiency (a urea cycle liver disorder with 500+ causative mutations — the same mutation-number challenge as PKHD1). Key findings with direct ARPKD relevance:

(1) 100× more efficient than HDR-dependent approaches in adult liver. Classic gene correction via homology-directed repair (HDR) works in cycling cells but achieves only 0.2–0.3% correction in adult hepatocytes. HITI, which uses the NHEJ repair pathway, achieved 24–40% correction in adult mouse liver and 48% correction in patient-derived human hepatocytes — a 100-fold improvement. Adult liver cells, including cholangiocytes, rarely divide. HITI's NHEJ-based mechanism works in non-dividing cells, making it far more relevant to ARPKD liver disease than HDR approaches.

(2) Mutation-agnostic — one construct treats all mutations. More than 500 different mutations cause OTC deficiency. PKHD1 has a similar mutation diversity. HITI uses a universal promoterless donor cassette that integrates at the first intron and captures the native gene's own regulatory machinery — so the same construct works regardless of which downstream mutation is causing disease.

(3) Durable through native locus integration. Conventional gene therapy adds an extra gene copy episomally — this is gradually diluted as a child's liver grows. HITI places the therapeutic sequence at the native chromosomal locus under the gene's own promoter, meaning corrected cells pass the correction to daughter cells. This directly addresses the pediatric durability problem: growing livers do not dilute the treatment.

(4) Native promoter capture restores physiological expression. The promoterless cassette integrates under the gene's own regulatory control — producing tissue-specific, physiologically appropriate expression. For PKHD1, this would mean fibrocystin expressed specifically in cholangiocytes and collecting duct cells under its own regulatory machinery, not overexpressed ectopically.

(5) Human patient-derived cells confirm clinical translatability. Up to 48% of OTC-deficient patient-derived human hepatocytes had expression restored. The authors state this would provide therapeutic benefit if recapitulated in humans — and they frame OTC deficiency as a deliberately challenging target, with broader positive implications for other liver diseases that require lower correction thresholds.

⚠️ Critical caveat for ARPKD-CHF patients specifically: The delivery vehicle is dual AAV (AAV8 for mouse, SYD12 capsid for human). Liver-directed AAV has caused deaths in children with pre-existing cholangiopathy. HITI as a technique is sound — but for patients with ARPKD-CHF, the delivery vehicle must be LNP rather than AAV. Combining the HITI technique with LNP delivery (Long/Williams/Gissen platforms) is the logical next step.

⚠️ Limitation: The donor cassette starts at exon 2 — mutations in exon 1 or upstream regulatory elements are not corrected. Neither of Gianna's variants (c.9370C>T, c.10926G>A) is in exon 1, so this limitation does not apply to her specifically.

⭐ Why ARPKD clinical trials have stalled — and how MRI can unlock them: a 2026 roadmap (July 28, 2026)

Source: MacAskill CJ, Smothers JB, Kretzler ME, Dell KM, Hartung EA, Flask CA et al. "Advanced MRI methods for children and young adults with ARPKD to support future multi-center clinical trials." Pediatric Radiology. July 28, 2026. DOI: 10.1007/s00247-026-06737-5. Open Access. From Cleveland Clinic / Case Western Reserve University + Children's Hospital of Philadelphia. Katherine M. Dell and Erum A. Hartung are co-authors.

The problem this paper addresses: Despite progress in understanding ARPKD biology, there are still no approved disease-modifying treatments — in part because clinical trials cannot proceed without validated, measurable endpoints. The standard tools don't work: total kidney volume (the metric used to approve tolvaptan for ADPKD) is explicitly stated in this paper as NOT useful for ARPKD — it can remain stable even as disease progresses, and in children it is confounded by normal kidney growth. Liver biopsies are explicitly not recommended in ARPKD due to risk of bile leak from dilated bile ducts, thrombocytopenia from portal hypertension, and anesthesia risks. Standard blood tests (ALT, AST, creatinine) are frequently normal despite active disease. Without endpoints, there can be no trials.

What the paper establishes:
(1) MR Fingerprinting (MRF) generates kidney T1 and T2 maps in 15 seconds per imaging slice — feasible in children as young as 6 without sedation. ARPKD kidneys show dramatically elevated T1 (2,218 vs. 1,473 ms) and T2 (97 vs. 69 ms) compared to healthy controls, with only ~3% day-to-day variation. Critically, MRF detected kidney disease even when eGFR was still normal — earlier than any blood test can. This matters because the window for intervention is before eGFR declines.
(2) MR Elastography (MRE) for liver stiffness is now FDA-cleared and available on essentially all major MRI scanners. In ARPKD patients: 1.74 kPa (healthy) → 2.49 kPa (ARPKD without portal hypertension) → 3.60 kPa (ARPKD with portal hypertension). MRE has entered the FDA Biomarker Qualification Program for liver fibrosis — meaning a formal regulatory pathway exists to accept MRE as a surrogate endpoint in clinical trials.
(3) Molecular MRI contrast agents (EP-3533, Gd-Hyd conjugates) that specifically bind collagen are now available for ARPKD animal model studies — providing a direct readout of fibrosis regression in pre-clinical gene therapy experiments before human trials.

What this means for ARPKD families: The mechanistic work is there. The biology is increasingly understood. What has held up trials is the measurement problem — how to know whether a treatment is actually working in a child's liver or kidney without invasive procedures. This paper lays out the solution: MRI-based biomarkers that are non-invasive, reproducible, sensitive even at early disease, and now on a formal FDA regulatory pathway. The ongoing IMAGE-ARPKD study (NCT07201025, Cleveland Clinic + CHOP) is building this measurement infrastructure right now.

MacAskill CJ, Dell KM, Hartung EA, Flask CA et al. Pediatric Radiology. 2026. Open Access. · IMAGE-ARPKD Study NCT07201025

Yale Human Liver Single Cell Atlas confirms cholangiocyte biology of ARPKD framework — We queried the Brancale & Vilarinho human liver single cell atlas (J Hepatol 2021;75:219-220, PMID 34016468; liveratlas-vilarinholab.med.yale.edu; 2,357 cholangiocytes from 28 healthy livers) for genes central to ARPKD-CHF. Key findings: SPP1 is rank 21 most cholangiocyte-specific gene in human liver (logFC 3.149, 51.3% of cells) — independently confirming our STAT3→SPP1 cascade from human tissue data. PKHD1 is rank 22 (logFC 3.132, 49.8%) — directly explaining why ARPKD specifically destroys biliary cells. SPTAN1 is confirmed in cholangiocytes — validating the open question of whether SPTAN1/RAC1 disruption occurs in PKHD1-deficient biliary cells (Morizane follow-up Sep 25). CFTR (rank 56) is specifically expressed in cholangiocytes — validating the Cebotaru AAV1-CFTR therapeutic approach at the cell-type level. Two new open questions identified: BICC1 (rank 8, ciliopathy gene causing kidney cysts + biliary fibrosis, 67.3% of cholangiocytes) and GLIS3 (rank 43, mutations cause polycystic kidneys + biliary atresia, 41.9% of cholangiocytes) — both ciliopathy genes are among the most specifically expressed genes in healthy human bile duct cells. If their expression is disrupted in ARPKD-CHF, this may compound PKHD1-driven dysfunction. Routing: Harris PC (Mayo), Germino GG (NIDDK).

Stellate cell atlas confirms the cellular mechanism behind biliary basement membrane loss in ARPKD-CHF — The same atlas queried for hepatic stellate cells and portal fibroblasts (1,604 cells). Decorin (DCN, rank 2, 71.5% of cells) and Biglycan (BGN, rank 5, 91.6% of cells) — the biliary basement membrane proteoglycans that Waddell and Boulter showed are specifically lost in ARPKD-CHF — are produced by stellate cells/portal fibroblasts in healthy liver. When cholangiocyte-derived chemokines (CXCL6, CCL2) activate these cells pathologically in ARPKD-CHF, BM production stops and fibrotic collagen (COL1A1, COL3A1) takes over. BM loss and fibrosis are two consequences of the same cell type dysfunction. Key distinction: NGFR (portal fibroblast marker, rank 43) is present in this cluster, confirming ARPKD-CHF fibrosis is driven by portal fibroblasts (NGFR+) — not hepatic stellate cells as in NAFLD. PDGFR-β (Peltz/Stanford target) confirmed at rank 25 (64.4% of cells).

EFHD1 is not expressed in biliary cholangiocytes — Chaudhuri replies same day — Dr. Dipayan Chaudhuri (University of Utah, dipayan.chaudhuri@hsc.utah.edu) replied to our EFHD1 question within hours. His transcriptional datasets show low or absent EFHD1 expression in cholangiocytes (Fig. S1A), with high expression in hepatocytes only. Two mechanistic reasons: hepatocytes have uniquely high mitochondria abundance; the PKR antiviral sensing pathway is hepatocyte-specific and may be absent in cholangiocytes. Yale atlas independently confirmed: EFHD1 not detected in any liver cell type at meaningful levels. EFHD1 removed from ARPKD-CHF biliary cascade. New question: EFHD2 (sister protein) IS expressed in cholangiocytes per atlas — whether it plays an analogous ER-mitochondria role in biliary cells is unknown. Permission pending to cite Dr. Chaudhuri's reply.

Germino framework correction — PKHD1 locus regulates TFAP2B, not the reverse — Dr. Gregory Germino (germinogg@nih.gov, NIDDK Deputy Director) replied within hours of our email, correcting a directionality error in our framework. Independent lines of evidence converging on the same targets.

CDK1 as convergence point — and ICD15→AMPK→CDK1 complete mechanistic chain

Two independent YAP overactivation pathways both drive CDK1 expression. CDK1 is the top biliary proliferative driver in PKHD1-deficient liver. CDK1 inhibition (RO-3306) reduces both cysts AND fibrosis in vivo. ⚠️ CDK1 paper is Liu S, Ma M et al. (iScience 2026, DOI: 10.1016/j.isci.2026.01663) — previously mislabeled as Djouder.

⭐ NEW (Sep 14, 2026) — Complete causal chain: FPC loss → ICD15 not generated → mitochondria unsupported (Walker Nat Commun 2023) → AMPK cannot activate properly (Long KRUK grant, Liebau 2021 mitochondria paper) → CDK1 not suppressed by AMPK → biliary proliferation → cysts + fibrosis. Bempedoic acid (BEAT-PKD), SCY-770, and CDK1 inhibitors all target this same axis. David Long (UCL, TAKA deadline Oct 19) is the critical middle node. This is the mechanistic core of the Sep 25 email to Long.

iScience 2026 · Fabris L et al. J Hepatol 2022 · Djouder N et al. Nat Cell Biol 2021

⭐ SPP1/STAT3 — five independent confirmations + full paper read Sep 11, 2026

Cholangiocyte-specific AAV8-HNF1β-shSpp1 knockdown reduces biliary fibrosis in Pkhd1del3-4/del3-4 mice — the most validated therapeutic approach in ARPKD-CHF. Full paper now read; key new details below.

New from full paper read (Sep 11, 2026): (1) Macrophages are NOT the dominant SPP1 source in ARPKD-CHF — OPN had almost no overlap with F4/80 macrophage marker. Cholangiocytes are the primary source. Clodronate macrophage depletion did not fully resolve CHF fibrosis — a direct cholangiocyte→HSC pathway operates independently. (2) Serum OPN reference values: ~100 ng/mL (1m) → ~400 ng/mL (6m) in KO mice; drops to ~5 ng/mL after AAV8-shSpp1. BOSTER EK0482 kit for human samples. (3) p-STAT3 binds SPP1 promoter at sites 1, 2, and 4 — NOT site 3 (ChIP-qPCR confirmed). (4) Stattic (5 μM) significantly reduces p-STAT3 and SPP1 expression in PKHD1-knockdown biliary cells — first in vitro validation of p-STAT3 inhibition in FPC-deficient cholangiocytes. (5) HNF1β promoter is validated for cholangiocyte-specific AAV delivery in vivo — should be used for any biliary AAV therapeutic program in ARPKD-CHF. (6) Per-cell SPP1 elevation confirmed — ~4-fold increase per individual FPC-deficient cholangiocyte, not just a cell number effect. (7) CHF patient clinical profile: ALT 18 U/L, AST 19.5 U/L (near-normal), GGT 152.7 U/L (elevated) despite esophageal varices — confirms standard LFTs underestimate disease severity.

Jiao M, Sun Y, Liu R et al. Commun Biol 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s42003-026-10476-6. PMID: 42265280. Full paper read Sep 11, 2026.

SPTAN1 reduced in FPC-deficient kidney cells → RAC1 hyperactivation. If the same applies in cholangiocytes, one molecule may connect both RAC1 cystogenesis AND biliary basement membrane disruption.

Maralixibat ↔ Odevixibat — two independent FDA-approved IBAT inhibitors converge on bile acid → p38δ-YAP fibrosis axis

Odevixibat (Bylvay, Ipsen) and maralixibat (Livmarli, Mirum) are developed by completely separate companies for separate indications, yet both inhibit the same ileal bile acid transporter and both show signals in biliary cholestatic pediatric liver disease. Odevixibat already has biliary fibrosis data in the Mdr2-/- cholestatic model (reduced pro-fibrogenic genes, preserved biliary HCO3-). Maralixibat now showing improved transplant-free survival signals in Alagille syndrome. Both FDA-approved from 3 months of age. In ARPKD-CHF, bile acid accumulation activates the p38δ-YAP axis driving biliary fibrosis — the same mechanism both drugs interrupt. Two drugs, same target, same pediatric approval window, independent validation.

Odevixibat — Aseem SO et al. Cell Mol Gastroenterol Hepatol 2024 · Maralixibat EXPAND — NCT04524390

MASLD genetic subtypes ↔ ARPKD-CHF — genetically-driven liver disease progresses without metabolic risk factors in both

Mayo Clinic's MASLD subtyping study (Nat Commun 2026) found that genetic MASLD subtypes progress to advanced liver disease at higher rates than metabolic subtypes — even without obesity or diabetes. This is exactly the ARPKD-CHF pattern: children develop portal hypertension and biliary fibrosis driven entirely by a single gene defect, with no metabolic contributors. Two completely different diseases, different genes, different mechanisms — but the same principle: genetic liver disease follows a distinct and more aggressive trajectory than metabolic liver disease. This validates the core ARPKD-CHF framework argument that standard MASLD-derived endpoints and timelines do not apply to ARPKD-CHF.

Sultana Priya T et al. Nat Commun 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-77410-6

ImP → p38γ/δ → mTORC1 in PSC ↔ Bile acid → p38δ → YAP in ARPKD-CHF — two independent diseases, same cholangiocyte kinase

The PSC gut microbiota paper (Molinaro, Hov et al. Nat Metab 2026) proves p38γ/δ is the obligate mediator of cholangiocyte fibrosis in PSC via genetic knockout. Our framework independently identified p38δ as the kinase through which bile acid accumulation drives YAP activation and biliary fibrosis in ARPKD-CHF. Two completely different diseases — one autoimmune/microbiota-driven, one genetic — arrive at the same cholangiocyte kinase from entirely different upstream triggers. p38 inhibition (pirfenidone, or p38δ-specific inhibitors) is now validated by genetic proof-of-concept in a biliary cholestatic disease, strengthening the therapeutic rationale for p38 targeting in ARPKD-CHF.
