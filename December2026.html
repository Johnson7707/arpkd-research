🧬 Molecular Biology & Biliary Cascade

🧬 MOLECULAR BIOLOGY & BILIARY CASCADE

⭐ FINDING — Collagen I is an active instructor of cholangiocyte specification and tubulogenesis: excess collagen I in ARPKD-CHF may drive abnormal ductal plate formation as a developmental feedback loop. ⭐ FINDING · Nov 2, 2026

Source: Thelwall IG, Morell CM, Dziedzicka D, Cabriales L, Hodgson A, Roos FJM, Elfari L, Vallier L, Chalut KJ. "Mechanical signalling through collagen I regulates cholangiocyte specification and tubulogenesis during liver development." Development 2026. Preprint: bioRxiv 2025.01.22.633528. Vallier lab (Charité Berlin) + Chalut lab (Cambridge). Ludovic Vallier is on this newsletter's distribution list.

What they did: Used StemBond hydrogels — collagen I-coated at varying stiffnesses — to investigate the influence of the ECM microenvironment on cholangiocyte differentiation from hiPSCs. Found that collagen I strongly promotes cholangiocyte specification (KRT19+) independently of matrix stiffness. Identified integrin α2β1 (ITGα2β1) as the primary mechanoreceptor co-localizing with cholangiocyte marker KRT19. Showed that inhibiting ITGα2β1-collagen I binding during tubulogenesis produces aberrant bile duct tube formation. First demonstration that collagen type I is essential for both biliary epithelial cell fate choice and morphogenesis.

⭐ ARPKD-CHF developmental feedback loop: In ARPKD-CHF, FPC loss → excess collagen I production via CTGF/TGF-β cascade → elevated periportal collagen I → abnormal bias toward cholangiocyte specification in liver progenitors → defective biliary tubulogenesis = ductal plate malformation. Collagen I excess in ARPKD-CHF may actively worsen ductal plate malformation by misguiding progenitor fate decisions — independently of FPC's direct effects on primary cilia signaling. This is a new mechanistic connection linking fibrotic ECM remodeling to developmental biliary pathology.

Connection to existing framework: Waddell/Boulter 2026 (Dis Model Mech): ECM primary defect in CHF is laminin/collagen IV LOSS; Yasoshima 2009 (J Pathol): basement membrane degraded in human CHF and Caroli disease by plasminogen/tPA overexpression. Now add: collagen I excess (fibrotic component) may simultaneously promote abnormal cholangiocyte fate specification via ITGα2β1. Two arms of the ECM story — loss of BM collagen IV AND gain of interstitial collagen I — both pathogenic by different mechanisms.

⚠️ Limits: hiPSC-derived model only. Not tested in Pkhd1-deficient cholangiocytes or ARPKD liver tissue. Developmental interpretation extrapolated from a stem cell differentiation model. Whether excess collagen I in an already-formed fibrotic ARPKD-CHF liver continues to misdirect any remaining progenitors is unknown.

⭐ FINDING — Third independent AlphaFold analysis of fibrocystin published today (Nov 3). Richards and Goggolidou (Wolverhampton) use AF2 to confirm spiral/curved IPT/TIG architecture and find 3D spatial proximity of compound het variant pairs does NOT correlate with disease severity — reinforcing the Pillai linear zone framework. ⭐ FINDING · Nov 3, 2026

Source: Richards T, Goggolidou P. "A computational report on Fibrocystin structure via AlphaFold2: considerations and limitations for ARPKD research." Front. Mol. Biosci. 13:1877678. DOI: 10.3389/fmolb.2026.1877678. Published November 3, 2026. Open access CC BY. University of Wolverhampton, UK. Emails: taylor.richards@wlv.ac.uk · p.goggolidou@wlv.ac.uk

Context — three fibrocystin structural papers in two weeks: Cockburn preprint (Oct 20, AF3, FOLDX classifier, 658 ARegPKD patients); Pillai et al. CSBJ (Oct 27, AF3, full idealized model, genotype-phenotype with 304 patients); Richards/Goggolidou (Nov 3, AF2, limitations and spatial variant analysis). Three independent groups working in parallel, none aware of the others at submission. The convergence on structural characterization of fibrocystin in October-November 2026 is unprecedented.

Finding 1 — Spiral/curved architecture independently confirmed: AF2 predicts IPT/TIG domains arrange in a spiral-like loop creating an internally hollow structure with two parallel G8-PbH1 stacks — not the traditional long linear model. Consistent with Cockburn/Bannell 2024 (plexin-like curvature suggestion) and the Pillai AF3 idealized model. Highest AF2 confidence: G8 and PbH1 domains (average pLDDT 76-78). Lowest: transmembrane domain and intracellular tail (<50). IPT/TIG domains: low-to-moderate (average 59).

Finding 2 — 3D spatial proximity of compound het variants ≠ disease severity: In 15 compound het patients (4 adult, 5 child, 6 severe), spatial distance between variant pairs in 3D space did not predict outcomes (ANOVA p > 0.691). Severe patients had both spatially close and spatially distant variant pairs. This reinforces the Pillai zone framework: what matters is which LINEAR ZONE a variant occupies (AA 709-1837 protective renal; 2625-4074 hepatic risk), not 3D proximity.

Limitation relevant to ARPKD-CHF research: AF2 cannot model post-translational modifications (64 glycosylation sites in fibrocystin) or membrane-bound regions — limitations AF3 partially addresses. The SEA domain S1 cleavage loop (where Met3642 sits, per Cockburn) was not confidently modeled in AF2. Neither the Cockburn nor Pillai papers were available to Richards/Goggolidou at submission time — all three groups were working in parallel.

New contacts: Taylor Richards (PhD student, Wolverhampton) and Paraskevi Goggolidou (corresponding, Wolverhampton) — neither in the network previously. Goggolidou also authored the 2022 ARPKD genetics review and a 2024 WNT signaling paper. Both were contacted today — same-day as publication, bridging them with the Cockburn and Pillai papers they don't yet know about.

📌 Noted — Feng Qian and Saikat Mukhopadhyay (Nature Reviews Nephrology, Nov 2, 2026): Major review of cilium-mediated cystogenesis in PKD confirms cilium–metabolism axis as central therapeutic node. Human ARPKD neonatal ELV-cilia interactions documented. 📌 NOTED · Nov 3, 2026

Source: Qian F, Mukhopadhyay S. "Primary cilium-mediated mechanisms of cyst formation in polycystic kidney disease." Nature Reviews Nephrology, November 2, 2026. University of Maryland (Qian) + UT Southwestern (Mukhopadhyay). Open access. Feng Qian is in this network (fqian@som.umaryland.edu — permission granted Sep 2026 for ICD15/NDFIP2 citation).

Primarily ADPKD-focused but three findings are directly relevant to ARPKD-CHF: (1) The cilium–metabolism axis — ciliary signals converging on mitochondrial metabolism as a checkpoint — is confirmed as the central therapeutic node in PKD cystogenesis. This is exactly the FPC-PC1-ICD15 mitochondrial interaction Walker et al. 2023 showed for ARPKD specifically. (2) GPS/proteolysis site cleavage (the PC1 analog of FPC's SEA domain S1/C2 cleavage) is listed as an actionable therapeutic target — reinforcing the significance of Met3642Ile's S1 cleavage loop location. (3) PKD-ELVs (exosome-like vesicles containing PC1, PC2, and FPC) have been documented to adhere to cilia in collecting ducts of a human ARPKD neonate (ref 86 in paper) — the first clinical observation of ELV-cilia interactions in human ARPKD tissue. ⚠️ The specific reference 86 citation should be identified — it documents human ARPKD ELV-cilia biology and would be directly citable in the framework.

Note: Feng Qian is not currently on the December distribution list. Add fqian@som.umaryland.edu for December send.

📌 Noted (Hypothesis) — IRE1α–NK cell axis in PSC biliary epithelium connects to His3124Tyr ER stress hypothesis in ARPKD-CHF. 📌 NOTED · Nov 5, 2026

Source: He C et al. "Bile Acid-Activated IRE1α Pathway Promotes NK Cell-Mediated Cholangiocyte Injury in Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis." J Leukocyte Biol 2026. PSC biliary epithelium — not tested in ARPKD.

The finding: In PSC cholangiocytes, bile acids activate IRE1α (the canonical ER stress sensor). Activated IRE1α promotes NK cell recruitment to the biliary epithelium, which in turn injures cholangiocytes — establishing IRE1α as a biliary fibrosis amplifier via immune recruitment.

The ARPKD-CHF hypothesis: His3124Tyr has high ΔΔGfold from a buried position in the PbH1 core (Cockburn Oct 2026) — misfolded FPC accumulates in the ER. ER protein accumulation is the canonical trigger for IRE1α activation. If His3124Tyr-driven FPC misfolding activates IRE1α in ARPKD-CHF cholangiocytes, the He et al. finding suggests NK cells are recruited and additional cholangiocyte injury occurs — an immune amplification loop on top of the existing macrophage/NLRP3 cascade. Each step is individually documented; their connection in ARPKD-CHF is completely untested.

Therapeutically actionable: IRE1α kinase inhibitors (KIRA6; ORIN1001 in Phase 1/2 clinical development) could reduce this arm of biliary injury independently of correcting FPC — a mutation-specific anti-inflammatory strategy for His3124Tyr carriers specifically. ⚠️ Hypothesis only. Added to framework open questions Nov 5, 2026.

🧪 GENE THERAPY & BASE EDITING

📌 Noted — AAV8 liver gene therapy for Crigler-Najjar syndrome shows sustained efficacy at 4 years — first peer-reviewed long-term durability data for liver-directed AAV in a congenital liver disease. 📌 NOTED · Nov 5, 2026

Source: D'Antiga L et al. "Durability of Gene Therapy in Patients with Crigler-Najjar Syndrome." New England Journal of Medicine 2026;395:1870-1872. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMc2603129. Lorenzo D'Antiga, Papa Giovanni XXIII Hospital, Bergamo + Généthon (CureCN consortium). Published November 5, 2026.

What it shows: GNT0003 (AAV8-UGT1A1) maintains efficacy at 4 years in Crigler-Najjar syndrome — a rare congenital liver enzyme deficiency (~500 patients in Europe). Of 3 patients at therapeutic dose: 2 discontinued phototherapy completely; 1 maintained partial response with reduced phototherapy. Bilirubin levels remained below neurotoxicity threshold in all 3. Sustained ALT normalization achieved — a liver health marker not achievable with phototherapy alone. One patient successfully completed a pregnancy and delivered a healthy baby post-gene therapy.

Relevance to ARPKD-CHF: This is the longest published follow-up data for liver-directed AAV gene therapy in a congenital liver disease — 4 years of sustained efficacy and safety. Baby KJ (k-abe, the closest ARPKD-CHF analogy) has only 7 weeks of follow-up data published. For planning purposes, the Crigler-Najjar 4-year data is the most relevant durability precedent for any future ARPKD-CHF liver gene therapy program. The pregnancy outcome — positive post-gene therapy — is a clinically important long-term safety data point for a disease predominantly affecting children who will grow up.

⚠️ Critical caveat: AAV8 targets hepatocytes, not cholangiocytes. Crigler-Najjar is a hepatocyte enzyme deficiency (UGT1A1 expressed in hepatocytes). ARPKD-CHF is a biliary cholangiocyte disease. The delivery system would need to be adapted for cholangiocyte targeting — the same challenge documented throughout this framework. Généthon's statement that these results "pave the way for the treatment of other metabolic diseases" signals platform extension intent.

📌 Noted — Local rapamycin immune modulation extends AAV gene therapy efficacy in rare tubular kidney disease; neonatal timing achieves immune tolerance. Dent disease CLCN5 series, Gene Therapy 2026. 📌 NOTED · Nov 2026

Source: Lyu P, Bostanci A, Jiang C, Yoo KW, Broadhurst R et al., Baisong Lu (Wake Forest). Third paper in the CLCN5/Dent disease gene therapy series. Gene Therapy 2026.

Key finding: Local rapamycin administration at the gene delivery site — not systemic immunosuppression — extends gene therapy efficacy by limiting immune responses to the transgene. The local approach avoids the toxicity of systemic immunosuppression while still controlling the anti-transgene T-cell response. Second finding: gene therapy in neonatal mice achieves immune tolerance via the neonatal immune window — long-term expression without immunosuppression. For ARPKD-CHF: local rapamycin co-administration via the hepatic artery delivery route could mitigate the immune rejection risk that makes systemic AAV dangerous in cholangiopathy patients — addressing Krampitz's specific warning without systemic toxicity. This decouples the immune safety problem from the delivery route. ⚠️ Not tested in ARPKD or biliary disease.

📌 Noted — Suppression-Replacement (SupRep) gene therapy for LMNA cardiomyopathy — a mutation-agnostic strategy applicable to compound heterozygous PKHD1. AHA 2026 abstract. 📌 NOTED · Nov 2026

Source: Gluscevic M, Kim CJ, Tester DJ et al. (Mayo Clinic, Ackerman lab). AHA Scientific Sessions abstract, Circulation 2026. Conference abstract only — not yet peer-reviewed full paper.

Key concept: SupRep combines shRNA to silence BOTH alleles simultaneously + shRNA-immune replacement cDNA (shIMM). The same construct treats all mutations regardless of which specific variants are present — truly mutation-agnostic. For PKHD1: would suppress both His3124Tyr and Met3642Ile and replace with functional PKHD1 cDNA, bypassing the Met3642Ile S1 cleavage loop pathogenic mechanism question entirely. Main challenge: 12.2 kb PKHD1 cDNA exceeds AAV capacity — would require mRNA-LNP delivery (no packaging limit) or dual-AAV trans-splicing. SupRep is a strategy applicable to any delivery platform. ⚠️ No PKHD1 application published.

📌 Noted — First systematic comparison of AAV capsid performance in ex situ human liver — not mice. Periportal zonation data directly relevant to cholangiocyte targeting. Broad Institute preprint 2026. 📌 NOTED · Nov 2026

Source: Unzu C et al. (Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard). Preprint DOI: 10.64898/2026.08.20.745184. Barcoded AAV capsid library tested in human liver maintained by ex situ normothermic perfusion. ⚠️ Preprint — peer review pending.

Key findings: (1) Capsid choice significantly affects transduction efficiency and episome formation in human liver — mouse model predictions do not reliably transfer; (2) steatotic and normal liver show different capsid preferences; (3) AAV5 delays concatemerization — affecting long-term durability; (4) pericentral vs periportal hepatocyte zonation affects transduction efficiency by capsid. For ARPKD-CHF: cholangiocytes are periportally located — this paper's periportal zonation data directly informs which AAV capsid to test first for biliary targeting. The ex situ normothermic perfusion platform enables testing biliary delivery strategies in intact human liver tissue before any patient exposure.

🧬 GENETICS & VARIANTS

⭐ FINDING — Deep intronic PKHD1 variant found by SpliceAI and confirmed by UREC from carrier mother's urine — newborn ARPKD case; parents now have a healthy child. Opens a critical third variant hypothesis for unresolved PKHD1 compound heterozygotes. ⭐ FINDING · Nov 2, 2026

Source: Richter F, Turro E et al. "Identification of a deep intronic PKHD1 variant in a neonate with lethal autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease using genome sequencing and SpliceAI." Am J Kidney Dis 83(5):829-833, 2024. Senior/corresponding: Ernest Turro, Associate Professor of Genetics, Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai. Identified via Illumina Nature partner content, November 2026.

What happened: A newborn died from ARPKD within two days. The father carried a known pathogenic PKHD1 variant. The mother carried a VUS not in gnomAD — in a deep intronic non-coding region invisible to standard protein prediction tools. Turro's group applied SpliceAI to all PKHD1 variants in gnomAD plus the maternal VUS. The tool predicted a very high probability of splice disruption. Pathogenicity was confirmed by growing kidney cells from the mother's urine (UREC method) — RNA analysis showed incorrect splicing exactly as predicted. Parents underwent IVF, screened embryos, and now have a healthy daughter.

Why this matters broadly: Two independent groups (Turro/Richter 2024 and Huang et al. Gene 2026, in November's newsletter) have now confirmed UREC from urine as a viable method to reclassify PKHD1 VUSs by direct RNA analysis. SpliceAI can identify pathogenic deep intronic variants that would be invisible to standard sequencing pipelines. For ARPKD patients with unresolved second variants, the answer may be in non-coding intronic regions of PKHD1 that standard variant annotation pipelines never examine.

⭐ The third variant hypothesis: In compound heterozygous PKHD1 cases where one variant is confirmed pathogenic and the second remains a VUS, a deep intronic variant on the same chromosome as the VUS — detectable by SpliceAI and confirmable by UREC — is a plausible explanation for cases where the VUS appears to be benign by standard tools. Turro's NICU network of 16 US centers is actively collecting such cases.

New contacts: Ernest Turro (ernest.turro@mssm.edu, Icahn Mount Sinai) — rare disease WGS, non-coding variant interpretation, SpliceAI, PKHD1. Felix Richter (neonatal clinical researcher, Mount Sinai) — NICU rare disease genetics, PKHD1 case series. Neither is currently in this newsletter's network. Turro will be contacted this week.

⚠️ Limits: Single published PKHD1 case. SpliceAI score was the trigger but urine cell RNA confirmation was required — the tool's prediction alone was not considered sufficient for clinical action. Deep intronic variant detection requires whole genome sequencing; exome sequencing may miss them. The UREC method was used here from a carrier mother, not the affected patient (who died); applying it to a living patient would sample the patient's own urine.

Open Questions

Open Questions for the Research Community

These questions came out of this month's reading and are not answered in the sources we found. If your work addresses any of them — preliminary data, an unpublished observation, or a paper we missed — a brief reply is all we ask.

GENETICS & VARIANTS
Q1. Which PKHD1 variants cause liver-predominant disease? Cohorts so far (Burgmaier's zones, Zhao, Lapunzina-Soler, and Bhuva's nine congenital hepatic fibrosis cases) have not been pooled into one table of variant, position and phenotype. Do missense variants in the C-terminal ectodomain (Zone 3, AA 2625–4074) cluster in liver-predominant cases? A 2025 series of prenatally diagnosed Caroli-type cases found three of four missense variants in this region, but the numbers are tiny. An older French cohort (Denamur 2010) lists missense variants in the same region (W2736G, I2957T, R3240K) among children who died perinatally, so the region is not liver-only. Small case reports point both ways (Giacobbe 2022; and a prenatally diagnosed Caroli case with one missense variant in this region who needed kidney and liver transplants by 16 months, Rivas 2019).
Q2. Does an interior-exon missense variant of uncertain significance, such as c.10926G>A (p.Met3642Ile), change splicing? Precedents exist (R760H through an enhancer motif; a last-nucleotide missense with exon 40 skipping). Would a minigene assay or urine-cell RNA testing settle it? (A SpliceAI and Pangolin lookup on Oct 8 predicted no splice effect, with all scores 0.05 or lower; these tools miss enhancer-mediated skipping.)
Q3. Do modifier genes beyond PKHD1 explain the wide variation in ARPKD severity, including liver involvement? Does the UK PKD genetics fellowship's dataset include ARPKD patients? Candidates raised in the older literature, from animal work, include HNF1B, KIF12 and interactions with PKD1 and PKD2; human evidence is lacking. A 2018 mouse study found that a truncating Pkhd1 variant caused clinical biliary disease only on the NOD autoimmune background (Huang et al.).
DELIVERY & THERAPY
Q4. How can cholangiocytes be reached? Five clinical AAV capsids transduced them poorly in perfused human livers. Has anyone screened capsids or tried retrograde bile-duct delivery for cholangiocytes? A 2026 antibody report (pIgR-engaging bispecifics, abstract level) names ARPKD in its introduction as a disease hard to reach with circulating antibodies.
Q5. Is there a primary, citable report of AAV-associated liver failure in children with pre-existing cholangiopathy, and does it apply to congenital hepatic fibrosis? The 2020 editorial on the myotubular myopathy deaths describes liver toxicity at high dose but does not mention pre-existing cholangiopathy.
Q6. Would activating long-form PDE4 (LoAc® compounds) reduce cyst growth in ARPKD cholangiocytes without worsening stellate-cell fibrosis? Published PDE4D data point the other way for fibrosis.
BIOMARKERS & TRIALS
Q7. What liver endpoint could regulators accept for ARPKD-CHF: a stiffness trend, platelet count and spleen size, a blood marker, or an imaging tracer? What are the pediatric reference ranges, and how should values from different elastography methods be compared?
Q8. Is PKHD1 detectable in healthy children's cholangiocytes in the pediatric liver atlas, and do adult-derived findings (for example the OLR1+ macrophages) hold at a pediatric baseline?
MODELS & CELLS
Q9. Can urine-derived cells from children with ARPKD-CHF yield cholangiocyte or assembloid models, and which labs are set up to receive samples with proper consent? A 2018 study used urine-derived kidney epithelial cells to test splicing of ciliopathy-gene variants (Molinari et al., cited in a 2026 review).
IMMUNE–CHOLANGIOCYTE INTERACTIONS
Q10. Do PKHD1-deficient human cholangiocytes and ARPKD-CHF livers show immune interactions like the T cell–cholangiocyte CD226–NECTIN2 signal found in the Pkhd1-dependent NOD.c3c4 mouse? Is peribiliary T-cell infiltration part of ARPKD-CHF, and could immune-modulating antibodies be tested in that model?
CELL THERAPY
Q11. Could patient-derived, gene-corrected cholangiocyte organoids be delivered into the bile ducts of a Pkhd1-deficient model, and would the corrected cells engraft and persist in cystic, fibrotic ducts as they do in healthy or acutely injured ones?
