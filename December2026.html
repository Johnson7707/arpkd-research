🧬 Molecular Biology & Biliary Cascade

🧬 MOLECULAR BIOLOGY & BILIARY CASCADE

⭐ FINDING — Collagen I is an active instructor of cholangiocyte specification and tubulogenesis: excess collagen I in ARPKD-CHF may drive abnormal ductal plate formation as a developmental feedback loop. ⭐ FINDING · Nov 2, 2026

Source: Thelwall IG, Morell CM, Dziedzicka D, Cabriales L, Hodgson A, Roos FJM, Elfari L, Vallier L, Chalut KJ. "Mechanical signalling through collagen I regulates cholangiocyte specification and tubulogenesis during liver development." Development 2026. Preprint: bioRxiv 2025.01.22.633528. Vallier lab (Charité Berlin) + Chalut lab (Cambridge). Ludovic Vallier is on this newsletter's distribution list.

What they did: Used StemBond hydrogels — collagen I-coated at varying stiffnesses — to investigate the influence of the ECM microenvironment on cholangiocyte differentiation from hiPSCs. Found that collagen I strongly promotes cholangiocyte specification (KRT19+) independently of matrix stiffness. Identified integrin α2β1 (ITGα2β1) as the primary mechanoreceptor co-localizing with cholangiocyte marker KRT19. Showed that inhibiting ITGα2β1-collagen I binding during tubulogenesis produces aberrant bile duct tube formation. First demonstration that collagen type I is essential for both biliary epithelial cell fate choice and morphogenesis.

⭐ ARPKD-CHF developmental feedback loop: In ARPKD-CHF, FPC loss → excess collagen I production via CTGF/TGF-β cascade → elevated periportal collagen I → abnormal bias toward cholangiocyte specification in liver progenitors → defective biliary tubulogenesis = ductal plate malformation. Collagen I excess in ARPKD-CHF may actively worsen ductal plate malformation by misguiding progenitor fate decisions — independently of FPC's direct effects on primary cilia signaling. This is a new mechanistic connection linking fibrotic ECM remodeling to developmental biliary pathology.

Connection to existing framework: Waddell/Boulter 2026 (Dis Model Mech): ECM primary defect in CHF is laminin/collagen IV LOSS; Yasoshima 2009 (J Pathol): basement membrane degraded in human CHF and Caroli disease by plasminogen/tPA overexpression. Now add: collagen I excess (fibrotic component) may simultaneously promote abnormal cholangiocyte fate specification via ITGα2β1. Two arms of the ECM story — loss of BM collagen IV AND gain of interstitial collagen I — both pathogenic by different mechanisms.

⚠️ Limits: hiPSC-derived model only. Not tested in Pkhd1-deficient cholangiocytes or ARPKD liver tissue. Developmental interpretation extrapolated from a stem cell differentiation model. Whether excess collagen I in an already-formed fibrotic ARPKD-CHF liver continues to misdirect any remaining progenitors is unknown.

Open Questions

Open Questions for the Research Community

These questions came out of this month's reading and are not answered in the sources we found. If your work addresses any of them — preliminary data, an unpublished observation, or a paper we missed — a brief reply is all we ask.

GENETICS & VARIANTS
Q1. Which PKHD1 variants cause liver-predominant disease? Cohorts so far (Burgmaier's zones, Zhao, Lapunzina-Soler, and Bhuva's nine congenital hepatic fibrosis cases) have not been pooled into one table of variant, position and phenotype. Do missense variants in the C-terminal ectodomain (Zone 3, AA 2625–4074) cluster in liver-predominant cases? A 2025 series of prenatally diagnosed Caroli-type cases found three of four missense variants in this region, but the numbers are tiny. An older French cohort (Denamur 2010) lists missense variants in the same region (W2736G, I2957T, R3240K) among children who died perinatally, so the region is not liver-only. Small case reports point both ways (Giacobbe 2022; and a prenatally diagnosed Caroli case with one missense variant in this region who needed kidney and liver transplants by 16 months, Rivas 2019).
Q2. Does an interior-exon missense variant of uncertain significance, such as c.10926G>A (p.Met3642Ile), change splicing? Precedents exist (R760H through an enhancer motif; a last-nucleotide missense with exon 40 skipping). Would a minigene assay or urine-cell RNA testing settle it? (A SpliceAI and Pangolin lookup on Oct 8 predicted no splice effect, with all scores 0.05 or lower; these tools miss enhancer-mediated skipping.)
Q3. Do modifier genes beyond PKHD1 explain the wide variation in ARPKD severity, including liver involvement? Does the UK PKD genetics fellowship's dataset include ARPKD patients? Candidates raised in the older literature, from animal work, include HNF1B, KIF12 and interactions with PKD1 and PKD2; human evidence is lacking. A 2018 mouse study found that a truncating Pkhd1 variant caused clinical biliary disease only on the NOD autoimmune background (Huang et al.).
DELIVERY & THERAPY
Q4. How can cholangiocytes be reached? Five clinical AAV capsids transduced them poorly in perfused human livers. Has anyone screened capsids or tried retrograde bile-duct delivery for cholangiocytes? A 2026 antibody report (pIgR-engaging bispecifics, abstract level) names ARPKD in its introduction as a disease hard to reach with circulating antibodies.
Q5. Is there a primary, citable report of AAV-associated liver failure in children with pre-existing cholangiopathy, and does it apply to congenital hepatic fibrosis? The 2020 editorial on the myotubular myopathy deaths describes liver toxicity at high dose but does not mention pre-existing cholangiopathy.
Q6. Would activating long-form PDE4 (LoAc® compounds) reduce cyst growth in ARPKD cholangiocytes without worsening stellate-cell fibrosis? Published PDE4D data point the other way for fibrosis.
BIOMARKERS & TRIALS
Q7. What liver endpoint could regulators accept for ARPKD-CHF: a stiffness trend, platelet count and spleen size, a blood marker, or an imaging tracer? What are the pediatric reference ranges, and how should values from different elastography methods be compared?
Q8. Is PKHD1 detectable in healthy children's cholangiocytes in the pediatric liver atlas, and do adult-derived findings (for example the OLR1+ macrophages) hold at a pediatric baseline?
MODELS & CELLS
Q9. Can urine-derived cells from children with ARPKD-CHF yield cholangiocyte or assembloid models, and which labs are set up to receive samples with proper consent? A 2018 study used urine-derived kidney epithelial cells to test splicing of ciliopathy-gene variants (Molinari et al., cited in a 2026 review).
IMMUNE–CHOLANGIOCYTE INTERACTIONS
Q10. Do PKHD1-deficient human cholangiocytes and ARPKD-CHF livers show immune interactions like the T cell–cholangiocyte CD226–NECTIN2 signal found in the Pkhd1-dependent NOD.c3c4 mouse? Is peribiliary T-cell infiltration part of ARPKD-CHF, and could immune-modulating antibodies be tested in that model?
CELL THERAPY
Q11. Could patient-derived, gene-corrected cholangiocyte organoids be delivered into the bile ducts of a Pkhd1-deficient model, and would the corrected cells engraft and persist in cystic, fibrotic ducts as they do in healthy or acutely injured ones?
