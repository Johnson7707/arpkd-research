TGR5–FXR Asymmetry: Two Bile Acid Receptors, Two Different Problems

Both bile acid receptors in cholangiocytes fail when FPC is absent. (Masyuk TV, LaRusso NF et al. ACS 2025. PMC12261218) TGR5 is displaced from cilia → plasma membrane → couples with Gαs (NOT Gαi) → pro-cystogenic cAMP increase. FXR requires ciliary bile acid sensing → impaired → fibrogenic cascade. Key asymmetry: pharmacological FXR agonists (linafexor) bypass the ciliary sensing requirement. TGR5 restoration requires ciliary architecture restoration.

⭐ FRAMEWORK CORRECTION (September 1, 2026): TGR5 ANTAGONISM is the correct therapeutic approach in ARPKD-CHF — NOT agonism. SBI-364 (first-in-class competitive TGR5 antagonist, LaRusso/Masyuk group): reduces hepatic cysts 41.7% and renal cysts 30.4% in PCK rat. TGR5−/−;Pkhd1del2/del2 mice: 45% hepatic cyst reduction vs Pkhd1del2/del2 alone. SBI-364 + pasireotide: 50% combined reduction. (LaRusso NF, Masyuk TV et al. ACS 2025. PMC12261218) INT-767 (dual FXR/TGR5 AGONIST) — the TGR5 agonist component is potentially harmful in ARPKD-CHF where TGR5 is plasma membrane-localized and pro-cystogenic via Gαs. Recommended approach: FXR AGONIST (linafexor) + TGR5 ANTAGONIST (SBI-364) + SOMATOSTATIN (SSTR2). Editorial note: The conclusion that INT-767 may be harmful in ARPKD-CHF is our interpretation of the published LaRusso/Masyuk data. Dr. LaRusso and Dr. Masyuk have not made this statement directly — we welcome any correction from their group.

CDK1 Is the Convergence Output of BOTH YAP Overactivation Pathways

Two independent YAP overactivation routes exist in ARPKD-CHF: (1) FPC loss → FXR impaired → FXR-YAP brake absent → YAP overactivated (Djouder N et al. Nat Cell Biol 2021. DOI: 10.1038/s41556-021-00666-z); (2) FPC loss → Scribble dysregulation → YAP overactivated (Fabris L, Strazzabosco M et al. J Hepatol 76(5):1386-1403, 2022. DOI: 10.1016/j.jhep.2022.01.017). YAP drives CDK1 expression. CDK1 is now confirmed as the top biliary proliferative driver (iScience June 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.isci.2026.113369). CDK1 inhibition therefore suppresses biliary expansion regardless of which YAP pathway dominates — making CDK1 more strategically positioned than framed. Research question for Strazzabosco: Is CDK1 upregulated in your PKHD1-/- mouse model downstream of Scribble/YAP?

Spermidine and TDI01 Both Preserve Hepatocyte Zonation via Wnt2/Wnt9b

Chain: Spermidine → LSEC restoration / TDI01 → LSEC fenestrations restored → both maintain LSEC Wnt2 and Wnt9b production (Zhang K et al. Cell 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2026.02.001) (confirmed by single-cell spatial transcriptomics, PMC9588996 — only Wnt2 and Wnt9b from pericentrally-located ECs control hepatocyte zonation; not WNT2A which is a distinct gene) → hepatocyte zonation maintained. Neither therapeutic is framed this way. Both address hepatocyte zonation loss as an undocumented third mechanism of benefit.

The Complete Bexotegrast → PICP Monitoring Chain

αvβ6 integrin (upregulated on FPC-deficient BECs) → activates latent TGFβ → SMAD3 → LOXL2 → collagen crosslinking → PICP neoepitope released into blood → PICP biosensor measurement (Mederacke I et al. Hepatology 2021. DOI: 10.1002/hep.32098 — αvβ6 in biliary fibrosis). Bexotegrast → reduced αvβ6 → reduced TGFβ → reduced LOXL2 → reduced PICP. The PICP biosensor we recommended to Hartung is the logical monitoring tool for a bexotegrast trial in ARPKD-CHF. Full mechanistic chain from intervention to biomarker now documented.

The ERCP Experiment That Could Answer Six Questions at Once

Three published methodologies never combined for ARPKD: ERCP retrograde delivery (Guimont 2021. DOI: 10.1016/j.omtm.2021.03.004) + bile-derived cholangiocyte organoids (Soroka CJ et al. Hepatology 2019. DOI: 10.1002/hep.30542PSC bile via ERCP) + Sampaziotis cholangiocyte organoid protocol (emailed August 24). The missing experiment: ARPKD patient bile collected during a clinically indicated ERCP → patient-specific PKHD1-mutant cholangiocyte organoids. This platform could simultaneously: (1) measure FXR activity; (2) assess TGR5 localization; (3) test CDK1 inhibitor; (4) test ECM restoration; (5) test linafexor; (6) validate urine RNA for Met3642Ile. No ARPKD patient-derived cholangiocyte organoids from ERCP bile currently exist.

Hirschfield GM et al. (INTEGRIS-PSC). J Hepatol 84:86-98, 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.jhep.2025.09.014. Phase 2 double-blind, 117 PSC patients, up to 40 weeks. ⭐ LZTFL1 — Progressive Secondary Ciliary Dysfunction as ARPKD-CHF Advances

LZTFL1 (BBSome regulator, controls ciliary trafficking of GPCRs + Smoothened) is markedly downregulated in fibrotic liver tissue. Sci Rep 16, 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-43852-7. BBSome mediates ciliary localization of TGR5 and SSTR2 — both GPCRs whose ciliary displacement drives ARPKD-CHF progression. Seo S et al. PLoS Genet 7:e1002358, 2011. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pgen.1002358. Proposed self-amplifying loop (hypothesis — ARPKD-specific testing not yet done): FPC absent → primary ciliary dysfunction → biliary fibrosis → LZTFL1 downregulated → BBSome impaired → TGR5 + SSTR2 further displaced from cilia → somatostatin becomes less effective → more cystogenesis → more fibrosis. Any treatment that reduces fibrosis (CDK1 inhibitor, TXA, FXR agonist) may also preserve LZTFL1 as a downstream benefit — maintaining somatostatin responsiveness.

⭐ HNF4G — Fourth Mechanism for Hepatocellular Dysfunction

HNF4G drives cholangiocyte → hepatocyte transdifferentiation via global chromatin reorganization. In ARPKD-CHF, FPC-deficient cholangiocytes may upregulate HNF4G → biphenotypic cells — neither proper cholangiocytes nor proper hepatocytes. Four mechanisms for hepatocellular dysfunction now documented: (1) PHT → Wnt2/Wnt9b loss → zonation; (2) DPM → periportal hepatocyte abnormality; (3) FXR impairment → lost hepatocyte identity signal; (4) HNF4G-driven cholangiocyte transdifferentiation. Galanakis V, Gribben C, Vallier L et al. — J Hepatol 2025. DOI: 10.1016/j.jhep.2025.08.014. Cambridge Stem Cell Institute / University of Cambridge.

SPTAN1 (α-II spectrin) is reduced in FPC-deficient kidney CNT/collecting duct cells → loosened GEF/RAC1 lattice → RAC1 hyperactivation (Kuraoka S, Morizane R et al. Adv Sci 2026. 📌 NEW August 26, 2026 — ECM in ARPKD-CHF: Primary Defect is Loss, Not Gain

Source: Waddell SH, Brennan E, Boulter L. Dis. Model. Mech. 19(8):dmm052835, August 24, 2026. DOI: 10.1242/dmm.052835. PMID: 42634972. Cancer Research UK Scotland Centre, University of Edinburgh. First author Scott Waddell — permission granted September 4, 2026. Research focus: "understanding inflammation in fibrocystic liver disease." Senior author Luke Boulter (l.boulter@ed.ac.uk) — new contact.

Critical reframing for ARPKD-CHF therapy: In most fibrotic diseases, the goal is to reduce ECM deposition. In CHF/ARPKD, the primary ECM defect is the opposite — loss of the normal biliary basement membrane.

  • What is lost: Laminin and type IV collagen — normally present in the BEC basement membrane — are absent in CHF and Caroli disease (confirmed in human patients and PCK rat)
  • How: Pkhd1-deficient BECs generate plasmin (via tissue plasminogen activator) → degrades laminin and collagen IV + fibronectin + CTGF
  • The consequence: Loss of normal basement membrane → pro-fibrotic niche → the periportal fibrosis develops as a secondary response to this structural void
  • Therapeutic implication: "Restoring basement membrane integrity, rather than simply suppressing fibrosis, may represent a more appropriate therapeutic objective in selected developmental cholangiopathies."
  • α2β1-integrin inhibition reduces cyst number; SMAD3 inhibition reduces fibronectin and cyst growth (Waddell SH et al. Sci Transl Med 15:eabq2491, 2023. DOI: 10.1126/scitranslmed.abq2491 — α2β1-integrin + SMAD3 inhibition in PLD biliary models)

Self-amplifying feedback circuits

These circuits explain why CHF worsens despite a static PKHD1 mutation. They are proposed mechanisms derived from published evidence — not yet validated in PKHD1-specific models.

Circuit 1 — CXCL10 → STAT3 positive feedback

Cholangiocyte injury → CXCL10 → Macrophages → IL-6 → STAT3 → SPP1 → fibrosis → MORE injury ⟳

ACT-777991 (Phase 1 cleared) at the CXCL10/CXCR3 node breaks both the inflammatory cascade AND the STAT3/SPP1 cascade simultaneously — more potent than a single-node intervention. Evidence: Kaffe, Strazzabosco et al. 2018; Comm Biol 2026.

Circuit 2 — Spleen-liver amplification loop

Fibrosis → Portal hypertension → Splenomegaly → TRNP1hi CD8+ T cells → INSR-α → MORE HSC ⟳

TRNP1hi CD8+ T cell mechanism: Nature Genetics 2026 (MASH). Extension to ARPKD proposed by Nathan Johnson, July 2026. TDI01 reduces portal pressure → may break this loop.

PKHD1 / FIBROCYSTIN (FPC) — MOLECULAR FUNCTION: COMPLETE CURRENT PICTURE

Despite 20+ years of research, FPC molecular function remains incompletely understood. Here is what we now know — and what remains unknown.

Notch-like processing — the unifying framework: (Hogan MC et al. J Mol Med 92:1061-1072, 2014. DOI: 10.1007/s00109-014-1185-7) FPC undergoes regulated 3-step proteolytic cleavage: C1 (proprotein convertase, Golgi) → PECD/PTM heterodimer. C2 (ADAM metalloprotease, triggered by unknown extracellular ligand) → PECD shed into urine as soluble protein or EV-bound polycystin complex (FPC+PC1+PC2) — basis for Chung's uEV therapy. C3 (γ-secretase) → releases FPC-CTT with ciliary targeting (CTS), mitochondrial (MLS→ICD15), and nuclear (NLS) sequences. FPC loss blocks all six downstream functions simultaneously.

1. Ciliary sensing/signaling: FPC+PC1 synergistically suppress the cilia-dependent cyst activation (CDCA) signal. Extracellular ligand unknown after 20+ years.

2. SRC→STAT3 suppression (DIRECT mechanism for cascade node): (Dafinger C, Liebau MC et al. J Clin Invest 130:3756-3771, 2020. DOI: 10.1172/JCI130503) FPC-CTT forms dynamic protein complex with SRC kinase. FPC-CTT directly suppresses SRC → prevents STAT3 pY705. FPC loss → SRC constitutively active → pSTAT3 → SPP1 → macrophage cascade. Confirmed in human ARPKD cyst-lining cells. cAMP/PKA (elevated by FPC loss) further activates SRC → double amplification. JAK2 also in complex → JAK inhibitors (ruxolitinib, baricitinib) are candidate therapeutics.

3. ⭐ Ubiquitination via NDFIP2/SMURF/NEDD4: (Kaimori JY, Germino GG et al. Nat Commun 8:1520, 2017. DOI: 10.1038/s41467-017-01378-x; confirmed Guay-Woodford LM et al. Pediatr Nephrol 2026. DOI: 10.1007/s00467-025-07129-x) FPC-CTT co-localizes with NDFIP2 (HECT E3 ligase adaptor). FPC loss → SMURF1/SMURF2/NEDD4 mislocalized → (a) SMURF dysfunction → TGF-β receptors/Smads not properly degraded → TGF-β pathway hyperactivated — DIRECT FPC-to-fibrosis mechanism, parallel to and independent of macrophage cascade; (b) NEDD4 → RhoA upregulation → cystogenesis; (c) NEDD4 → ENaC retention → sodium reabsorption → direct mechanism for ARPKD hypertension. Zone 3 variants (AA 2625-4074, including our daughter's c.9370C>T at AA 3124 and c.10926G>A at AA 3642) specifically disrupt CTT function → explaining why Zone 3 variants cause more severe liver disease via loss of SMURF-mediated TGF-β regulation in cholangiocytes. (Lapunzina-Soler P et al. Genes 17(2):229, 2026. DOI: 10.3390/genes17020229 — Zone 3 genotype-phenotype data)

4. Mitochondrial function via ICD15: (Walker RV, Qian F et al. Nat Commun 14:3217, 2023. DOI: 10.1038/s41467-023-38688-0) ICD15 → mitochondria → maintains structural integrity and bioenergetics. FPC loss → mitochondrial defects confirmed by EM. GDF15 upregulated as stress response → brain-liver glucocorticoid anti-inflammatory (Wang D, Steinberg GR et al. Cell Metab 2026. Breakthrough: CFTR mislocalization in ARPKD

The published finding (2021-2023) — not yet acted on

FPC deficiency → HSP27↓ / HSP70/90↑ → CFTR mislocalized to apical membrane only → excessive Cl⁻ secretion into bile duct lumen → biliary cyst fluid accumulation. [Cebotaru, Outeda et al., Cell Mol Gastroenterol Hepatol 2021, PMID: 34329764]

⭐⭐ Eight new findings from Cebotaru lab papers (September 2, 2026)

  1. HSP70 and HSP90 are therapeutic targets, not just biomarkers. Silencing either HSP70 or HSP90 alone inhibits cyst growth in FPC-null cholangiocytes in 3D culture. HSP90 has a direct independent role in fibrosis etiology. This connects the HSP dysregulation cascade to the PAK4/LOXL2 fibrosis node — HSP90 inhibition is a druggable target in ARPKD-CHF. (Yanda MK, Zeidan A, Cebotaru L. Am J Physiol Gastrointest Liver Physiol 324:G404–G414, 2023. DOI: 10.1152/ajpgi.00255.2022)
  2. CFTR and PC2 are both increased in primary cilia of ARPKD cholangiocytes — not simply absent. VX-809 restored both CFTR and PC2 cilia levels toward wild type. This is more nuanced than simple mislocalization — a ciliary overcompensation is occurring, consistent with dysregulated ciliary gating when FPC is absent. DOI: 10.1152/ajpgi.00255.2022
  3. ⭐ Standard liver and kidney function tests are NORMAL despite active histological disease. ALB, AST, ALT, BUN, and creatinine were all within normal range at 35 days in Pkhd1del3-4/del3-4 mice even while cholangiocyte proliferation was dramatically elevated and bile ducts were malformed. Biochemical markers lag histological disease. Clinical labs will not detect early ARPKD-CHF — only imaging (MRE) or histology will. Direct implication for trial endpoint design. DOI: 10.1152/ajpgi.00255.2022
  4. Liver disease precedes kidney disease — 60% liver phenotype at 3 months vs only 20% severe kidney phenotype. Treatment at PND5 (5 days old) is early intervention during active liver disease progression. Human equivalent: weeks 20-40 gestation through first 1-2 years of life is the critical biliary window. DOI: 10.1152/ajpgi.00255.2022
  5. AAV1 transduces both cholangiocytes AND hepatocytes in ARPKD liver. Not just cyst cells — surrounding hepatocytes are also efficiently transduced by IP-injected AAV1 in ARPKD mice. This is an advantage: hepatocyte CFTR regulates ER stress independently, adding a second therapeutic mechanism. DOI: 10.1152/ajpgi.00109.2025
  6. α2,3-Sialic acid (MAL) is the dominant AAV1 receptor on ARPKD biliary cyst surfaces — present at high levels apically and basolaterally on cysts, absent from normal bile ducts. α2,6-sialic acid (SNA) is present but lower. This explains why systemically injected AAV1 preferentially targets ARPKD cysts: the sialic acid receptors are basolaterally exposed on cyst membranes, accessible from the bloodstream via IP injection. Hepatocytes surrounding cysts show both MAL and SNA, decreased in ARPKD. DOI: 10.1152/ajpgi.00109.2025 · DOI: 10.1152/ajpcell.00057.2025

PKHD1 / FIBROCYSTIN (FPC) — MOLECULAR FUNCTION: COMPLETE CURRENT PICTURE

Despite 20+ years of research, FPC molecular function remains incompletely understood. Here is what we now know — and what remains unknown.

Notch-like processing — the unifying framework: (Hogan MC et al. J Mol Med 92:1061-1072, 2014. DOI: 10.1007/s00109-014-1185-7) FPC undergoes regulated 3-step proteolytic cleavage: C1 (proprotein convertase, Golgi) → PECD/PTM heterodimer. C2 (ADAM metalloprotease, triggered by unknown extracellular ligand) → PECD shed into urine as soluble protein or EV-bound polycystin complex (FPC+PC1+PC2) — basis for Chung's uEV therapy. C3 (γ-secretase) → releases FPC-CTT with ciliary targeting (CTS), mitochondrial (MLS→ICD15), and nuclear (NLS) sequences. FPC loss blocks all six downstream functions simultaneously.

1. Ciliary sensing/signaling: FPC+PC1 synergistically suppress the cilia-dependent cyst activation (CDCA) signal. Extracellular ligand unknown after 20+ years.

2. SRC→STAT3 suppression (DIRECT mechanism for cascade node): (Dafinger C, Liebau MC et al. J Clin Invest 130:3756-3771, 2020. DOI: 10.1172/JCI130503) FPC-CTT forms dynamic protein complex with SRC kinase. FPC-CTT directly suppresses SRC → prevents STAT3 pY705. FPC loss → SRC constitutively active → pSTAT3 → SPP1 → macrophage cascade. Confirmed in human ARPKD cyst-lining cells. cAMP/PKA (elevated by FPC loss) further activates SRC → double amplification. The developmental distinction: ARPKD-CHF is fundamentally a developmental disease. The structurally abnormal intrahepatic bile ducts are present from birth. The postnatal FXR-YAP → SPP1 → TREM2 → fibrosis cascade described throughout this document is the CONSEQUENCE of the DPM, not its cause. Postnatal therapies can slow fibrosis progression; they cannot reverse the structural DPM. Only two approaches address established DPM: (1) intervention during the neonatal developmental window; (2) postnatal biliary cell replacement via ERCP (cholangiocyte organoid transplantation — Sampaziotis F, Vallier L et al. Science 371:839-846, 2021. DOI: 10.1126/science.aaz6462).

⭐ MECHANISM 1 — CFTR and cord hollowing (most therapeutically actionable): Cord hollowing — the tubulogenesis process creating bile duct lumina — requires CFTR at the apical cholangiocyte membrane to secrete chloride and water into nascent lumina. (Yanda MK, Zeidan A, Cebotaru L. Am J Physiol GI 324:G404–G414, 2023. DOI: 10.1152/ajpgi.00255.2022): FPC-null cholangiocytes have dramatically reduced apical CFTR → impaired cord hollowing → failed tubulogenesis → DPM. VX-809 in 5-day-old Pkhd1 mice improved liver pathology. Critical neonatal window: ductal plate remodeling continues postnatally (P1-P14 mouse; weeks 20-40 gestation→neonatal in humans). CFTR correction during this window could potentially reduce DPM severity — not just slow postnatal fibrosis. Trikafta (VX-445) neonatal testing in PKHD1 models is an unanswered question (Q23 in this document). Cebotaru (JHU, already in network) is positioned to answer this. (Ciobanu C, Outeda P, Guggino WB, Cebotaru L. Am J Physiol GI 329:G434–G442, 2025. DOI: 10.1152/ajpgi.00109.2025): CFTR gene therapy for ARPKD — direct extension of this mechanism.

MECHANISM 2 — Non-canonical Wnt5a/PCP disruption: FPC in primary cilia maintains planar cell polarity (PCP) during ductal plate morphogenesis. FPC loss → disrupted ciliary PCP signaling → Wnt5a abnormally activated → failed planar arrangement of ductal plate cells → structurally abnormal duct formation. SOX9/Notch amplification: Sox9b↔Notch positive feedback maintains biliary identity; FPC loss disrupts this → reduced SOX9 → impaired branching morphogenesis (SOX9 loss causes DPM-like phenotype — bioRxiv 2024. DOI: 10.1101/2024.09.18.613676). Confirmed in cholangiopathy models: Wnt5a drives biliary PCP disruption → ductular reaction + portal fibroblast accumulation. (Tsunoda T et al. J Hepatol 71:333-344, 2019. DOI: 10.1016/j.jhep.2019.01.024; CELSR1/PCP upregulation confirmed in PCK rat: Sci Rep 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-43852-7) New research question (Q24): Is Wnt5a/PCP disrupted in FPC-deficient ductal plate cells during the developmental window — and can PCP pathway modulators reduce DPM severity neonatally?

Five major gaps and curative approaches not previously in this framework

⚠️ GAP 1 — Portal fibroblasts, not HSCs, are the primary fibrosis-producing cells in biliary disease: Lineage tracing confirmed portal fibroblasts contribute >70% of myofibroblasts at onset in cholestatic/biliary liver injury. (Mederacke I et al. PNAS 111:14516-14521, 2014. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1414373111) HSCs dominate only in hepatotoxic injury. Our cascade throughout attributes fibrosis to HSC activation — likely incorrect for ARPKD-CHF as a biliary disease. Portal fibroblasts are periductal, self-renewing, apoptosis-resistant, and express PDGFR-β — explaining why Peltz's PDGFR-β inhibitors worked and why CDH11 (Huch assembloid, portal mesenchyme) is an anti-fibrotic target. Framework correction: distinguish portal fibroblasts (primary biliary responders) from HSCs (secondary) throughout cascade descriptions.

⚠️ GAP 2 — Ferroptosis in congenital cholestatic liver disease — YAP/FBXL5 connects to our root cascade (Ohta Y et al. Am J Pathol 196(3):700-715, 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.ajpath.2025.11.008): Iron + bile acids → GPX4 depletion → ferroptosis in congenital cholestatic pediatric liver disease. The hepatorenal molecular linkage — why one gene causes two diseases

The most fundamental question in ARPKD biology. The answer reveals shared therapeutic targets, explains divergent drug effects, and defines the sequential organ failure pattern that determines prognosis.

Fibrocystin — one protein, two organs

FPC is expressed on primary cilia of both renal collecting duct cells AND biliary epithelial cells (Masyuk TV et al. J Clin Invest 111:549, 2003. DOI: 10.1172/JCI200318499). These are the only two cell types requiring high levels of FPC for normal function — precisely why ARPKD affects only kidneys and liver.

The mechanism is identical in both: FPC deficiency → impaired cilia Ca²⁺ entry → elevated cAMP (Masyuk AI et al. PNAS 103:5466, 2006. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.0602064103) → proliferative and pro-secretory cascades. But disease manifestation diverges because V2R (the primary cAMP driver in collecting ducts) is absent in biliary cells — which is why tolvaptan works in the kidney but has no direct effect on CHF.

⚠ Divergent drug effects — where treating one organ harms the other

mTOR inhibitors: Beneficial in kidney (reduces cyst proliferation). Harmful in liver via three mechanisms: (1) autophagy → HDAC6 → ARL3/ARL13B depletion from biliary cilia (Masyuk AI et al. Hepatology 75:1110, 2022. DOI: 10.1002/hep.32298); (2) suppresses HNF4G → impairs cholangiocyte regenerative plasticity (Galanakis V, Vallier L et al. J Hepatol 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.jhep.2025.09.021); (3) possible direct biliary fibrosis worsening. Every ARPKD trial using mTOR inhibitors MUST include hepatic endpoints — three reasons, not one.

Statins: Beneficial in cirrhotic portal hypertension. Potentially harmful in non-cirrhotic portal hypertension (Uschner FE et al. Hepatology 61(5):1648-1659, 2015. DOI: 10.1002/hep.27603 — activate canonical Hedgehog → aggravate portal hypertension). ARPKD-CHF portal hypertension is partially non-cirrhotic. Caution warranted without ARPKD-specific data.

Why gene therapy is the logical endpoint

FPC restoration via lentiviral delivery (Gissen — Gissen P et al., personal communication July 2026) or correcting FPC mislocalization via elexacaftor would address both organs simultaneously — because the protein dysfunction is identical in both cell types. This is the fundamental biological rationale for gene therapy as the definitive therapeutic strategy for ARPKD: one intervention, two diseases.

Kidney disease — complete framework

ARPKD is a hepatorenal disease. Both sides require understanding. This section was added July 24, 2026 following recognition that the kidney side had been under-represented in the framework.

Root kidney mechanism

Kidney cysts arise from collecting ducts and distal tubules — not glomeruli (Guay-Woodford LM et al. Pediatric Nephrology 29:1289-1301, 2014. DOI: 10.1007/s00467-013-2456-6). FPC-deficient primary cilia → calcium entry failure → elevated cAMP (Masyuk TV et al. J Clin Invest 111:549-557, 2003. DOI: 10.1172/JCI200318499; Masyuk AI et al. PNAS 103:5466-5471, 2006. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.0602064103) → PKA → Src/STAT3 (Dafinger C, Liebau MC et al. J Clin Invest 130:3756-3771, 2020. DOI: 10.1172/JCI130503) → epithelial proliferation and fluid secretion → cyst expansion.

The vasopressin/V2R loop: ARPKD kidneys lose concentrating ability early → the body secretes more vasopressin (AVP) to compensate → AVP binds V2R on collecting duct cells → more cAMP → faster cyst growth. A self-amplifying cycle. This is why tolvaptan (V2R antagonist) is now in ARPKD-specific trials. (Torres VE et al. (TEMPO 3:4). Kidney Int 96:434-445, 2019. DOI: 10.1016/j.kint.2019.02.030 — tolvaptan ADPKD mechanism; NCT04786574 — ARPKD tolvaptan study withdrawn before enrollment)

SCY-770 — First direct AMPK activator in clinical development for PKD (June 2026)

SCYNEXIS acquired SCY-770 (formerly PXL-770, Poxel) in March 2026 for up to $196M. FDA Orphan Drug Designation. Phase 1 initiated June 30, 2026 with Phase 2 ADPKD proof-of-concept trial targeted for Q4 2026. (SCYNEXIS press release March 2026 — Company reported, not peer-reviewed) More potent and direct than metformin or bempedoic acid. ADPKD-focused — ARPKD kidney and biliary data pending. Key mechanism: AMPK activation suppresses both mTOR/cAMP (kidney cysts) AND LATS1/2→YAP/TAZ (liver HSC fibrosis), providing potential dual-organ benefit.

SPTAN1/RAC1 (Kuraoka, Morizane et al., Adv Sci 2026): SPTAN1 (αII-spectrin) is reduced in PKHD1-deficient cells → activates RAC1/c-FOS → distal nephron cysts. RAC1 inhibitors reduced cystogenesis in human PKHD1-deficient organoids. SLC8A1 (sodium/calcium exchanger) is elevated in cystic epithelium — a new biomarker. RAC1 and SPTAN1 are confirmed druggable targets.

⚡ First-ever interventional trials in ARPKD — active now

Tolvaptan — Study 204 (NCT04786574, withdrawn before enrollment) + Study 307 (NCT04782258, Otsuka)
Two multinational trials testing tolvaptan in pediatric ARPKD. Study 204: neonates to 12 weeks, severe phenotype. Study 307: 28 days to 18 years. Both active, primary completion 2028. These are the first drug trials ever conducted in ARPKD.

Critical hepatorenal mortality finding

64-80% of mortality in ARPKD kidney transplant patients is from cholangitis and hepatobiliary sepsis — not kidney failure. Patients who successfully receive a kidney transplant are then dying from liver disease complications. Treating CHF is survival-critical for the post-transplant population, not just quality of life. Telega, Cronin, Avner, Pediatr Transplant 2013 (PMID 23593929)

Kidney progression biomarkers

  • haeTKV (height-adjusted total kidney volume): Best early predictor of kidney survival. Maximal haeTKV in first 18 months of life stratifies risk — highest quartile: mean eGFR of 21 ml/min/1.73m² at year 1 vs 101 in lowest quartile. (ARegPKD — Burgmaier K et al. Sci Rep 11:12138, 2021. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-021-91506-1)
  • eGFR: Late marker. ARPKD-specific data: Mean yearly decline = 1.3 ml/min/1.73m² (Burgmaier K et al. Kidney Int 107(2):314-325, 2025. DOI: 10.1016/j.kint.2024.09.015 — 658 patients). Kidney failure probability by age 20 = 50.1%. Note: the >3 ml/min/year "rapid progression" threshold is derived from ADPKD/ERA tolvaptan guidelines — not validated specifically for ARPKD.
  • Urine concentrating ability: Early defect confirmed in ARPKD. Drives AVP elevation → cAMP → accelerated cyst growth. Torres VE et al. JASN 15(12):3184-3192, 2004. PMID: 15579505 (PCK rat ARPKD model: AVP knockout nearly abolished cystogenesis).
  • Shrinking kidney phenotype: A subgroup develops shrinking (not enlarging) kidneys with progressive function loss. Size alone cannot monitor disease. (Gregory, Harris, Dahl, Kidney Int Reports 2026)

Second ARPKD gene — DZIP1L

Rare ARPKD-like disease is also caused by biallelic DZIP1L mutations (cilia transition zone protein), causing an identical phenotype including CHF. Genetic testing should include DZIP1L when PKHD1 sequencing is negative. (Lu H et al. Nat Genet 49:1025-1034, 2017. DOI: 10.1038/ng.3871)

⚠ Transplant decision framework: Isolated kidney transplant is appropriate when liver disease is minimal. Combined liver-kidney transplant should be considered when recurrent cholangitis or severe portal hypertension complications are present. Given that 64-80% of post-transplant mortality is from biliary sepsis, pre-transplant liver disease severity assessment is mandatory.

Neonatal ARPKD — the earliest presentation

Approximately 30-50% of ARPKD patients present in the neonatal period with massively enlarged kidneys. This phenotype carries the highest early mortality. (Guay-Woodford LM et al. Pediatric Nephrology 29:1289-1301, 2014. DOI: 10.1007/s00467-013-2456-6)

⚠ Pulmonary hypoplasia is the leading cause of neonatal death — not kidney failure

FPC-deficient kidneys lose concentrating ability in utero → reduced fetal urine output → oligohydramnios → Potter sequence: flat nasal bridge, low-set ears, limb positional deformities from uterine constraint, and pulmonary hypoplasia. Many neonates die of respiratory failure before kidney replacement therapy becomes relevant.

Bilateral nephrectomy decision

When massively enlarged kidneys compress the diaphragm and cause respiratory failure, bilateral nephrectomy may be performed as a life-saving intervention followed immediately by dialysis. This eliminates residual kidney function but respiratory survival takes priority. Otsuka's Study 204 (tolvaptan) is specifically designed for this most severe neonatal phenotype.

Anemia — two mechanisms

CKD reduces erythropoietin (EPO) → normocytic normochromic anemia. Erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESAs) are standard once eGFR falls below 30-40 ml/min/1.73m². (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) CKD-MBD Work Group. JASN 2010 — standard of care reference) Splenomegaly from portal hypertension causes hypersplenism → thrombocytopenia and hemolytic anemia — a separate mechanism that compounds EPO-deficiency anemia. Accurate diagnosis of the dominant mechanism is essential before treating.

No ARPKD-specific dietary trials exist. The following is extrapolated from cholestatic biliary disease literature (primarily PBC, which shares impaired bile flow — Hirschfield GM et al. PBC guidelines, J Hepatol 77(5):1469-1484, 2022. DOI: 10.1016/j.jhep.2021.11.016) and renal management literature — both apply simultaneously in ARPKD.

🔬 Diagnostics — What Standard Testing Misses

  • Deep-intronic PKHD1 (AJKD 2025. DOI: 10.1053/j.ajkd.2025.03.016): c.8643-2945 G>A — 2,945 bp into intron, ALL prediction tools gave benign score. Only long-read WGS + urine-derived tubular cell RNA detected it. Mechanism: donor-gain pseudo-exon inclusion (121 bp, premature stop). Non-invasive urine RNA analysis validated as diagnostic method. (Molinari E et al. JASN 31:2422, 2020. DOI: 10.1681/ASN.2020010010)
  • Genomic autopsy (PMID: 40816622, 2025): Trio exome/WGS achieves 100% diagnostic yield in atypical prenatal PKD. Biallelic PKD1 mimics ARPKD prenatally — absent oligohydramnios discriminates from ARPKD.
  • CYS1 as third ARPKD gene (Kidney Int 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.kint.2026.01.022): 3 patients, 2 families. ~24kb regulatory deletion (missed by exon sequencing) + c.4G>A myristoylation motif variant. Long-read WGS essential.
