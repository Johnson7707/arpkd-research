My name is Nathan Johnson. My wife Flora and I are the parents of Gianna, a child with ARPKD-CHF. Over the past fourteen months we have been conducting a systematic review of the literature across hepatology, biliary biology, gene therapy, organoid modeling, and genetics as they relate to this disease. What began as a family effort has grown into a network of 43 investigators across 12 countries, several of whom have already contributed answered questions, unpublished observations, and corrections that have meaningfully improved this work.
Section 1

Open Questions for the Research Community

If your work addresses any of these questions, a brief reply is all we ask. Preliminary data, an unpublished observation, or a paper we may have missed is equally valuable. Answered questions appear at the bottom of each field group.

MOLECULAR MECHANISMS & CELL SIGNALING
Q1. Does the SPTAN1/RAC1 pathway operate in ARPKD biliary epithelial cells? Confirmed in kidney CNT cells (Morizane, Adv Sci 2026) — untested in cholangiocytes.
Q2. Does CXCR3 blockade reduce biliary cyst fluid secretion, or only periportal fibrosis?
Q3. Is AMPK → LATS1/2 → YAP/TAZ the mechanism behind bempedoic acid's liver benefit in PCK rat?
Q4. Does tolvaptan (V2R antagonist) have any secondary effect on ARPKD biliary disease?
Q5. Has FXR been directly measured in PKHD1-deficient or fibrocystin-null biliary epithelial cells?
Q6. Is CDK1 upregulated downstream of Scribble/YAP dysregulation in PKHD1-/- mouse models?
Q7. Does somatostatin signaling through ciliary SSTR2 affect TGR5 ciliary localization in FPC-deficient cells?
Q8. Is SPTAN1 (α-II spectrin) reduced in FPC-deficient cholangiocytes, and does this disrupt integrin-BM anchoring?
Q9. ANSWERED — Does the SPTAN1/RAC1 pathway operate in ARPKD kidney cells?
Kuraoka S, Morizane R et al. confirmed RAC1/SPTAN1 cystogenesis pathway in ARPKD kidney collecting duct cells. Biliary cell confirmation remains an open question. (Adv Sci 2026. DOI: 10.1002/advs.202524001)
Q10. ANSWERED — Has ICD15 (the fibrocystin C-terminal mitochondrial domain) been studied in biliary cholangiocytes or CHF models?
Prof. Feng Qian (University of Maryland — Walker, Qian et al. Nat Commun 14:3217, 2023. Permission granted September 4, 2026):
"To my knowledge, I am not aware of any study that has specifically investigated the fibrocystin ICD15 domain, particularly its role in mitochondrial function, in biliary cholangiocytes."
The D67 digenic mouse model used in the 2023 paper limited liver phenotype analysis. Prof. Qian characterizes the ICD15-in-biliary-cells hypothesis as "interesting and biologically testable" and plans to extend ICD15 studies to liver/biliary cells.

⭐ Additional: Proximity of c.10926G>A (Met3642Ile) to the ICD15-encoding region raises the possibility that if this variant disrupts exon splicing, it could specifically alter ICD15 production. Liver is considerably more accessible to exogenous gene expression approaches than kidney. (Prof. Qian's scientific opinion — not a published finding)
ECM & BILIARY BASEMENT MEMBRANE
Q1. Has tranexamic acid or any plasmin inhibitor been tested in a PKHD1-deficient model for biliary laminin/collagen IV preservation?
Q2. Have PRO-C4 (type IV collagen formation) or C4M (type IV collagen degradation) been measured in any CHF or Caroli disease cohort?
Q3. What is the current clinical development status of INT-767 (dual FXR/TGR5 agonist)?
Q4. What agents can restore laminin or type IV collagen production in FPC-deficient biliary cells?
DRUG DISCOVERY & PHARMACOLOGY
Q1. Does ACT-777991 (dual somatostatin/dopamine agonist) reduce biliary fibrosis in a PKHD1-specific model?
Q2. Does SCY-770 (direct AMPK activator) benefit ARPKD biliary fibrosis independently of bempedoic acid?
Q3. Does elexacaftor simultaneously improve kidney AND biliary disease in the same PKHD1-deficient cells?
Q4. ANSWERED — Have CFTR correctors been tested in PKHD1-deficient biliary cells and animal models?
Prof. Liudmila Cebotaru (Johns Hopkins University. Permission granted September 2, 2026):
"The CFTR correctors would reduce the proliferation, inflammation, fibrosis and dilatation of bile ducts in PKHD1-deficient biliary cells, and animal models. Truncated form of CFTR, which was introduced in cells and mouse models works as a genetic corrector and has the ability to bind ER-retained CFTR, reduce ER calcium, and carry CFTR, PC2, PC1, and FPC to the correct cellular location."
Published support: (1) Yanda, Zeidan, Cebotaru — Am J Physiol GI 2023 · (2) Ciobanu, Outeda, Guggino, Cebotaru — Am J Physiol GI 2025 · (3) Ciobanu, Cebotaru — Am J Physiol Cell Physiol 2025
(1) HSP70 and HSP90 are druggable targets — silencing either inhibits cyst growth in FPC-null cholangiocytes in 3D culture.
(2) CFTR and PC2 are both INCREASED in primary cilia of ARPKD cholangiocytes. VX-809 restores both to normal.
(3) Standard liver and kidney function labs are NORMAL despite active histological ARPKD disease.
(4) Liver disease precedes kidney disease — 60% of ARPKD mice show liver phenotype at 3 months vs only 20% with severe kidney disease.
(5) AAV1 transduces both cholangiocytes AND hepatocytes in ARPKD liver.
(6) α2,3-Sialic acid (MAL) is the dominant AAV1 receptor on ARPKD biliary cyst surfaces — explains preferential targeting.
(7) Δ27-264-CFTR construct (~5,060 bp) fits within AAV packaging capacity.
(8) ⭐ Harris-Cebotaru scientific collaboration already exists — pkd1RC/RC ADPKD mice provided by Dr. Peter C. Harris (Mayo Clinic).
GENE THERAPY & DELIVERY
Q1. Can lentiviral delivery restore functional FPC expression in human PKHD1-deficient biliary organoids?
Q2. Can ERCP retrograde biliary delivery achieve sufficient transfection of intrahepatic bile ducts in PKHD1-deficient human-sized models?
Q3. Does KRT19-promoter restriction of AAV-base editor prevent hepatocyte off-target editing in ARPKD liver?
Q4. ANSWERED — Has biliary AAV gene therapy been tested in a PKHD1-specific model?
Prof. Holger Willenbring (UCSF. Permission granted September 3, 2026):
"Recent progress in gene delivery and genome editing technologies support the feasibility of gene therapy of the liver disease in ARPKD-CHF, which has been challenging because of the large size of the PKHD1 gene."
Q5. ANSWERED — Base editing PKD1/PKHD1 — what is the current state of the art?
Ibel A, Krappitz M, Kaminski MM et al. confirmed ABEmax base editing corrected a PKD1 missense variant in vivo. Only 5% liver editing efficiency was sufficient to produce significant liver cyst reduction in ADPKD mice. (Mol Ther 33(11):5373-5382, 2025. DOI: 10.1016/j.ymthe.2025.08.026. PMID: 40842155)
IMMUNOLOGY & MACROPHAGE BIOLOGY
Q1. Does bacteriology in ARPKD bile ducts differ from primary biliary disease? Which organisms predominate?
Q2. ANSWERED — What organisms predominate in ARPKD cholangitis?
E. coli (22-37%), Pseudomonas spp. (28%), Enterococcus faecalis (15-39%). COBRA trial: 1-day antibiotics after biliary drainage noninferior to 4-7 days (413 patients, 31 Dutch centers). (Cozma MA et al. Antibiotics 2025 · Overdevest AG et al. COBRA trial, Trials 2026)
CLINICAL & TRANSLATIONAL
Q1. Can ELF score (HA + PIIINP + TIMP-1) be validated as a serial endpoint for ARPKD-CHF clinical trials?
Q2. Has MRE liver stiffness been measured serially in ARPKD-CHF patients to assess treatment response?
Q3. Is SPP1/osteopontin measurable in ARPKD patient serum and does it correlate with CHF severity?
Q4. ANSWERED — What are validated non-biopsy endpoints for ARPKD-CHF clinical trials?
IMAGE-ARPKD trial (NCT07201025, Flask CA, Hartung EA, Dell KM et al., Pediatric Radiology 2026) confirms: MRE liver stiffness = 3.60 kPa in ARPKD patients with portal hypertension. Liver biopsy NOT recommended. Total kidney volume is not useful for ARPKD trials. MRE + SPP1 + ELF score are the recommended endpoints.
Q5. ANSWERED — What is the dominant cause of post-transplant mortality in ARPKD?
64-80% of mortality in ARPKD kidney transplant patients is from cholangitis and hepatobiliary sepsis — not kidney failure. Treating CHF is survival-critical, not just quality of life. (Telega, Cronin, Avner. Pediatr Transplant 2013. PMID: 23593929)
Q6. ANSWERED — Are mTOR inhibitors safe in ARPKD-CHF?
mTOR inhibitors are NOT safe for ARPKD patients with liver involvement. Three independent biliary harm mechanisms confirmed. Every ARPKD trial using mTOR inhibitors MUST include dedicated hepatic endpoints. (Masyuk AI et al. Hepatology 75(5):1110, 2022)

Q1. Can the Peltz/Stanford PKHD1 hepatic organoid platform be used for FXR agonist + SPP1 siRNA combination screening?
Q2. Has the Huch periportal assembloid been applied to ARPKD-CHF modeling using PKHD1-mutant cells?
Q3. Can Morizane's ARPKD organoid-on-chip be combined with ETV2-vascularization to create the first vascularized ARPKD kidney organoid?
Q4. ANSWERED — Is the Morizane ARPKD organoid-on-chip platform moving toward clinical translation?
Dr. Ryuji Morizane (MGH/Harvard. Permission granted September 3, 2026):
Pursuing translational studies with the longer-term goal of moving toward clinical studies if results are sufficiently promising. Letter of support from our network is welcomed.
GENETICS & VARIANT ANALYSIS
Q1. Does c.10926G>A (Met3642Ile) disrupt an exonic splicing enhancer (ESE) motif, causing exon skipping and functional truncation?
Q2. Can urine-derived renal tubular epithelial cells provide RNA for non-invasive PKHD1 variant confirmation?
Q3. ANSWERED — Is Met3642Ile (c.10926G>A) reclassified from VUS toward pathogenic at any institution?
Prof. Peter C. Harris (Mayo Clinic, September 2026):
This variant has been observed in trans with a confirmed pathogenic PKHD1 variant in a Mayo family — relevant PM3 context. Under formal clinical ACMG guidelines it would still be scored as a VUS. This is a research evaluation only, not a clinical reclassification.
Q4. ANSWERED — Have CHF variants in genotype zones 1-3 been associated with distinct hepatic outcomes?
Zone 3 (AA 2625-4074) is associated with the poorest hepatic outcomes — our daughter's variants (His3124Tyr at AA 3124 and Met3642Ile at AA 3642) both map to Zone 3. (Lapunzina-Soler P et al. Genes 17(2):229, 2026) This explains the liver-predominant phenotype.

Section 4

Key Cross-Connections

Independent lines of evidence converging on the same targets — these are the highest-confidence therapeutic nodes.

CDK1 as convergence point

Two independent YAP overactivation pathways (FXR/YAP loss + Scribble/YAP mislocalization) both drive CDK1 expression. CDK1 is now confirmed as the top biliary proliferative driver in PKHD1-deficient liver. CDK1 inhibition (RO-3306) reduces both cysts AND fibrosis in vivo in the correct PKHD1 model.

iScience 2026 · Fabris L et al. J Hepatol 2022 · Djouder N et al. Nat Cell Biol 2021

SPP1/STAT3 — five independent confirmations

Human CHF scRNA-seq, PKHD1 hepatic organoids (Peltz), PCK rat transcriptomics, Pkhd1-KO mouse IHC/western, and AAV8 knockdown in ARPKD mice. Cholangiocyte-specific SPP1 knockdown reduces biliary fibrosis in PKHD1-deficient mice — the most validated therapeutic approach in ARPKD-CHF.

PMID: 42265280 — Liebau/Ziegler group. Commun Biol 2026. DOI: 10.1038/s42003-026-10476-6

SPTAN1/RAC1 — Morizane ↔ Waddell/Boulter connection

SPTAN1 reduced in FPC-deficient kidney cells → RAC1 hyperactivation (Morizane 2026). α-II spectrin also anchors integrin-basement membrane attachments. If SPTAN1 is reduced in FPC-deficient cholangiocytes, it could simultaneously drive RAC1 cystogenesis AND disrupt the biliary basement membrane. One molecule may connect both mechanisms.

Kuraoka S, Morizane R et al. Adv Sci 2026 · Waddell SH, Brennan E, Boulter L. Dis Model Mech 2026

Cebotaru AAV1-CFTR vs Trikafta

AAV1-Δ27-264-CFTR reduces liver cyst area and proliferation in ARPKD mice; AAV1 preferentially targets ARPKD cysts via α2,3-sialylated glycan receptors. Same mechanism confirmed in kidney. Gene therapy bypasses the potentiator problem entirely.

Ciobanu C, Outeda P, Guggino WB, Cebotaru L. Am J Physiol GI 2025 · Ciobanu C, Cebotaru L. Am J Physiol Cell Physiol 2025
