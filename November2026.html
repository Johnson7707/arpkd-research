Clarifications to Issue 3 (October 2026) CLARIFICATION · Oct 5, 2026

Base editing. Issue 3 described our daughter's variants as confirmed adenine base editor substrates. Both are transition mutations that adenine base editing can in principle correct, but guide design, editing window and PAM context have not been tested at either site, so "predicted" is the accurate word.

Gene insertion (HITI). Issue 3 presented the Ginn et al. approach as mutation-agnostic. The donor has to carry all coding sequence downstream of the insertion point. That is small for OTC but far beyond single-AAV capacity for PKHD1 (12.2 kb coding sequence), so the approach cannot be assumed to transfer. The authors also reported that many integration events were in more complex arrangements than predicted.

AAV safety. Issue 3 stated, without a citation, that liver-directed AAV has caused deaths in children with pre-existing cholangiopathy. The published record we have reviewed since reports fatal liver failure after high-dose AAV8 in X-linked myotubular myopathy (see the Kim et al. item above), and the 2026 meta-analysis above notes severe hepatobiliary complications in high-dose patients with pre-existing intrahepatic cholestasis. We are reading the primary reports and will restate the claim only with citations.

🧬 Molecular Biology & Biliary Cascade

🧬 MOLECULAR BIOLOGY & BILIARY CASCADE

Noted (Tier 3 lead): CFTR assembles with the Hippo proteins TAZ and YAP in ADPKD mouse kidney; the CFTR corrector VX-809 reverses the pattern — ADPKD data, no ARPKD or biliary data yet 📌 NOTED · Oct 1, 2026

Sharma A, Afshani M, Ciobanu C, Sharma T, Kress JT, Guggino WB, Cebotaru L. "The ion channel, CFTR, assembles with HIPPO pathway proteins TAZ and YAP in polycystic kidney disease." J Biol Chem 302(7):113152, July 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.jbc.2026.113152. Open access (CC BY-NC-ND). ✅ Full text read October 1, 2026.

In Pkd1 R3277C mice and Pkd1-null kidney cells, CFTR co-immunoprecipitates with TAZ and YAP. In cystic epithelium all three accumulate at the apical membrane and nuclear TAZ/YAP is higher; VX-809 (lumacaftor) moves them back toward the normal basolateral pattern and lowers nuclear TAZ/YAP. Why it is noted here: the same group's ARPKD cholangiocyte study (Sharma, Cebotaru et al., Cell Calcium, in press) showed CFTR correctors reducing cholangiocyte proliferation and cyst growth. Whether the CFTR–TAZ/YAP link exists in Pkhd1-deficient cholangiocytes has not been tested. ⚠️ Limits: ADPKD mouse model, kidney only, correlative binding and imaging data; the authors' causal wording goes further than the data.

🧪 Gene Therapy & Base Editing

🧪 GENE THERAPY & BASE EDITING

⭐⭐ Human-liver AAV capsid screening on machine perfusion: the standard clinical capsids reach hepatocytes well and cholangiocytes poorly — Nature Biotechnology (published January 2025; added to our tracking October 2, 2026) 📌 NEW · Oct 2, 2026

Kim JJ, Kurial SNT, et al. (senior author H. Willenbring, UCSF). "AAV capsid prioritization in normal and steatotic human livers maintained by machine perfusion." Nat Biotechnol 43(12):1966–1978, 2025. DOI: 10.1038/s41587-024-02523-6. Open access (CC BY). ✅ Full text read October 2, 2026. Tier 2.

Method: Barcoded AAV vectors were infused through the portal vein of discarded adult human livers kept alive on normothermic machine perfusion, and the transduced cells were profiled by single-cell RNA sequencing. AAV8 was studied in two normal livers; AAV8, AAV5, AAV-LK03, AAV6 and AAV-NP59 were compared in four livers (two normal, two steatotic).

Findings: All five capsids transduced mainly hepatocytes, and cholangiocytes showed low transduction. AAV-LK03 was the most hepatocyte-specific. AAV8 also reached Kupffer cells (up to 8.6%) and sinusoidal endothelial cells. In fatty livers the vector formed fewer concatemers, so strategies that depend on DNA recombination — dual-vector delivery of large genes and Cas9 with AAV repair templates — may work less well there.

Why it matters for ARPKD-CHF: this is the first single-cell evidence in intact human liver that the standard clinical capsids reach cholangiocytes poorly. Biliary delivery therefore needs new tools: capsids engineered or screened for cholangiocytes, or a different route. The authors themselves propose using this platform to screen capsid libraries for delivery to cholangiocytes in inherited biliary disease. The paper also notes fatal liver failure after high-dose AAV8 in X-linked myotubular myopathy.

⚠️ Limits: discarded adult livers, two to four per comparison, portal-vein delivery only. No cholangiopathic liver was studied and retrograde delivery through the bile duct was not tested. Low cholangiocyte transduction in normal human liver does not show how cyst-lining biliary epithelium would behave.

Noted (Tier 3 context): meta-analysis of immunosuppression in clinical AAV gene therapy — 22 liver-directed trials show frequent antibody responses and liver enzyme rises despite steroids; no regimen shown superior 📌 NOTED · Oct 5, 2026

Wang Z, Wu N, Fu D, Zhao Q, Wang H. "A systematic review and meta-analysis of immunosuppressive regimens and immune-related outcomes in adeno-associated virus-based gene therapy." Sci Rep 2026 (Article in Press; published online October 1, 2026). DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-71872-w. Open access (CC BY 4.0). ✅ Full text read October 5, 2026.

73 studies and 1,773 participants. The 22 liver-directed studies (455 participants, all intravenous, mostly hemophilia) pooled to a 75.8% humoral immune response, 49.5% elevated ALT and 26.0% cellular response. Corticosteroids were used in 71 of the 73 studies, and the authors found no regimen shown to be superior; certainty was very low for most outcomes and most studies were single-arm. Why it is noted here: a benchmark for what systemic AAV does even in adults with normal bile ducts. ⚠️ Limits: no biliary disease, no children's liver disease and no gene editing were examined, so applicability to ARPKD-CHF is untested.

🏥 Clinical & Biomarkers

🏥 CLINICAL & BIOMARKERS

⭐⭐ PKHD1 missense variant at last exon nucleotide (-1G position) causes complete exon skipping in Caroli disease — UREC urine RNA testing method validated for PKHD1 variant reclassification — Gene (August 4, 2026 online / November 20, 2026 print) 📌 NEW · Oct 1, 2026

Huang H, Cheng C, Chen P, Yang F, Pan S, Xie F, Zhao S, Chen X (corresponding). "A novel PKHD1 missense variant disrupting splicing in a fetus with Caroli disease." Gene 1011:150340, November 20, 2026. Published online August 4, 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.gene.2026.150340. PMID: 42551719. ⚠️ NOT open access — Elsevier all rights reserved. ✅ Abstract and highlights confirmed October 1, 2026. Case Report.

Case: Fetus at 23 weeks gestation. Hepatic cysts on ultrasound, "central dot" sign on MRI (Caroli disease), ARPKD features, oligohydramnios. Exome sequencing: paternal nonsense c.5323C>T (Arg1775*); two maternal missense variants c.6682G>C (Glu2228Gln) and c.8012G>T (Arg2671Leu).

Key finding — c.6682G>C is the last nucleotide of exon 40 (-1G position): Minigene assay confirmed complete exon 40 skipping → in-frame deletion of 64 amino acids (Gly2164_Arg2227del) within a β-barrel domain of fibrocystin. AlphaFold3 predicts this deletion compromises β-barrel structural stability. The variant was initially annotated as missense (Glu2228Gln) but its primary effect is splice site disruption — not amino acid substitution.

Four highlights with direct ARPKD-CHF implications:

(1) Exonic last-nucleotide (-1G) variants cause severe splice disruption and exon skipping. This validates a variant class that is likely underdiagnosed across PKHD1 variant databases — many variants annotated as missense at exon-intron boundaries may be primarily splice disruptors.

(2) Systematic retrospective re-evaluation of unclassified PKHD1 VUS using splice-aware tools is needed. Any PKHD1 VUS with "predicted splice impact" annotation should be re-evaluated with SpliceAI and, where possible, functional RNA analysis.

(3) Endogenous RNA splicing in patient-derived primary cells (URECs/organoids) reveals tissue-specific penetrance. URECs (urine-derived renal epithelial cells) can be collected non-invasively from a urine sample and used to test whether a specific PKHD1 variant causes exon skipping in the patient's own cells. Tissue-specific penetrance means splicing may differ between kidney and liver cells — directly relevant to liver-dominant ARPKD-CHF.

(4) Structural and transcriptomic integration refines genotype-phenotype spectrum in ARPKD/Caroli disease.

⚠️ What this paper does NOT establish for our daughter's variant c.10926G>A (Met3642Ile): We confirmed October 1, 2026 that c.10926G>A is at genomic position chr6:51,659,200 (hg38) — confirmed from ClinVar primary database. This position is 268 bp interior to the exon that also contains c.10658 (Ile3553Thr, chr6:51,659,468). Because the genomic distance (268 bp) equals the CDS distance exactly, there are no introns between the two variants — they are in the same exon. Therefore c.10926G>A is NOT a -1G last-nucleotide splice variant of the class studied in Huang et al. It is located within the interior of its exon, not at the splice donor boundary. Its splice disruption (if real) would operate through ESE motif disruption — a different and less predictable mechanism. ClinVar: Conflicting classifications — all three reporting laboratories classify as VUS (uncertain significance). No published functional RNA data for c.10926G>A exists.

What the Huang paper still opens for c.10926G>A: Highlight 3 — UREC RNA testing — applies regardless of mechanism class. A urine sample from our daughter could directly test whether c.10926G>A causes exon skipping in her own cells, via endogenous RNA analysis without biopsy, iPSC reprogramming, or organoid culture. This is the most direct available experiment. Highlight 2 — systematic VUS re-evaluation — also applies: c.10926G>A has computational splice prediction and should be formally evaluated with SpliceAI (chr6:51,659,200 G>A lookup at spliceailookup.broadinstitute.org).

Companion paper: PMID 42697289 ("Structural and diagnostic implications of exonic splice-disrupting variants in PKHD1-related Caroli disease") refers to the Huang et al. case report — likely a review or commentary. Not yet indexed. New contact: Xinlin Chen (corresponding author of Huang et al.) — email not yet identified. Priority contact for UREC testing methodology.

🔬 Organoids & Disease Models

🔬 ORGANOIDS & DISEASE MODELS

Noted (regulatory context, indirect relevance): FDA replaces "animal tests" with "nonclinical tests" in its drug and biologic regulations — patient-derived models may count for more 📌 NOTED · Oct 8, 2026

On September 21, 2026 the FDA announced a direct final rule that replaces "animal tests/studies" with "nonclinical tests/studies" in its drug and biologic regulations. The FDA states that it adds no new requirements and sets no acceptance thresholds; a trade summary gives an effective date of February 2027, and the FDA also released a database of 25 retrospective examples of new approach methodologies. Source: the FDA announcement as excerpted in search results and a PharmTech summary (Oct 6, 2026); the primary page was not opened. Why it is noted here: it may make patient-derived organoid and cell models more usable as nonclinical evidence for rare-disease therapies — our inference; the announcement says nothing about ARPKD or gene therapy. ⚠️ Limits: a wording change, not an approval pathway; animal studies are not banned.

Study 307 (NCT04782258; ages 28 days to under 18 years; open-label; sponsor Otsuka) is listed as recruiting, with primary completion estimated July 2028 and the registry record last updated August 7, 2026. Study 204 (NCT04786574; infants 28 days to under 12 weeks) is listed as withdrawn — stopped before enrolling any participants (registry status verified December 2025). A 2023 design paper noted that the effect of tolvaptan on liver function in the ARPKD population is unknown (BMC Nephrol 2023, DOI: 10.1186/s12882-023-03072-x). Sources: ClinicalTrials.gov registry records and the sponsor's trial listing.

💰 FUNDING LANDSCAPE

Noted: PKD Foundation 2026 grants — two ARPKD-specific awards, both kidney-directed; none described as targeting ARPKD liver or biliary disease 📌 NOTED · Oct 1, 2026

The PKD Foundation announced 12 grants and one fellowship for 2026 (about September 24), as reported by BioQuick News. ARPKD-specific: Zhongwei Li (USC), in vitro and in vivo organoid platforms for ARPKD modeling; Ryan Williams (Stony Brook), targeted gene therapy strategies for ARPKD using kidney-directed nanocarriers. The other awards are ADPKD-focused, including CRISPR editing of PKD2 (Eun Ji Chung, USC) and base editing for polycystic kidney and liver disease (Matteus Krappitz, Young Investigator Award; the public summary centers on polycystins and does not name PKHD1). Based on the published descriptions, none targets ARPKD liver or biliary disease.

Noted: UK PKD Partnership (Kidney Research UK + PKD Charity) — 2026 awards include three UCL ARPKD-relevant projects and a PKD genetics fellowship 📌 NOTED · Oct 8, 2026

Funder announcements, read Oct 7–8, 2026; no results reported for any project. Long (UCL Great Ormond Street): £200,000 to test ARPKD therapies aimed at how cysts use energy through AMPK, plus a £126,000 PhD studentship on how a parent's diet before and during pregnancy affects PKD severity in offspring (animal work; no dietary advice follows from it). Chandler (UCL Great Ormond Street): £98,000 to test a virus-delivered therapy that restores the functional part of the faulty protein in human ARPKD kidney cells (kidney cells only). Pieri (UCL): £350,000 inaugural Tess Harris Fellowship to build a UK PKD genetic and clinical dataset (about 2,000 patients plus about 25,000 controls, linked to the national rare kidney disease registry) and look for markers of faster disease; whether ARPKD patients are included is not stated. Gittus (Sheffield): £19,000 on why polycystic liver disease varies in ADPKD, using a UK registry. ⚠️ Limits: news summaries only; several projects concern ADPKD.
