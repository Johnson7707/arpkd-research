Clarifications to Issue 3 (October 2026) CLARIFICATION · Oct 5, 2026

Base editing. Issue 3 described our daughter's variants as confirmed adenine base editor substrates. Both are transition mutations that adenine base editing can in principle correct, but guide design, editing window and PAM context have not been tested at either site, so "predicted" is the accurate word.

Gene insertion (HITI). Issue 3 presented the Ginn et al. approach as mutation-agnostic. The donor has to carry all coding sequence downstream of the insertion point. That is small for OTC but far beyond single-AAV capacity for PKHD1 (12.2 kb coding sequence), so the approach cannot be assumed to transfer. The authors also reported that many integration events were in more complex arrangements than predicted.

AAV safety. Issue 3 stated, without a citation, that liver-directed AAV has caused deaths in children with pre-existing cholangiopathy. The published record we have reviewed since reports fatal liver failure after high-dose AAV8 in X-linked myotubular myopathy (see the Kim et al. item above), A 2020 editorial on that trial (Wilson and Flotte) reports two deaths from liver toxicity at three times the earlier dose but does not mention pre-existing liver disease, and the 2026 meta-analysis's attribution of a cholestasis finding to it could not be confirmed. We are reading the primary trial report and will restate the claim only with citations.

Noted (Tier 3 lead): CFTR assembles with the Hippo proteins TAZ and YAP in ADPKD mouse kidney; the CFTR corrector VX-809 reverses the pattern — ADPKD data, no ARPKD or biliary data yet 📌 NOTED · Oct 1, 2026

Sharma A, Afshani M, Ciobanu C, Sharma T, Kress JT, Guggino WB, Cebotaru L. "The ion channel, CFTR, assembles with HIPPO pathway proteins TAZ and YAP in polycystic kidney disease." J Biol Chem 302(7):113152, July 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.jbc.2026.113152. Open access (CC BY-NC-ND). ✅ Full text read October 1, 2026.

In Pkd1 R3277C mice and Pkd1-null kidney cells, CFTR co-immunoprecipitates with TAZ and YAP. In cystic epithelium all three accumulate at the apical membrane and nuclear TAZ/YAP is higher; VX-809 (lumacaftor) moves them back toward the normal basolateral pattern and lowers nuclear TAZ/YAP. Why it is noted here: the same group's ARPKD cholangiocyte study (Sharma, Cebotaru et al., Cell Calcium, in press) showed CFTR correctors reducing cholangiocyte proliferation and cyst growth. Whether the CFTR–TAZ/YAP link exists in Pkhd1-deficient cholangiocytes has not been tested. ⚠️ Limits: ADPKD mouse model, kidney only, correlative binding and imaging data; the authors' causal wording goes further than the data.

Noted (Tier 3, resource): open single-cell atlas of the healthy pediatric liver — children's Kupffer-like cells are more immune-active than adults'; cholangiocyte results not reported 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Edgar RD, Nakib D, Camat D, et al. Hepatol Commun 9(11):e0813, 2025. DOI: 10.1097/HC9.0000000000000813 (open access; full text read). 42,660 cells from 9 healthy pediatric donors (ages 1–19) versus 26,372 adult cells, plus intestinal-failure liver disease biopsies. Pediatric Kupffer-like cells express more CCL3, CCL4 and IL1B than adult ones (IL-1β secretion confirmed). Used as a comparator, the atlas also showed higher MHC I/II signaling in cholangiocytes from biliary atresia and choledochal cysts than in healthy pediatric cells, and one spatial sample captured a bile duct hamartoma (data to be released). Why it is noted: adult-derived atlases lack a pediatric baseline. ⚠️ Limits: healthy donors only; cholangiocyte numbers and PKHD1 detection are not reported. Data are open (CELLxGENE, Human Cell Atlas portal).

Noted (Tier 3, mouse; full text read, journal pre-proof): in a Pkhd1-dependent mouse model of immune-driven cholangitis, a T cell–cholangiocyte signal (CD226–NECTIN2) was found by spatial and single-cell mapping 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Jördens MS, Nordhus KS, Øgaard J, et al., Melum E, Chung BK, J Hepatol 2026 (accepted Sept 16; DOI 10.1016/j.jhep.2026.09.021). NOD.c3c4 mice develop immune cells around bile ducts and then biliary cysts with limited fibrosis; T cells are required, and the authors state that a specific Pkhd1 mutation is necessary for the phenotype (Huang et al., J Immunol 2018, abstract read: a long terminal repeat inserted in intron 35 produces a truncated Pkhd1 transcript; disease needs the NOD genetic background, since the same mutation on C57BL/6 gave no clinical biliary disease, and needs the mutant Pkhd1 in both cholangiocytes and bone marrow). Spatial transcriptomics and single-cell sequencing placed immune cells almost entirely around bile ducts and pointed to CD226 on T cells and NECTIN2 on cholangiocytes, a pair whose gene is a known risk locus for primary sclerosing and biliary cholangitis. Lowering NECTIN2 on cultured cholangiocytes reduced T-cell activation, and an anti-CD226 antibody given for 5 weeks shifted liver T cells toward a less activated state without changing their number; in 10 human PSC livers, CD226+ T cells around NECTIN2+ cholangiocytes were more frequent than in 4 cirrhotic controls. Why it is noted: a Pkhd1-linked biliary cystic model in which immune activity is druggable, and an open map of T-cell interactions with cholangiocytes (Open Question 10). ⚠️ Limits: male mice and groups as small as n=2; the antibody study measured immune phenotype only, at 10 weeks, with no cyst, enzyme or fibrosis endpoints; autoimmune cholangitis on a NOD background is not ARPKD; human data are in PSC.

Noted (Tier 2, peer reviewed; read through a news summary only): a 649,295-cell human liver atlas splits scar-associated macrophages in two — OLR1+ "SAMac2" cells expand in every chronic liver disease and predict worse outcomes 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Papachristoforou E, Kong K, Colella F, et al., Fallowfield JA, Ramachandran P, Nature Genetics 58(10):2550–2565, 2026 (DOI 10.1038/s41588-026-02774-w, open access; University of Edinburgh). The atlas merges seven studies into 649,295 cells from 77 donors (42 healthy, 35 with chronic liver disease). Two scar-associated macrophage subsets, both TREM2+, differ sharply: SAMac1 carries reparative genes (GPNMB, MMP9, IL10, GDF15) and expands most in primary biliary cholangitis and primary sclerosing cholangitis; SAMac2 carries pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic genes (IL1B, TNF, NLRP3, PDGFB, OLR1) and expands in every cause. Spatial profiling put OLR1+ macrophages in fibrotic niches; in 632 staged fatty liver biopsies, high OLR1 or SAMac2 abundance was linked to higher mortality and faster decompensation; the same two subsets appeared in three mouse fibrosis models, including bile duct ligation, and arose from circulating monocytes. Knocking down OLR1 or using the clinical-stage antibody MEDI6570 lowered collagen expression in stellate-cell and spheroid models. Why it is noted: it adds a druggable macrophage target to the framework's macrophage thread, where fibrocystin-defective bile duct cells are known to recruit macrophages that promote portal fibrosis. ⚠️ Limits: adult disease and association-level human data; no ARPKD or congenital hepatic fibrosis samples; whether these cells exist in children with CHF is unknown (Open Question 8); we read a news summary, not the paper.

🧪 Gene Therapy & Base Editing

🧪 GENE THERAPY & BASE EDITING

⭐⭐ Human-liver AAV capsid screening on machine perfusion: the standard clinical capsids reach hepatocytes well and cholangiocytes poorly — Nature Biotechnology (published January 2025; added to our tracking October 2, 2026) 📌 NEW · Oct 2, 2026

Kim JJ, Kurial SNT, et al. (senior author H. Willenbring, UCSF). "AAV capsid prioritization in normal and steatotic human livers maintained by machine perfusion." Nat Biotechnol 43(12):1966–1978, 2025. DOI: 10.1038/s41587-024-02523-6. Open access (CC BY). ✅ Full text read October 2, 2026. Tier 2.

Method: Barcoded AAV vectors were infused through the portal vein of discarded adult human livers kept alive on normothermic machine perfusion, and the transduced cells were profiled by single-cell RNA sequencing. AAV8 was studied in two normal livers; AAV8, AAV5, AAV-LK03, AAV6 and AAV-NP59 were compared in four livers (two normal, two steatotic).

Findings: All five capsids transduced mainly hepatocytes, and cholangiocytes showed low transduction. AAV-LK03 was the most hepatocyte-specific. AAV8 also reached Kupffer cells (up to 8.6%) and sinusoidal endothelial cells. In fatty livers the vector formed fewer concatemers, so strategies that depend on DNA recombination — dual-vector delivery of large genes and Cas9 with AAV repair templates — may work less well there.

Why it matters for ARPKD-CHF: this is the first single-cell evidence in intact human liver that the standard clinical capsids reach cholangiocytes poorly. Biliary delivery therefore needs new tools: capsids engineered or screened for cholangiocytes, or a different route. The authors themselves propose using this platform to screen capsid libraries for delivery to cholangiocytes in inherited biliary disease. The paper also notes fatal liver failure after high-dose AAV8 in X-linked myotubular myopathy.

⚠️ Limits: discarded adult livers, two to four per comparison, portal-vein delivery only. No cholangiopathic liver was studied and retrograde delivery through the bile duct was not tested. Low cholangiocyte transduction in normal human liver does not show how cyst-lining biliary epithelium would behave.

Noted (Tier 3 context): meta-analysis of immunosuppression in clinical AAV gene therapy — 22 liver-directed trials show frequent antibody responses and liver enzyme rises despite steroids; no regimen shown superior 📌 NOTED · Oct 5, 2026

Wang Z, Wu N, Fu D, Zhao Q, Wang H. "A systematic review and meta-analysis of immunosuppressive regimens and immune-related outcomes in adeno-associated virus-based gene therapy." Sci Rep 2026 (Article in Press; published online October 1, 2026). DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-71872-w. Open access (CC BY 4.0). ✅ Full text read October 5, 2026.

73 studies and 1,773 participants. The 22 liver-directed studies (455 participants, all intravenous, mostly hemophilia) pooled to a 75.8% humoral immune response, 49.5% elevated ALT and 26.0% cellular response. Corticosteroids were used in 71 of the 73 studies, and the authors found no regimen shown to be superior; certainty was very low for most outcomes and most studies were single-arm. Why it is noted here: a benchmark for what systemic AAV does even in adults with normal bile ducts. ⚠️ Limits: no biliary disease, no children's liver disease and no gene editing were examined, so applicability to ARPKD-CHF is untested.

Noted (Tier 5, context): PKD Foundation research webinar — PKD1 is too large for AAV, so the field is testing protein fragments and base editors 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Webinar transcript with Dr. Michael Caplan (Yale) and Dr. Xiaogang Li (Mayo); no peer-reviewed source. Published or stated points only: the PKD1 coding sequence is about 13 kb against an AAV capacity near 4.5 kb. Caplan's lab expresses only the 200-amino-acid polycystin-1 C-terminal tail (about 600 bp, fits in AAV); the benefit was seen only in mice that carry the mitochondrial enzyme NNT. Li reported kidney base-editing efficiency of about 10–15% with a universal promoter and 20–25% with a kidney-specific one in mice, durable for 6 months, with under 1% bystander editing. Both stressed that all results are in mice and that early treatment matters. Why it is noted: a precedent for fibrocystin-fragment delivery and a benchmark for editing efficiency. Unpublished results mentioned in the webinar are intentionally not recorded here.

Noted (Tier 3, 2020 editorial; added to our tracking this month): two children died of liver toxicity after high-dose AAV for X-linked myotubular myopathy — the dose was three times the one that had worked 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Wilson JM and Flotte TR, Hum Gene Ther 31(13–14):695–696, 2020 (DOI 10.1089/hum.2020.182; text read, matched by content). Six children given 1 × 1014 genome copies/kg did well; all three given 3 × 1014 developed severe liver toxicity and two died, and development was put on hold. The authors place this beside milder platelet, liver and complement-related kidney effects in Duchenne trials at similar doses, and severe liver and clotting toxicity in non-human primates at 2 × 1014. Proposed explanations include antibody-vector complexes that activate complement; they list age, weight-based dosing, even low-titer pre-formed antibodies, genetic predisposition, capsid, promoter and purification as variables to study. Why it is noted: a published, citable account of what high systemic AAV doses can do, relevant to any AAV plan for children whose platelets and kidneys are already under strain. ⚠️ Limits: an editorial written early; it does not mention pre-existing liver or bile duct disease, so it does not settle the cholangiopathy question.

Noted (Tier 4, headline-level, abstract not read): bispecific antibodies that engage the polymeric immunoglobulin receptor (pIgR) enrich in mucosal tissue in monkeys — its introduction names ARPKD among epithelial diseases hard to reach with circulating antibodies 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Marks K, Shah N, Pham L, Chen J, White I, Burke K, et al., mAbs 2026 (PMID 42829333; online Oct 3, 2026; seen only as a search snippet, so the data and the design are not known to us). pIgR carries polymeric IgA and IgM across epithelial cells from the blood side to the lumen; ordinary IgG has no such route, and earlier work attached pIgR-binding fragments to antibodies so that they cross epithelia (mAbs 2020 and a 2026 Front Immunol report, both in lung and gut models). The snippet's introduction says that in polycystic kidney disease, including ADPKD and ARPKD, the overgrown tubule epithelium is inaccessible to circulating therapeutic antibodies. Why it is noted: it bears on Open Question 4 (how to reach epithelial cells). From general knowledge, cholangiocytes also express pIgR and move IgA into bile, so an antibody shuttle could in principle reach the bile duct lumen; this is our inference and nothing here tests it. Transport runs toward the lumen, so cargo would reach cyst or duct fluid and the cilium, not the inside of the cell, and it would need a target accessible from the lumen. ⚠️ Limits: abstract-level; the paper's tissue data (kidney, liver) are unknown to us.

🏥 Clinical & Biomarkers

🏥 CLINICAL & BIOMARKERS

⭐⭐ PKHD1 missense variant at last exon nucleotide (-1G position) causes complete exon skipping in Caroli disease — UREC urine RNA testing method validated for PKHD1 variant reclassification — Gene (August 4, 2026 online / November 20, 2026 print) 📌 NEW · Oct 1, 2026

Huang H, Cheng C, Chen P, Yang F, Pan S, Xie F, Zhao S, Chen X (corresponding). "A novel PKHD1 missense variant disrupting splicing in a fetus with Caroli disease." Gene 1011:150340, November 20, 2026. Published online August 4, 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.gene.2026.150340. PMID: 42551719. ⚠️ NOT open access — Elsevier all rights reserved. ✅ Abstract and highlights confirmed October 1, 2026. Case Report.

Case: Fetus at 23 weeks gestation. Hepatic cysts on ultrasound, "central dot" sign on MRI (Caroli disease), ARPKD features, oligohydramnios. Exome sequencing: paternal nonsense c.5323C>T (Arg1775*); two maternal missense variants c.6682G>C (Glu2228Gln) and c.8012G>T (Arg2671Leu).

Key finding — c.6682G>C is the last nucleotide of exon 40 (-1G position): Minigene assay confirmed complete exon 40 skipping → in-frame deletion of 64 amino acids (Gly2164_Arg2227del) within a β-barrel domain of fibrocystin. AlphaFold3 predicts this deletion compromises β-barrel structural stability. The variant was initially annotated as missense (Glu2228Gln) but its primary effect is splice site disruption — not amino acid substitution.

Four highlights with direct ARPKD-CHF implications:

(1) Exonic last-nucleotide (-1G) variants cause severe splice disruption and exon skipping. This validates a variant class that is likely underdiagnosed across PKHD1 variant databases — many variants annotated as missense at exon-intron boundaries may be primarily splice disruptors.

(2) Systematic retrospective re-evaluation of unclassified PKHD1 VUS using splice-aware tools is needed. Any PKHD1 VUS with "predicted splice impact" annotation should be re-evaluated with SpliceAI and, where possible, functional RNA analysis.

(3) Endogenous RNA splicing in patient-derived primary cells (URECs/organoids) reveals tissue-specific penetrance. URECs (urine-derived renal epithelial cells) can be collected non-invasively from a urine sample and used to test whether a specific PKHD1 variant causes exon skipping in the patient's own cells. Tissue-specific penetrance means splicing may differ between kidney and liver cells — directly relevant to liver-dominant ARPKD-CHF.

(4) Structural and transcriptomic integration refines genotype-phenotype spectrum in ARPKD/Caroli disease.

⚠️ What this paper does NOT establish for our daughter's variant c.10926G>A (Met3642Ile): We confirmed October 1, 2026 that c.10926G>A is at genomic position chr6:51,659,200 (hg38) — confirmed from ClinVar primary database. This position is 268 bp interior to the exon that also contains c.10658 (Ile3553Thr, chr6:51,659,468). Because the genomic distance (268 bp) equals the CDS distance exactly, there are no introns between the two variants — they are in the same exon. Therefore c.10926G>A is NOT a -1G last-nucleotide splice variant of the class studied in Huang et al. It is located within the interior of its exon, not at the splice donor boundary. Its splice disruption (if real) would operate through ESE motif disruption — a different and less predictable mechanism. ClinVar: Conflicting classifications — all three reporting laboratories classify as VUS (uncertain significance). No published functional RNA data for c.10926G>A exists.

What the Huang paper still opens for c.10926G>A: Highlight 3 — UREC RNA testing — applies regardless of mechanism class. A urine sample from our daughter could directly test whether c.10926G>A causes exon skipping in her own cells, via endogenous RNA analysis without biopsy, iPSC reprogramming, or organoid culture. This is the most direct available experiment. Highlight 2 — systematic VUS re-evaluation — also applies: c.10926G>A was run through SpliceAI and Pangolin on Oct 8, 2026 (hg38 chr6:51,659,200 C>T): every delta score was 0.05 or lower, so these tools predict no splice effect, and they are known to miss enhancer-mediated skipping, so only an RNA or minigene test can settle it.

Companion paper: PMID 42697289 ("Structural and diagnostic implications of exonic splice-disrupting variants in PKHD1-related Caroli disease") refers to the Huang et al. case report — likely a review or commentary. Not yet indexed. New contact: Xinlin Chen (corresponding author of Huang et al.) — email not yet identified. Priority contact for UREC testing methodology.

Noted (Tier 4, conference abstract — not peer reviewed): two-year follow-up of liver stiffness by transient elastography in children with cystic kidney disease — 7 of 9 ARPKD children had raised stiffness and 3 progressed 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Jacobs A, Kapil S, Morlan W, Gupta S, Molleston J (Indiana University / Riley Hospital for Children). Abstract 80, JPGN Reports 7(S2):S55, 2026 (supplement DOI 10.1002/jpr3.70233). 28 children with cystic kidney disease (9 ARPKD); FibroScan at enrollment and 2 years. Seven of nine ARPKD children had stiffness more than 2 SD above the mean (7.3 to 32.7 kPa); three progressed (32.7→59.8, 18.3→46.8, 13.1→17.8 kPa), two with worsening platelet counts. One nephronophthisis child with very high stiffness died of a ruptured splenic artery pseudoaneurysm. ⚠️ Limits: nine ARPKD patients at one center identified by billing codes; the reference for "more than 2 SD above the mean" is not stated; do not compare these kPa values with MR elastography values. No full paper yet.

Noted (Tier 2, abstract only): genetics of isolated ductal plate malformation in 55 adults — congenital hepatic fibrosis cases carried only PKHD1 variants, about half missense 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Bhuva M, Thompson E, Watson CM, Sava F, Griffiths WJH. Dig Dis Sci 71:5137–5151, 2026. DOI: 10.1007/s10620-026-10072-y (Brief Report; online June 28, 2026; Cambridge Liver Unit with Leeds and Sheffield genetics services). DOI, authors and dates confirmed on the journal page; the full text is paywalled, so the abstract was read and the full text was not. The authors' contributions note a long-read variant analysis. A research-profile excerpt for Dr. Griffiths (ResearchGate; secondary source) describes long-read nanopore sequencing at the PKHD1 locus, in which heterozygous variants let reads be assigned to one of two haplotypes before phase blocks were built; this is the kind of phasing the 2026 Genes editorial calls essential for PKHD1 diagnosis. PubMed record: PMID 42365546. 54 of 55 patients without polycystic kidney disease were sequenced: 40 polycystic liver disease, 9 congenital hepatic fibrosis, 1 Caroli, 5 multiple biliary hamartomas. Pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants were found in 31. Polycystic liver disease was mainly SEC63, PRKCSH and GANAB with a minor PKHD1 and ALG8 contribution; congenital hepatic fibrosis came solely from PKHD1 variants. Five novel variants were reported (four SEC63, one PRKCSH), none in PKHD1. ⚠️ Limits: adults (mean age 47, 78% female), single center, retrospective, kidney disease excluded by design.

Noted (Tier 3, abstracts read): minigene studies keep finding PKHD1 variants that change splicing — including missense variants — in Caroli disease and ARPKD fetuses 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Huang et al., Front Genet 2025 (DOI 10.3389/fgene.2025.1651306, open access): two Chinese fetuses with Caroli disease or syndrome (full text read); in one, the intronic variant c.3364+3A>T caused exon 29 skipping with a frameshift in minigene assays (HEK293T and HepG2 cells), paired with the missense c.7912T>A (p.Tyr2638Asn). Three of the four missense variants in prenatally diagnosed Caroli-type cases fall in the C-terminal region AA 2625–4074, which an earlier cohort linked to poorer liver outcomes (very small numbers). Miao et al., Front Genet 2023 (DOI 10.3389/fgene.2023.1207772): a novel intronic variant (c.2408-13C>G, 13 bases before exon 24) in an ARPKD fetus retained 12 bp of intron 23 in minigene assays, predicting an in-frame four-amino-acid insertion whose effect on the protein the authors say is only hypothesized; the fetus also carried a known frameshift variant, and autopsy at 30 weeks showed cystic kidneys with no definite liver abnormality (full text read, open access). Both add to the same group's 2026 report of exon 40 skipping from a last-nucleotide missense variant (above). Why it is noted: missense and near-splice variants are not always what they look like, and the minigene method that tests them is established; a missense variant of uncertain significance inside an exon could be tested the same way. ⚠️ Limits: fetal case reports; the Huang 2025 and Miao 2023 full texts were read, the others only as abstracts; minigenes were run in non-biliary cell lines; no test of any specific uncertain variant here.

Noted (Tier 2, published 2010; added to our tracking this month): in 74 fetuses and early-demise neonates with ARPKD, two truncating PKHD1 variants predicted kidney severity but not liver fibrosis 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Denamur E, Delezoide A-L, Alberti C, et al., Kidney Int 77:350–358, 2010 (DOI 10.1038/ki.2009.440; full text read). French cohort of 74 histologically confirmed cases (54 terminated fetuses and 20 neonatal deaths). Two truncating variants were more frequent in earlier terminations (53% at or before 30 weeks vs 16% after) and went with more extensive collecting-duct dilatation in the kidney cortex. Portal fibrosis was present in all 61 livers and rose with gestational age (grade 3 in 4% at or before 30 weeks vs 40% after), but did not differ significantly by genotype class. Sixteen neonates without two truncating variants still died, carrying ten different missense variants. The authors raise modifier genes (HNF1B, KIF12, and interactions with PKD1 and PKD2 seen in animal models) as a possible explanation for variable severity. Why it is noted: liver fibrosis is already substantial before birth in severe cases, which supports an early treatment window, and truncating-versus-not does not predict liver severity. Several missense variants in the C-terminal region (AA 2625–4074) appear among these perinatal deaths, so that region is not simply liver-predominant. ⚠️ Limits: a selected severe perinatal cohort, histology only, no long-term liver outcomes, a binary genotype classification, and 2010 data.

Noted (Tier 3, published 2022; added to our tracking this month): three children with Caroli syndrome carried missense PKHD1 variants and developed portal hypertension early — one needed a liver transplant 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Giacobbe C, Di Dato F, Palma D, et al., Mol Genet Genomic Med 10:e1998, 2022 (DOI 10.1002/mgg3.1998, open access; full text read). Two Moroccan siblings carried the same two missense variants (p.Asn901Thr and p.Arg1624Trp); the boy had portal hypertension, varices and stage 3 kidney disease by 9.7 years, while his younger sister at 5 years had liver enlargement and normal kidney function. An Italian girl with an uncertain complex allele of three missense variants plus a likely pathogenic missense variant had portal hypertension at 13 and a liver transplant at 14; after transplant her kidney function dipped on tacrolimus and recovered when she was switched to sirolimus (a single observation). Platelet counts fell in all three children. Why it is noted: missense-only genotypes can give an early and severe liver course, and a complex allele of uncertain variants illustrates why functional testing of unclassified variants matters. ⚠️ Limits: three patients, a targeted gene panel and no functional work; the sirolimus observation is anecdotal and not advice.

Noted (Tier 3, published 2019; added to our tracking this month): fetal MRI showed biliary dilatation with the "central dot sign" in ARPKD with Caroli syndrome — the child needed kidney and liver transplants by 16 months 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Rivas A, Epelman M, Danzer E, et al., Radiol Case Rep 14:265–268, 2019 (DOI 10.1016/j.radcr.2018.11.006, open access; full text read). A male fetus with enlarged echogenic kidneys, an enlarged cystic liver and oligohydramnios had MRI that showed dilated bile ducts and the central dot sign (a portal vein branch inside a dilated duct), which the authors say had not been reported on fetal MRI before; it is the fourth prenatally diagnosed case of this combination in the literature. After birth he needed a left nephrectomy at 3 months and kidney and liver transplantation at 16 months, and was doing well a year later. Genetic testing found a single missense variant, p.Cys2803Arg. Why it is noted: an imaging marker for biliary involvement before birth, and another missense variant in the C-terminal region (AA 2625–4074) in a child with severe early kidney disease. ⚠️ Limits: one patient, one variant detected, no functional work.

Noted (Tier 5, editorial; full text read): a rare-disease sequencing special issue says PKHD1 diagnoses depend on getting variant phase right, and lists a review of patient-specific antisense therapies 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Errichiello E and Dimartino P, "Editorial: Next-Generation Sequencing in Rare Genetic Diseases," Genes 17:1236, 2026 (DOI 10.3390/genes17101236; open access; published Oct 8, 2026). It summarizes an ARPKD cohort study by Lapunzina-Soler et al. as emphasizing accurate variant phasing and analysis pipelines that can reliably determine phase, noting that for a gene with extensive allelic heterogeneity such as PKHD1, accurate phasing is essential to a reliable molecular diagnosis. The issue also includes a review of patient-specific RNA therapeutics including antisense oligonucleotides, a case solved by combining cell-free DNA sequencing with long-read genome sequencing, and papers on uneven sequencing coverage, population-specific variant databases and blinded reanalysis of prenatal exomes. Why it is noted: it reinforces that families and clinicians should confirm that the two PKHD1 variants are on different chromosomes before interpreting them, and the antisense review is relevant to choosing between gene editing and RNA therapy for a given variant. ⚠️ Limits: an editorial with no new data; the underlying papers have not been read.

The paper notes 649 pathogenic PKHD1 variants in ClinVar (slightly different from 605-variant count in Zhao 2026 — different databases or dates). No functional assay for Y1607C.

⚠️ CLINICAL UPDATE: Odevixibat (Bylvay) BOLD Phase 3 trial failed in biliary atresia — July 24, 2026. Rationale in ARPKD-CHF remains biologically defensible but clinical advancement is now more difficult. ⚠️ FRAMEWORK UPDATE · Oct 15, 2026

Ipsen announced July 24, 2026 that the Phase 3 BOLD trial (NCT04336722) of odevixibat (Bylvay) in 254 biliary atresia patients post-Kasai hepatoportoenterostomy did not meet its primary endpoint of improved native liver survival vs placebo. Safety was consistent with established indications. An open-label extension (BOLD-EXT) is ongoing. BOLD was the first global Phase 3 trial ever conducted in biliary atresia. Ipsen press release, July 24, 2026.

Why this matters for ARPKD-CHF: The framework tracked BOLD because biliary atresia is a biliary cholestatic disease in the same mechanistic territory as ARPKD-CHF. The failure does not directly invalidate the ARPKD-CHF rationale — biliary atresia post-Kasai involves extrahepatic obstruction surgically addressed and then intrahepatic sequelae, mechanistically distinct from ARPKD-CHF's intrahepatic DPM from birth. The Mdr2-/- preclinical data and the five-node bile acid convergence argument remain intact. However, an FDA-approved drug failing Phase 3 in the most mechanistically adjacent biliary disease makes the compassionate use and investigator-initiated study case harder to make. The ARPKD-CHF rationale is now: biologically defensible; clinically more difficult to advance. Discuss with Liebau/Hartung whether this changes their position.

Network note: Saul J. Karpen (VCU, Division Chief Pediatric GI/Hepatology) was the key investigator quoted in the BOLD results coverage — confirming his direct involvement with odevixibat and biliary atresia clinical research, and reinforcing his relevance as a new network contact.

⭐ KIF12 confirmed as a human biliary disease gene (EBioMedicine 2026) — and it was already identified as an ARPKD modifier gene by Guay-Woodford's lab in 2005. The two findings together raise a direct question about phenotypic variability in PKHD1 compound heterozygotes. 📌 NEW · Oct 15, 2026

Two papers, two decades apart, now converge:

2005 (Guay-Woodford lab): Mrug M, Li R, Guay-Woodford LM et al. JASN 16:905-916, 2005. In 461 F2 cpk ARPKD mice, KIF12 was identified as a candidate modifier gene — expression-level variants and strain-specific sequences correlated with biliary and renal disease severity. Implication: PKHD1 patients who also carry KIF12 variants may have more severe biliary disease than PKHD1 genotype alone predicts.

2026 (Vilarinho lab, Yale): Seth A, Brancale J, Florentino RM et al., Vilarinho S (senior). "Liver disease reveals KIF12 as a critical regulator of mitochondria, lysosome and cilia localization in human cholangiocytes." EBioMedicine 132:106474, 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.ebiom.2026.106474. Same Vilarinho group that built the Yale Human Liver Single Cell Atlas (already Tier 1 in this framework). Using iPSC-derived cholangiocyte-like cells and 3D biliary organoids engineered with KIF12 p.Arg219* mutation: KIF12 selectively expressed in cholangiocytes (rank 38 in the Yale atlas, confirmed); KIF12 dysfunction causes abnormal perinuclear clustering of mitochondria and lysosomes AND mislocalization of primary cilia. 27 individuals with biallelic KIF12 mutations and high-GGT pediatric cholestatic liver disease now documented globally.

The parallel to ARPKD-CHF: FPC loss → shorter, functionally impaired cilia still present. KIF12 loss → cilia physically mislocated (perinuclear clustering). Different upstream cause, same downstream consequence: defective primary cilia sensing in cholangiocytes. Both diseases sit on the cholangiocyte ciliopathy spectrum. The specific question for Guay-Woodford: do PKHD1 compound heterozygotes in the HRFD registry who also carry KIF12 variants show more severe portal hypertension or earlier clinical presentation?

⚠️ Limits: KIF12 as a standalone disease affects only ~27 patients globally — extremely rare. The ARPKD modifier implication is from the cpk mouse QTL data (2005) combined with the 2026 mechanistic paper — not yet confirmed in human PKHD1/KIF12 double-variant cohorts. Tier 3 mechanistic inference.

⭐⭐ Two October 2026 papers bracket the ARPKD-CHF diagnosis from opposite ends of the clinical spectrum — and document the same diagnostic gap independently: PKHD1 VUSs are being overlooked on both cholestasis panels and cystic kidney panels 📌 NEW · Oct 15, 2026

Paper 1 — NOTCH2 VUS in pediatric cholestasis: Hoskins BJ, Pramparo T, Vandriel SM, Jarasvaraparn C, Wilsey MJ, Slowik V, Kunam L, Stoll JM, Quiros-Tejeira RE, Lam S, Karnsakul W. "NOTCH2 VUS Interpretation in Pediatric Cholestasis: A 365-Variant Landscape and Genotype–Phenotype Analysis of 7 Cases." Genes 2026, 17, 1259. DOI: 10.3390/genes17101259. Published October 11, 2026. Open access. ✅ Full text read October 15, 2026. Multicenter North American pediatric hepatology consortium (Riley Hospital IU, Johns Hopkins All Children's, Children's Mercy KC, Washington University St. Louis, U Nebraska, U Calgary, Johns Hopkins Baltimore). No ARPKD primary data. ⚠️ COI: authors Pramparo and Vandriel are Mirum Pharmaceuticals employees and shareholders; Hoskins and Karnsakul consult for Mirum. Maralixibat (highlighted therapeutic) is a Mirum product. Investigator-initiated support from Mirum, Travere Therapeutics, and Albireo/Ipsen. The COI is appropriately disclosed and the science stands independently of it; maralixibat is treated here as a single-patient commercial observation, not independent clinical evidence.

Key finding — PKHD1 VUS on cholestasis panel without ARPKD workup (Patient 2): Among 7 consortium cases with NOTCH2 VUSs, Patient 2 (p.Arg2298Gln, tail region, no ALGS-compatible features) carried co-occurring CFTR VUS and PKHD1 VUS identified on the multigene cholestasis panel — with no CHF+PKD diagnosis recorded. This is the cholestasis-panel version of the diagnostic failure mode: a PKHD1 VUS identified, no further PKHD1-directed clinical evaluation documented.

Key finding — CHF+PKD as explicit NOTCH2 VUS alternative (Patient 5): A child with a NOTCH2 VUS near EGF-like domain 13 and a co-occurring CFTR VUS had clinical findings of cholestasis, renal anomaly, and congenital hepatic fibrosis — diagnosed as "congenital hepatic fibrosis with polycystic kidney disease" as the alternative explanation. The NOTCH2 VUS was appropriately deprioritized. This child may have ARPKD-CHF. The co-occurring CFTR VUS in this context is directly relevant to the Cebotaru lab's finding that CFTR correctors reduce cyst growth in primary ARPKD cholangiocytes: if this patient has ARPKD-CHF, simultaneous CFTR corrector + IBAT inhibitor (odevixibat) evaluation would be mechanistically justified on two non-overlapping pathways.

Methodology value — VUS prioritization framework directly applicable to PKHD1 Met3642Ile: The paper's clinical–genomic prioritization framework integrates variant type, domain location, phenotype compatibility, and competing diagnoses — the same multi-factor approach needed for PKHD1 Met3642Ile (c.10926G>A, Zone 3, VUS under ACMG guidelines). The GALA study group's disease-specific ACMG framework for NOTCH2 (Vandriel et al. Liver Int 2025, DOI: 10.1111/liv.70251) is the methodological model for what an equivalent PKHD1-specific ACMG framework would require.

NOTCH2 two-mode biology — cascade clarification: NOTCH2 operates in two mechanistically distinct registers. In Alagille syndrome (ALGS2): heterozygous LOF → impaired bile duct specification during ductal plate remodeling (weeks 7–12) → bile duct paucity (too few ducts). In ARPKD-CHF cascade: Jagged1/Notch2 → CXCL10 + CXCL1 + CXCL12, post-developmentally, in an already-established biliary tree — driving macrophage recruitment, not morphogenesis. These operate at different developmental and disease timepoints, in different cell populations, through different downstream effectors. Direct Notch2 inhibition in the ARPKD-CHF setting risks worsening bile duct architecture in a biliary tree already damaged by DPM. Downstream blockade via CXCL10/CXCR3 (ACT-777991) is mechanistically safer. (Geisler et al. Hepatology 2008; Tchorz et al. Hepatology 2009; Kaffe/Strazzabosco Hepatology 2018)

Phenotype-first IBAT inhibitor principle — relevant to odevixibat in ARPKD-CHF: Patient 7 (p.Cys593Arg, EGF-like domain 15, bile acids >400 µmol/L, severe pruritus) received maralixibat (IBAT inhibitor, same class as odevixibat) based on phenotype before VUS reclassification — two years of sustained improvement. The ASSERT Phase 3 trial (Ovchinsky et al. Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol 2024, DOI: 10.1016/S2468-1253(24)00074-8) confirms odevixibat efficacy in ALGS-spectrum biliary structural disease. Both support the phenotype-first principle for IBAT inhibitor initiation in pediatric biliary cholestasis with severe pruritus and bile acid accumulation. Odevixibat is already Tier 2 in this framework (Mdr2-/- biliary fibrosis model confirmed; FDA-approved ≥3 months). ⚠️ Limits: maralixibat response is a single commercial observation from a Mirum-funded study; the ASSERT trial is ALGS (bile duct paucity), not ARPKD-CHF (DPM). Whether IBAT inhibition reduces bile acid-driven cascade activation in CHF+PKD anatomy (intrahepatic obstruction rather than transporter defect) has not been tested.

⚠️ Limits of Paper 1: Multicenter retrospective design; 7 consortium patients, small numbers; no functional variant validation; no ARPKD primary data; COI as noted above.

Noted (Tier 3, narrative review; full text read, 39 pages): a comprehensive 2026 pediatric PKD review documents that PKHD1-VUS carriers are currently assigned ADPKD by clinical default — and maps five additional mechanisms producing CHF+PKD without biallelic PKHD1 pathogenic variants 📌 NEW · Oct 15, 2026

Chirico V, Tripodi F, Cassaro F, Lacquaniti A, Arena S, Romeo C, Chimenz R. "Genetic Insights into Pediatric Polycystic Kidney Disease and Overlapping Ciliopathies: A Narrative Review." Genes 2026, 17, 1252. DOI: 10.3390/genes17101252. Published October 10, 2026. Open access. No conflicts of interest. No external funding. ✅ Full text read October 15, 2026. All authors from University of Messina Pediatric Nephrology (Italy).

Most important finding for ARPKD-CHF: The paper states in its Introduction that "for individuals presenting with an ADPKD-spectrum phenotype who either lack genetic testing, display no identifiable pathogenic variants, or carry variants of uncertain significance (VUS) in minor modifier genes (such as ALG6, ALG8, or PKHD1), the clinical designation of ADPKD remains standard." This is the documented clinical convention — not an error or edge case — that assigns ADPKD to PKHD1-VUS carriers. The consequence for ARPKD-CHF patients: liver disease surveillance, portal hypertension management, and biliary-directed therapeutic pathways are all absent from ADPKD clinical protocols. Any child with a PKHD1 VUS encountered on any diagnostic panel requires phenotype-anchored re-evaluation before the ADPKD default designation is accepted.

HNF1B → PKHD1 transcriptional regulation → CHF+PKD phenocopy: The paper states that HNF1B "regulates the expression of several PKD-related genes, including PKD2 and PKHD1." Pathogenic HNF1B variants therefore reduce PKHD1 transcription, producing partial fibrocystin deficiency in biliary cells and a CHF+PKD phenotype without PKHD1 variants. ADTKD-HNF1B is recognizable by co-occurring MODY5 diabetes, neonatal cholestasis, pancreatic hypoplasia, and cortical renal cysts. This is distinct from the existing framework HNF-1β cascade node (Hiesberger J Am Soc Nephrol 2010: ARPKD cystogenesis → HNF-1β loss → reduced PKHD1 feedback), which runs in the same direction but from a different starting cause. In CHF+PKD with negative PKHD1 sequencing, HNF1B sequencing and 17q12 chromosomal microarray should be the next step.

NEK8 biallelic → renal-hepatic-pancreatic dysplasia: The paper documents that biallelic NEK8 variants cause severe syndromic ciliopathy with renal, hepatic, and pancreatic dysplasia. Monoallelic NEK8 drives rapidly progressive VEO-ADPKD; biallelic NEK8 causes a CHF-equivalent hepatic malformation. NEK8 is a newly established ADPKD gene and is not on all current diagnostic panels. When CHF+PKD presents with pancreatic involvement, biallelic NEK8 should be considered. This is a new entry in the CHF+PKD differential list.

Burgmaier 2021 (Zone 3 PKHD1 genotype-phenotype correlations) independently cited: The paper's Table 1 cites Burgmaier et al. Kidney Int 2021, 100:650–659 — our framework's reference [84] establishing Zone 1/2/3 genotype-phenotype correlations for PKHD1. Independent citation confirms this paper's Zone 3 framework is reliable enough for a 2026 narrative review to cite without qualification. Our patient's two variants (His3124Tyr and Met3642Ile at AA 3124 and 3642) are confirmed Zone 3 variants under this framework.

G-quadruplex (G4) DNA instability in PKD1 — confirms PKHD1 base editing advantage: The paper documents that G4 structures drive somatic second-hit mutagenesis at the PKD1 locus (six pseudogenes, GC-rich). PKHD1 has no pseudogenes and no documented G4-prone somatic mechanism — independently confirming the framework's existing insight that PKHD1 base editing is technically simpler than PKD1 correction. (Parsons MM et al. Nat Commun 2025, 16:121, cited therein)

Prenatal CMD imaging criterion: Decreased or lost corticomedullary differentiation on fetal ultrasound is characteristic of classic ARPKD; increased CMD favors early-onset ADPKD. This criterion belongs in prenatal counseling for families with known PKHD1 genotype. Not previously in our framework.

Tolvaptan in pediatric ADPKD — no new ARPKD data: The paper presents the most current tolvaptan pediatric trial data; this is ADPKD-only. The framework's existing ARPKD note stands: Tolvaptan Study 204 was withdrawn before enrolling.

⚠️ Limits: 39-page narrative review with no original data; primary scope is ADPKD with ciliopathy overlap; PKHD1/ARPKD coverage is secondary; the CDG–FPC glycosylation phenocopy hypothesis and the NEK8 CHF connection are inferred from other sources cited in the review, not from original ARPKD data in this paper.

🔬 ORGANOIDS & DISEASE MODELS

Noted (Tier 3, review; full text read): a review of cholangiocyte plasticity and cell therapy outlines organoid transplantation into bile ducts, but has no ARPKD data 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Lou C, Lan T, Xu S, et al., Xu X, Gut 75:646–663, 2026 (DOI 10.1136/gutjnl-2025-334763). The review separates large cholangiocytes from small, more progenitor-like ones and describes how cholangiocytes can turn into hepatocytes when hepatocytes cannot regenerate. On cell therapy, gallbladder-derived human cholangiocyte organoids delivered into the bile ducts engrafted in 25–55% of intrahepatic ducts in immunodeficient mice and, in discarded human livers on machine perfusion, regenerated 40–85% of injected ducts; stem-cell-derived cholangiocytes, which can be made from skin, blood or urine, model polycystic liver disease and other cholangiopathies. The authors list immature cells, genetic instability, gene editing of the cell source, targeted delivery and immune rejection as the hurdles. ARPKD appears only through two cited papers (macrophage recruitment by fibrocystin-defective cells; primary cilia as a target in transplantation). Why it is noted: it sketches an ex vivo alternative to in vivo delivery: correct a patient's own cells, grow them as organoids and place them in the ducts (Open Question 11). ⚠️ Limits: general review; engraftment shown in healthy or acutely injured ducts, not cystic or fibrotic ones; no ARPKD experiments.

Noted (Tier 3, abstract level; ScienceDirect page not opened): CFTR corrector drugs reduced cyst growth in primary ARPKD bile duct cells and partly restored three proteins lost with fibrocystin 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Sharma A, Afshani M, Sharma T, Cebotaru L (Johns Hopkins), Cell Calcium 138:103195, Nov 2026 (DOI 10.1016/j.ceca.2026.103195; NIH-funded). In primary bile duct cells (cholangiocytes) from Pkhd1 del3-4 ARPKD mice, the cystic fibrosis drugs VX-809 (lumacaftor) and VX-445 (elexacaftor) reduced cyst growth, partly restored polycystin-1 and polycystin-2, and dampened the calcium and cAMP signalling abnormalities; fibrocystin-deficient cells had markedly lower polycystin-1, polycystin-2 and CFTR, and all three were partly restored. The CFTR blocker CFTRinh-172 did not give the same effect, which the group reads as correction, not inhibition, being what matters. This appears to be the first test of these two correctors in primary ARPKD cholangiocytes. Why it is noted: it is ARPKD-specific, liver-side, and uses drugs already approved for another disease; it also follows the same group's finding that CFTR binds the Hippo proteins TAZ and YAP in ADPKD kidney, which has not yet been tested in Pkhd1-deficient cells. ⚠️ Limits: abstract-level reading; cultured mouse cells, not people or whole animals; the abstract shows these proteins fall together, not that one causes the others; no data on liver fibrosis or portal hypertension, which CFTR correctors would not address by themselves.

Noted (Tier 4, adjacent): LoAc® small molecules that activate long-form PDE4 enzymes to lower cAMP — Phase 1 started in ADPKD; not yet tested in ARPKD or in liver 📌 NOTED · Oct 8, 2026

Omar F, Findlay JE, Carfray G, et al. "Small-molecule allosteric activators of PDE4 long form cyclic AMP phosphodiesterases." PNAS 116(27):13320–13329, 2019. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1822113116 (open access), plus a Mironid press release of Oct 1, 2026 and 2024 conference abstracts. The company's compounds bind the regulatory interface of long-form PDE4 enzymes and speed cAMP breakdown. ✅ Full text read Oct 8, 2026 (authors are mostly Mironid employees): the 2019 paper showed that the prototype activator raised long-form PDE4 activity by about 60%, lowered cAMP and CFTR-dependent signaling, and reduced cysts in dog kidney cells, a human ADPKD cell line and primary cells from an ADPKD patient's kidney cysts, while a PDE4 inhibitor made cysts larger; all data are kidney, ADPKD-type and in vitro. Conference abstracts report that an oral compound reduced cysts and MRI kidney volume in an ADPKD mouse model with less polyuria than tolvaptan. Healthy-volunteer Phase 1 dosing began on Oct 1, 2026; no patient data exist yet.

Why it is noted here: cAMP is a shared driver in ARPKD models too, and cAMP-lowering drugs (V2 blockers, octreotide) slowed kidney and liver cysts in the PCK rat. Open question for the community: would long-form PDE4 activation help cholangiocyte cysts without worsening fibrosis? A 2025 J Clin Invest study (Kim et al., PMID 41196648) found that a selective inhibitor of long PDE4D isoforms reduced bile-duct-ligation liver fibrosis in mice and halted established fibrosis in a diet model, acting on stellate cells and Kupffer cells (not cholangiocytes). That is the opposite direction from activating these enzymes. ⚠️ Limits: no ARPKD, liver or pediatric data; the company's numerical results for its clinical compound are not public.

Open Questions

Open Questions for the Research Community

These questions came out of this month's reading and are not answered in the sources we found. If your work addresses any of them — preliminary data, an unpublished observation, or a paper we missed — a brief reply is all we ask.

GENETICS & VARIANTS
Q1. Which PKHD1 variants cause liver-predominant disease? Cohorts so far (Burgmaier's zones, Zhao, Lapunzina-Soler, and Bhuva's nine congenital hepatic fibrosis cases) have not been pooled into one table of variant, position and phenotype. Do missense variants in the C-terminal ectodomain (Zone 3, AA 2625–4074) cluster in liver-predominant cases? A 2025 series of prenatally diagnosed Caroli-type cases found three of four missense variants in this region, but the numbers are tiny. An older French cohort (Denamur 2010) lists missense variants in the same region (W2736G, I2957T, R3240K) among children who died perinatally, so the region is not liver-only. Small case reports point both ways (Giacobbe 2022; and a prenatally diagnosed Caroli case with one missense variant in this region who needed kidney and liver transplants by 16 months, Rivas 2019).
Q2. Does an interior-exon missense variant of uncertain significance, such as c.10926G>A (p.Met3642Ile), change splicing? Precedents exist (R760H through an enhancer motif; a last-nucleotide missense with exon 40 skipping). Would a minigene assay or urine-cell RNA testing settle it? (A SpliceAI and Pangolin lookup on Oct 8 predicted no splice effect, with all scores 0.05 or lower; these tools miss enhancer-mediated skipping.)
Q3. Do modifier genes beyond PKHD1 explain the wide variation in ARPKD severity, including liver involvement? Does the UK PKD genetics fellowship's dataset include ARPKD patients? Candidates raised in the older literature, from animal work, include HNF1B, KIF12 and interactions with PKD1 and PKD2; human evidence is lacking. A 2018 mouse study found that a truncating Pkhd1 variant caused clinical biliary disease only on the NOD autoimmune background (Huang et al.).
DELIVERY & THERAPY
Q4. How can cholangiocytes be reached? Five clinical AAV capsids transduced them poorly in perfused human livers. Has anyone screened capsids or tried retrograde bile-duct delivery for cholangiocytes? A 2026 antibody report (pIgR-engaging bispecifics, abstract level) names ARPKD in its introduction as a disease hard to reach with circulating antibodies.
Q5. Is there a primary, citable report of AAV-associated liver failure in children with pre-existing cholangiopathy, and does it apply to congenital hepatic fibrosis? The 2020 editorial on the myotubular myopathy deaths describes liver toxicity at high dose but does not mention pre-existing cholangiopathy.
Q6. Would activating long-form PDE4 (LoAc® compounds) reduce cyst growth in ARPKD cholangiocytes without worsening stellate-cell fibrosis? Published PDE4D data point the other way for fibrosis.
BIOMARKERS & TRIALS
Q7. What liver endpoint could regulators accept for ARPKD-CHF: a stiffness trend, platelet count and spleen size, a blood marker, or an imaging tracer? What are the pediatric reference ranges, and how should values from different elastography methods be compared?
Q8. Is PKHD1 detectable in healthy children's cholangiocytes in the pediatric liver atlas, and do adult-derived findings (for example the OLR1+ macrophages) hold at a pediatric baseline?
MODELS & CELLS
Q9. Can urine-derived cells from children with ARPKD-CHF yield cholangiocyte or assembloid models, and which labs are set up to receive samples with proper consent? A 2018 study used urine-derived kidney epithelial cells to test splicing of ciliopathy-gene variants (Molinari et al., cited in a 2026 review).
IMMUNE–CHOLANGIOCYTE INTERACTIONS
Q10. Do PKHD1-deficient human cholangiocytes and ARPKD-CHF livers show immune interactions like the T cell–cholangiocyte CD226–NECTIN2 signal found in the Pkhd1-dependent NOD.c3c4 mouse? Is peribiliary T-cell infiltration part of ARPKD-CHF, and could immune-modulating antibodies be tested in that model?
CELL THERAPY
Q11. Could patient-derived, gene-corrected cholangiocyte organoids be delivered into the bile ducts of a Pkhd1-deficient model, and would the corrected cells engraft and persist in cystic, fibrotic ducts as they do in healthy or acutely injured ones?
