⭐⭐ PKHD1 missense variant at last exon nucleotide (-1G position) causes complete exon skipping in Caroli disease — UREC urine RNA testing method validated for PKHD1 variant reclassification — Gene (August 4, 2026 online / November 20, 2026 print) 📌 NEW · Oct 1, 2026

Huang H, Cheng C, Chen P, Yang F, Pan S, Xie F, Zhao S, Chen X (corresponding). "A novel PKHD1 missense variant disrupting splicing in a fetus with Caroli disease." Gene 1011:150340, November 20, 2026. Published online August 4, 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.gene.2026.150340. PMID: 42551719. ⚠️ NOT open access — Elsevier all rights reserved. ✅ Abstract and highlights confirmed October 1, 2026. Case Report.

Case: Fetus at 23 weeks gestation. Hepatic cysts on ultrasound, "central dot" sign on MRI (Caroli disease), ARPKD features, oligohydramnios. Exome sequencing: paternal nonsense c.5323C>T (Arg1775*); two maternal missense variants c.6682G>C (Glu2228Gln) and c.8012G>T (Arg2671Leu).

Key finding — c.6682G>C is the last nucleotide of exon 40 (-1G position): Minigene assay confirmed complete exon 40 skipping → in-frame deletion of 64 amino acids (Gly2164_Arg2227del) within a β-barrel domain of fibrocystin. AlphaFold3 predicts this deletion compromises β-barrel structural stability. The variant was initially annotated as missense (Glu2228Gln) but its primary effect is splice site disruption — not amino acid substitution.

Four highlights with direct ARPKD-CHF implications:

(1) Exonic last-nucleotide (-1G) variants cause severe splice disruption and exon skipping. This validates a variant class that is likely underdiagnosed across PKHD1 variant databases — many variants annotated as missense at exon-intron boundaries may be primarily splice disruptors.

(2) Systematic retrospective re-evaluation of unclassified PKHD1 VUS using splice-aware tools is needed. Any PKHD1 VUS with "predicted splice impact" annotation should be re-evaluated with SpliceAI and, where possible, functional RNA analysis.

(3) Endogenous RNA splicing in patient-derived primary cells (URECs/organoids) reveals tissue-specific penetrance. URECs (urine-derived renal epithelial cells) can be collected non-invasively from a urine sample and used to test whether a specific PKHD1 variant causes exon skipping in the patient's own cells. Tissue-specific penetrance means splicing may differ between kidney and liver cells — directly relevant to liver-dominant ARPKD-CHF.

(4) Structural and transcriptomic integration refines genotype-phenotype spectrum in ARPKD/Caroli disease.

⚠️ What this paper does NOT establish for our daughter's variant c.10926G>A (Met3642Ile): We confirmed October 1, 2026 that c.10926G>A is at genomic position chr6:51,659,200 (hg38) — confirmed from ClinVar primary database. This position is 268 bp interior to the exon that also contains c.10658 (Ile3553Thr, chr6:51,659,468). Because the genomic distance (268 bp) equals the CDS distance exactly, there are no introns between the two variants — they are in the same exon. Therefore c.10926G>A is NOT a -1G last-nucleotide splice variant of the class studied in Huang et al. It is located within the interior of its exon, not at the splice donor boundary. Its splice disruption (if real) would operate through ESE motif disruption — a different and less predictable mechanism. ClinVar: Conflicting classifications — all three reporting laboratories classify as VUS (uncertain significance). No published functional RNA data for c.10926G>A exists.

What the Huang paper still opens for c.10926G>A: Highlight 3 — UREC RNA testing — applies regardless of mechanism class. A urine sample from Gianna could directly test whether c.10926G>A causes exon skipping in her own cells, via endogenous RNA analysis without biopsy, iPSC reprogramming, or organoid culture. This is the most direct available experiment. Highlight 2 — systematic VUS re-evaluation — also applies: c.10926G>A has computational splice prediction and should be formally evaluated with SpliceAI (chr6:51,659,200 G>A lookup at spliceailookup.broadinstitute.org).

Companion paper: PMID 42697289 ("Structural and diagnostic implications of exonic splice-disrupting variants in PKHD1-related Caroli disease") refers to the Huang et al. case report — likely a review or commentary. Not yet indexed. New contact: Xinlin Chen (corresponding author of Huang et al.) — email not yet identified. Priority contact for UREC testing methodology.
