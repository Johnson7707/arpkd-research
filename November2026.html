Clarifications to Issue 3 (October 2026) CLARIFICATION · Oct 5, 2026

Base editing. Issue 3 described our daughter's variants as confirmed adenine base editor substrates. Both are transition mutations that adenine base editing can in principle correct, but guide design, editing window and PAM context have not been tested at either site, so "predicted" is the accurate word.

Gene insertion (HITI). Issue 3 presented the Ginn et al. approach as mutation-agnostic. The donor has to carry all coding sequence downstream of the insertion point. That is small for OTC but far beyond single-AAV capacity for PKHD1 (12.2 kb coding sequence), so the approach cannot be assumed to transfer. The authors also reported that many integration events were in more complex arrangements than predicted.

AAV safety. Issue 3 stated, without a citation, that liver-directed AAV has caused deaths in children with pre-existing cholangiopathy. The published record we have reviewed since reports fatal liver failure after high-dose AAV8 in X-linked myotubular myopathy (see the Kim et al. item above), and the 2026 meta-analysis above notes severe hepatobiliary complications in high-dose patients with pre-existing intrahepatic cholestasis. We are reading the primary reports and will restate the claim only with citations.

Noted (Tier 3 lead): CFTR assembles with the Hippo proteins TAZ and YAP in ADPKD mouse kidney; the CFTR corrector VX-809 reverses the pattern — ADPKD data, no ARPKD or biliary data yet 📌 NOTED · Oct 1, 2026

Sharma A, Afshani M, Ciobanu C, Sharma T, Kress JT, Guggino WB, Cebotaru L. "The ion channel, CFTR, assembles with HIPPO pathway proteins TAZ and YAP in polycystic kidney disease." J Biol Chem 302(7):113152, July 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.jbc.2026.113152. Open access (CC BY-NC-ND). ✅ Full text read October 1, 2026.

In Pkd1 R3277C mice and Pkd1-null kidney cells, CFTR co-immunoprecipitates with TAZ and YAP. In cystic epithelium all three accumulate at the apical membrane and nuclear TAZ/YAP is higher; VX-809 (lumacaftor) moves them back toward the normal basolateral pattern and lowers nuclear TAZ/YAP. Why it is noted here: the same group's ARPKD cholangiocyte study (Sharma, Cebotaru et al., Cell Calcium, in press) showed CFTR correctors reducing cholangiocyte proliferation and cyst growth. Whether the CFTR–TAZ/YAP link exists in Pkhd1-deficient cholangiocytes has not been tested. ⚠️ Limits: ADPKD mouse model, kidney only, correlative binding and imaging data; the authors' causal wording goes further than the data.

Noted (Tier 3, resource): open single-cell atlas of the healthy pediatric liver — children's Kupffer-like cells are more immune-active than adults'; cholangiocyte results not reported 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Edgar RD, Nakib D, Camat D, et al. Hepatol Commun 9(11):e0813, 2025. DOI: 10.1097/HC9.0000000000000813 (open access; full text read). 42,660 cells from 9 healthy pediatric donors (ages 1–19) versus 26,372 adult cells, plus intestinal-failure liver disease biopsies. Pediatric Kupffer-like cells express more CCL3, CCL4 and IL1B than adult ones (IL-1β secretion confirmed). Used as a comparator, the atlas also showed higher MHC I/II signaling in cholangiocytes from biliary atresia and choledochal cysts than in healthy pediatric cells, and one spatial sample captured a bile duct hamartoma (data to be released). Why it is noted: adult-derived atlases lack a pediatric baseline. ⚠️ Limits: healthy donors only; cholangiocyte numbers and PKHD1 detection are not reported. Data are open (CELLxGENE, Human Cell Atlas portal).

Noted (Tier 4, adjacent): a sympathetic nerve signal, NPY, drives pro-fibrotic macrophage states in two mouse liver fibrosis models, one of them cholestatic 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Lin Q, Zheng X, et al. Cell Mol Life Sci 83(1):346, 2026 (DOI 10.1007/s00018-026-06428-2, as given in a news summary; the paper itself was not read). NPY rose in human and mouse fibrotic livers and acted through macrophage NPY1R/NPY2R and PI3K–PAK1; mice lacking NPY, or with the receptors silenced, had less fibrosis in carbon tetrachloride and DDC (cholestatic) models. The authors say a direct effect on stellate cells is not validated. Why it is noted: adds a nerve-derived signal to the macrophage-driven fibrosis thread. ⚠️ Limits: mouse chemical-injury models; untested in ARPKD; no stress or diet inference follows.

🧪 Gene Therapy & Base Editing

🧪 GENE THERAPY & BASE EDITING

⭐⭐ Human-liver AAV capsid screening on machine perfusion: the standard clinical capsids reach hepatocytes well and cholangiocytes poorly — Nature Biotechnology (published January 2025; added to our tracking October 2, 2026) 📌 NEW · Oct 2, 2026

Kim JJ, Kurial SNT, et al. (senior author H. Willenbring, UCSF). "AAV capsid prioritization in normal and steatotic human livers maintained by machine perfusion." Nat Biotechnol 43(12):1966–1978, 2025. DOI: 10.1038/s41587-024-02523-6. Open access (CC BY). ✅ Full text read October 2, 2026. Tier 2.

Method: Barcoded AAV vectors were infused through the portal vein of discarded adult human livers kept alive on normothermic machine perfusion, and the transduced cells were profiled by single-cell RNA sequencing. AAV8 was studied in two normal livers; AAV8, AAV5, AAV-LK03, AAV6 and AAV-NP59 were compared in four livers (two normal, two steatotic).

Findings: All five capsids transduced mainly hepatocytes, and cholangiocytes showed low transduction. AAV-LK03 was the most hepatocyte-specific. AAV8 also reached Kupffer cells (up to 8.6%) and sinusoidal endothelial cells. In fatty livers the vector formed fewer concatemers, so strategies that depend on DNA recombination — dual-vector delivery of large genes and Cas9 with AAV repair templates — may work less well there.

Why it matters for ARPKD-CHF: this is the first single-cell evidence in intact human liver that the standard clinical capsids reach cholangiocytes poorly. Biliary delivery therefore needs new tools: capsids engineered or screened for cholangiocytes, or a different route. The authors themselves propose using this platform to screen capsid libraries for delivery to cholangiocytes in inherited biliary disease. The paper also notes fatal liver failure after high-dose AAV8 in X-linked myotubular myopathy.

⚠️ Limits: discarded adult livers, two to four per comparison, portal-vein delivery only. No cholangiopathic liver was studied and retrograde delivery through the bile duct was not tested. Low cholangiocyte transduction in normal human liver does not show how cyst-lining biliary epithelium would behave.

Noted (Tier 3 context): meta-analysis of immunosuppression in clinical AAV gene therapy — 22 liver-directed trials show frequent antibody responses and liver enzyme rises despite steroids; no regimen shown superior 📌 NOTED · Oct 5, 2026

Wang Z, Wu N, Fu D, Zhao Q, Wang H. "A systematic review and meta-analysis of immunosuppressive regimens and immune-related outcomes in adeno-associated virus-based gene therapy." Sci Rep 2026 (Article in Press; published online October 1, 2026). DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-71872-w. Open access (CC BY 4.0). ✅ Full text read October 5, 2026.

73 studies and 1,773 participants. The 22 liver-directed studies (455 participants, all intravenous, mostly hemophilia) pooled to a 75.8% humoral immune response, 49.5% elevated ALT and 26.0% cellular response. Corticosteroids were used in 71 of the 73 studies, and the authors found no regimen shown to be superior; certainty was very low for most outcomes and most studies were single-arm. Why it is noted here: a benchmark for what systemic AAV does even in adults with normal bile ducts. ⚠️ Limits: no biliary disease, no children's liver disease and no gene editing were examined, so applicability to ARPKD-CHF is untested.

Noted (Tier 5, context): PKD Foundation research webinar — PKD1 is too large for AAV, so the field is testing protein fragments and base editors 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Webinar transcript with Dr. Michael Caplan (Yale) and Dr. Xiaogang Li (Mayo); no peer-reviewed source. Published or stated points only: the PKD1 coding sequence is about 13 kb against an AAV capacity near 4.5 kb. Caplan's lab expresses only the 200-amino-acid polycystin-1 C-terminal tail (about 600 bp, fits in AAV); the benefit was seen only in mice that carry the mitochondrial enzyme NNT. Li reported kidney base-editing efficiency of about 10–15% with a universal promoter and 20–25% with a kidney-specific one in mice, durable for 6 months, with under 1% bystander editing. Both stressed that all results are in mice and that early treatment matters. Why it is noted: a precedent for fibrocystin-fragment delivery and a benchmark for editing efficiency. Unpublished results mentioned in the webinar are intentionally not recorded here.

Also seen (headline-level, abstracts not read): gene therapy in other diseases — a mechanism-based strategy review, a vascular gene therapy, a recessive-disease precedent, and the monitoring and readiness side of AAV treatment 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Listed with the connection I could find for each; none involves ARPKD. (1) Zhang et al., Mol Ther Nucleic Acids 2026, a review of gene therapy for hereditary neuromuscular diseases, organizes strategies by mechanism: loss-of-function disorders by gene replacement, gene editing or RNA-based correction, and gain-of-function disorders by silencing or removing the toxic product. Connection: ARPKD is loss of function, so the same three options apply; from general knowledge (not the snippet), the muscular dystrophy field also supplies the best-known precedent for delivering a shortened working version of a gene too large for AAV, the logic behind the polycystin-1 and fibrocystin fragment ideas. I could not locate the Zhang review itself; a 2026 review in Front Cell Dev Biol (DOI 10.3389/fcell.2026.1765367) states the same framework and notes that AAV gene replacement suits small genes with recessive loss of function (SMN1, SGCA, FKRP), which PKHD1 is not. (2) Bogdan et al., 2026: a VEGF165/Ang-1 plasmid increased capillary density in rat limb ischemia over 28 days. Connection: proof that gene delivery can rebuild vessels, relevant to the framework's idea that epithelial failure leads to vascular failure and scarring; the caution is that new vessels also feed portal hypertension, so the direction of any vascular approach in the liver is unresolved. (3) "Night Blindness–Retinitis Pigmentosa" reference entry: voretigene neparvovec is approved for biallelic RPE65 disease, and in its phase 3 trial 65% of treated patients completed a low-light mobility course. Connection: an approved gene replacement for a biallelic recessive disease, delivered locally to a small organ with a small gene, the opposite of PKHD1's size and access problem. (4) Wang et al., Clin Chem 2026 (B-216): validating a total antibody test for AAV9. Connection: pre-existing anti-AAV antibodies reduce delivery and add safety risk, and screening assays are a prerequisite for any AAV trial; this one is for AAV9, not the capsids discussed here. (5) Sobolak et al., Clin Chem 2026: troponin assays for cardiac monitoring during SMA and Duchenne gene therapy, which the FDA recommends. Connection: shows what an AAV safety-monitoring panel includes beyond liver enzymes. (6) Garcia Uzquiano et al., Front Med: 67.6% of pediatric neurologists were strongly willing to adopt Duchenne gene therapy, with clinical-readiness barriers. Connection: a template for asking pediatric hepatologists and nephrologists the same question about ARPKD. (7) Tattan, analytical strategy for gene therapies, and Rodriguez Lopez et al., 2026 (carbon footprint of hemophilia B gene therapy versus lifelong prophylaxis): development-analytics and one-time-versus-lifelong assessment frameworks that any future ARPKD program would need. Two other items in the alert had no connection I could find.

🏥 Clinical & Biomarkers

🏥 CLINICAL & BIOMARKERS

⭐⭐ PKHD1 missense variant at last exon nucleotide (-1G position) causes complete exon skipping in Caroli disease — UREC urine RNA testing method validated for PKHD1 variant reclassification — Gene (August 4, 2026 online / November 20, 2026 print) 📌 NEW · Oct 1, 2026

Huang H, Cheng C, Chen P, Yang F, Pan S, Xie F, Zhao S, Chen X (corresponding). "A novel PKHD1 missense variant disrupting splicing in a fetus with Caroli disease." Gene 1011:150340, November 20, 2026. Published online August 4, 2026. DOI: 10.1016/j.gene.2026.150340. PMID: 42551719. ⚠️ NOT open access — Elsevier all rights reserved. ✅ Abstract and highlights confirmed October 1, 2026. Case Report.

Case: Fetus at 23 weeks gestation. Hepatic cysts on ultrasound, "central dot" sign on MRI (Caroli disease), ARPKD features, oligohydramnios. Exome sequencing: paternal nonsense c.5323C>T (Arg1775*); two maternal missense variants c.6682G>C (Glu2228Gln) and c.8012G>T (Arg2671Leu).

Key finding — c.6682G>C is the last nucleotide of exon 40 (-1G position): Minigene assay confirmed complete exon 40 skipping → in-frame deletion of 64 amino acids (Gly2164_Arg2227del) within a β-barrel domain of fibrocystin. AlphaFold3 predicts this deletion compromises β-barrel structural stability. The variant was initially annotated as missense (Glu2228Gln) but its primary effect is splice site disruption — not amino acid substitution.

Four highlights with direct ARPKD-CHF implications:

(1) Exonic last-nucleotide (-1G) variants cause severe splice disruption and exon skipping. This validates a variant class that is likely underdiagnosed across PKHD1 variant databases — many variants annotated as missense at exon-intron boundaries may be primarily splice disruptors.

(2) Systematic retrospective re-evaluation of unclassified PKHD1 VUS using splice-aware tools is needed. Any PKHD1 VUS with "predicted splice impact" annotation should be re-evaluated with SpliceAI and, where possible, functional RNA analysis.

(3) Endogenous RNA splicing in patient-derived primary cells (URECs/organoids) reveals tissue-specific penetrance. URECs (urine-derived renal epithelial cells) can be collected non-invasively from a urine sample and used to test whether a specific PKHD1 variant causes exon skipping in the patient's own cells. Tissue-specific penetrance means splicing may differ between kidney and liver cells — directly relevant to liver-dominant ARPKD-CHF.

(4) Structural and transcriptomic integration refines genotype-phenotype spectrum in ARPKD/Caroli disease.

⚠️ What this paper does NOT establish for our daughter's variant c.10926G>A (Met3642Ile): We confirmed October 1, 2026 that c.10926G>A is at genomic position chr6:51,659,200 (hg38) — confirmed from ClinVar primary database. This position is 268 bp interior to the exon that also contains c.10658 (Ile3553Thr, chr6:51,659,468). Because the genomic distance (268 bp) equals the CDS distance exactly, there are no introns between the two variants — they are in the same exon. Therefore c.10926G>A is NOT a -1G last-nucleotide splice variant of the class studied in Huang et al. It is located within the interior of its exon, not at the splice donor boundary. Its splice disruption (if real) would operate through ESE motif disruption — a different and less predictable mechanism. ClinVar: Conflicting classifications — all three reporting laboratories classify as VUS (uncertain significance). No published functional RNA data for c.10926G>A exists.

What the Huang paper still opens for c.10926G>A: Highlight 3 — UREC RNA testing — applies regardless of mechanism class. A urine sample from our daughter could directly test whether c.10926G>A causes exon skipping in her own cells, via endogenous RNA analysis without biopsy, iPSC reprogramming, or organoid culture. This is the most direct available experiment. Highlight 2 — systematic VUS re-evaluation — also applies: c.10926G>A has computational splice prediction and should be formally evaluated with SpliceAI (chr6:51,659,200 G>A lookup at spliceailookup.broadinstitute.org).

Companion paper: PMID 42697289 ("Structural and diagnostic implications of exonic splice-disrupting variants in PKHD1-related Caroli disease") refers to the Huang et al. case report — likely a review or commentary. Not yet indexed. New contact: Xinlin Chen (corresponding author of Huang et al.) — email not yet identified. Priority contact for UREC testing methodology.

Noted (Tier 4, conference abstract — not peer reviewed): two-year follow-up of liver stiffness by transient elastography in children with cystic kidney disease — 7 of 9 ARPKD children had raised stiffness and 3 progressed 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Jacobs A, Kapil S, Morlan W, Gupta S, Molleston J (Indiana University / Riley Hospital for Children). Abstract 80, JPGN Reports 7(S2):S55, 2026 (supplement DOI 10.1002/jpr3.70233). 28 children with cystic kidney disease (9 ARPKD); FibroScan at enrollment and 2 years. Seven of nine ARPKD children had stiffness more than 2 SD above the mean (7.3 to 32.7 kPa); three progressed (32.7→59.8, 18.3→46.8, 13.1→17.8 kPa), two with worsening platelet counts. One nephronophthisis child with very high stiffness died of a ruptured splenic artery pseudoaneurysm. ⚠️ Limits: nine ARPKD patients at one center identified by billing codes; the reference for "more than 2 SD above the mean" is not stated; do not compare these kPa values with MR elastography values. No full paper yet.

Noted (Tier 3): first-in-human dosimetry of a PET tracer for active fibrosis (PDGFRβ) — not yet tested in biliary disease or children 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Lubberink M, Lohith TG, Eriksson O, et al. "Biodistribution and radiation dosimetry of the PDGFRβ targeting PET tracer [68Ga]Ga-ATH001." Eur J Nucl Med Mol Imaging, Short Communication, open access, Sept 8, 2026. DOI: 10.1007/s00259-026-08167-9 (NCT06562361; DOI and date confirmed on the journal page). The tracer binds PDGFRβ on activated stellate cells and pericytes. ✅ Full text read Oct 8, 2026: six participants (three healthy volunteers, all 22 years old, and three men aged 54–65 with MASH); liver uptake at 2 hours was higher in MASH (SUV 4.0 versus 2.8); the kidneys were the dose-limiting organ (about 50 mGy at the 2 MBq/kg target, effective dose 4.1 mSv); the spleen also stayed strongly avid; no adverse effects were recorded to 24 hours. Earlier preclinical work (EJNMMI Radiopharm Chem 2025, DOI 10.1186/s41181-025-00410-2) showed tracer binding on human liver sections tracked PDGFRβ staining and separated fibrotic from healthy liver. Why it is noted: a possible readout of fibrosis activity alongside collagen MRI and elastography. ⚠️ Limits: tiny adult cohort; no biliary or pediatric data; kidney radiation dose is a concern in kidney disease; funded by Takeda and Antaros, with employees of both among the authors; further MASH stages are being studied in the ongoing trial.

Noted (Tier 2, abstract only): genetics of isolated ductal plate malformation in 55 adults — congenital hepatic fibrosis cases carried only PKHD1 variants, about half missense 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Bhuva M, Thompson E, Watson CM, Sava F, Griffiths WJH. Dig Dis Sci 71:5137–5151, 2026. DOI: 10.1007/s10620-026-10072-y (Brief Report; online June 28, 2026; Cambridge Liver Unit with Leeds and Sheffield genetics services). DOI, authors and dates confirmed on the journal page; the full text is paywalled, so the abstract was read and the full text was not. The authors' contributions note a long-read variant analysis. 54 of 55 patients without polycystic kidney disease were sequenced: 40 polycystic liver disease, 9 congenital hepatic fibrosis, 1 Caroli, 5 multiple biliary hamartomas. Pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants were found in 31. Polycystic liver disease was mainly SEC63, PRKCSH and GANAB with a minor PKHD1 and ALG8 contribution; congenital hepatic fibrosis came solely from PKHD1 variants. Five novel variants were reported (four SEC63, one PRKCSH), none in PKHD1. ⚠️ Limits: adults (mean age 47, 78% female), single center, retrospective, kidney disease excluded by design.

Noted (Tier 4, adjacent): adult cirrhosis prevention guideline and an MRI-based MASH diagnostic meta-analysis — benchmarks for composite noninvasive scores, not for children with congenital hepatic fibrosis 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

American College of Gastroenterology release, Oct 6, 2026 (link; papers not read). The guideline (DOI 10.14309/ajg.0000000000004186) gives strong recommendations on variceal prevention, twice-yearly liver cancer surveillance, and hepatitis A, influenza, COVID-19 and RSV vaccination in adults with cirrhosis. The meta-analysis (DOI 10.14309/ajg.0000000000003919) found fibroscan-plus-AST performed best for at-risk MASH, with MR elastography plus FIB-4 also performing well. Why it is noted: a template for combining stiffness with blood tests, and a reminder that vaccination matters before any immunosuppressive treatment. ⚠️ Limits: adult cirrhosis and MASH only.

Also seen (headline-level, abstracts not read): noninvasive fibrosis measurement in adults — a device-versus-blood-test comparison, score comparisons and population cutoffs 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Listed for completeness; none involves children, biliary disease or ARPKD. (1) Zuo et al., PLoS One 2026: FibroTouch (a transient elastography device) compared with serum CHI3L1 for liver fibrosis in Chinese patients with chronic liver disease, with a cost comparison. (2) Schulte Farina et al., GE Port J Gastroenterol 2026: five clinical scores compared for early fibrosis in obese MASLD. (3) Orahoske et al., Clin Chem 2026 (abstract A-161): how cardiovascular disease and diabetes relate to the ELF score. (4) Hindson, Nat Rev Gastroenterol Hepatol 2026, on the CHESS-LiverHealth study: a stiffness of 8 kPa or more in 4.4% of adults in the Chinese community. Noted (Tier 3, abstracts read): minigene studies keep finding PKHD1 variants that change splicing — including missense variants — in Caroli disease and ARPKD fetuses 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Huang et al., Front Genet 2025 (DOI 10.3389/fgene.2025.1651306, open access): two Chinese fetuses with Caroli disease or syndrome (full text read); in one, the intronic variant c.3364+3A>T caused exon 29 skipping with a frameshift in minigene assays (HEK293T and HepG2 cells), paired with the missense c.7912T>A (p.Tyr2638Asn). Three of the four missense variants in prenatally diagnosed Caroli-type cases fall in the C-terminal region AA 2625–4074, which an earlier cohort linked to poorer liver outcomes (very small numbers). Miao et al., Front Genet 2023 (DOI 10.3389/fgene.2023.1207772): a novel intronic variant (c.2408-13C>G, 13 bases before exon 24) in an ARPKD fetus retained 12 bp of intron 23 in minigene assays, predicting an in-frame four-amino-acid insertion whose effect on the protein the authors say is only hypothesized; the fetus also carried a known frameshift variant, and autopsy at 30 weeks showed cystic kidneys with no definite liver abnormality (full text read, open access). Both add to the same group's 2026 report of exon 40 skipping from a last-nucleotide missense variant (above). Why it is noted: missense and near-splice variants are not always what they look like, and the minigene method that tests them is established; a missense variant of uncertain significance inside an exon could be tested the same way. ⚠️ Limits: fetal case reports; the Huang 2025 and Miao 2023 full texts were read, the others only as abstracts; minigenes were run in non-biliary cell lines; no test of any specific uncertain variant here.

Noted (Tier 2, published 2010; added to our tracking this month): in 74 fetuses and early-demise neonates with ARPKD, two truncating PKHD1 variants predicted kidney severity but not liver fibrosis 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Denamur E, Delezoide A-L, Alberti C, et al., Kidney Int 77:350–358, 2010 (DOI 10.1038/ki.2009.440; full text read). French cohort of 74 histologically confirmed cases (54 terminated fetuses and 20 neonatal deaths). Two truncating variants were more frequent in earlier terminations (53% at or before 30 weeks vs 16% after) and went with more extensive collecting-duct dilatation in the kidney cortex. Portal fibrosis was present in all 61 livers and rose with gestational age (grade 3 in 4% at or before 30 weeks vs 40% after), but did not differ significantly by genotype class. Sixteen neonates without two truncating variants still died, carrying ten different missense variants. The authors raise modifier genes (HNF1B, KIF12, and interactions with PKD1 and PKD2 seen in animal models) as a possible explanation for variable severity. Why it is noted: liver fibrosis is already substantial before birth in severe cases, which supports an early treatment window, and truncating-versus-not does not predict liver severity. Several missense variants in the C-terminal region (AA 2625–4074) appear among these perinatal deaths, so that region is not simply liver-predominant. ⚠️ Limits: a selected severe perinatal cohort, histology only, no long-term liver outcomes, a binary genotype classification, and 2010 data.

Noted (Tier 3, published 2022; added to our tracking this month): three children with Caroli syndrome carried missense PKHD1 variants and developed portal hypertension early — one needed a liver transplant 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Giacobbe C, Di Dato F, Palma D, et al., Mol Genet Genomic Med 10:e1998, 2022 (DOI 10.1002/mgg3.1998, open access; full text read). Two Moroccan siblings carried the same two missense variants (p.Asn901Thr and p.Arg1624Trp); the boy had portal hypertension, varices and stage 3 kidney disease by 9.7 years, while his younger sister at 5 years had liver enlargement and normal kidney function. An Italian girl with an uncertain complex allele of three missense variants plus a likely pathogenic missense variant had portal hypertension at 13 and a liver transplant at 14; after transplant her kidney function dipped on tacrolimus and recovered when she was switched to sirolimus (a single observation). Platelet counts fell in all three children. Why it is noted: missense-only genotypes can give an early and severe liver course, and a complex allele of uncertain variants illustrates why functional testing of unclassified variants matters. ⚠️ Limits: three patients, a targeted gene panel and no functional work; the sirolimus observation is anecdotal and not advice.

Noted (regulatory context, indirect relevance): FDA replaces "animal tests" with "nonclinical tests" in its drug and biologic regulations — patient-derived models may count for more 📌 NOTED · Oct 8, 2026

On September 21, 2026 the FDA announced a direct final rule that replaces "animal tests/studies" with "nonclinical tests/studies" in its drug and biologic regulations. The FDA states that it adds no new requirements and sets no acceptance thresholds; a trade summary gives an effective date of February 2027, and the FDA also released a database of 25 retrospective examples of new approach methodologies. Source: the FDA announcement as excerpted in search results and a PharmTech summary (Oct 6, 2026); the primary page was not opened. Why it is noted here: it may make patient-derived organoid and cell models more usable as nonclinical evidence for rare-disease therapies — our inference; the announcement says nothing about ARPKD or gene therapy. ⚠️ Limits: a wording change, not an approval pathway; animal studies are not banned.

Noted (Tier 5, context): a perspective argues liver-on-a-chip models fail by copying the adult liver instead of recreating development 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Kamei K. Commun Biol 9:1204, Sept 16, 2026 (DOI 10.1038/s42003-026-10887-5; open access). No new data and no ARPKD or biliary content. It names patient iPSCs plus gene editing (matched edited and unedited pairs) as the route for variant studies. Why it is noted: framing for patient-derived modeling of a developmental disease such as ductal plate malformation.

💊 THERAPEUTICS & DRUG CANDIDATES

Noted (status check): tolvaptan in pediatric ARPKD — one trial recruiting, one withdrawn 📌 NOTED · Oct 1, 2026

Study 307 (NCT04782258; ages 28 days to under 18 years; open-label; sponsor Otsuka) is listed as recruiting, with primary completion estimated July 2028 and the registry record last updated August 7, 2026. Study 204 (NCT04786574; infants 28 days to under 12 weeks) is listed as withdrawn — stopped before enrolling any participants (registry status verified December 2025). A 2023 design paper noted that the effect of tolvaptan on liver function in the ARPKD population is unknown (BMC Nephrol 2023, DOI: 10.1186/s12882-023-03072-x). Sources: ClinicalTrials.gov registry records and the sponsor's trial listing.

Noted (Tier 4, adjacent): LoAc® small molecules that activate long-form PDE4 enzymes to lower cAMP — Phase 1 started in ADPKD; not yet tested in ARPKD or in liver 📌 NOTED · Oct 8, 2026

Omar F, Findlay JE, Carfray G, et al. "Small-molecule allosteric activators of PDE4 long form cyclic AMP phosphodiesterases." PNAS 116(27):13320–13329, 2019. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1822113116 (open access), plus a Mironid press release of Oct 1, 2026 and 2024 conference abstracts. The company's compounds bind the regulatory interface of long-form PDE4 enzymes and speed cAMP breakdown. ✅ Full text read Oct 8, 2026 (authors are mostly Mironid employees): the 2019 paper showed that the prototype activator raised long-form PDE4 activity by about 60%, lowered cAMP and CFTR-dependent signaling, and reduced cysts in dog kidney cells, a human ADPKD cell line and primary cells from an ADPKD patient's kidney cysts, while a PDE4 inhibitor made cysts larger; all data are kidney, ADPKD-type and in vitro. Conference abstracts report that an oral compound reduced cysts and MRI kidney volume in an ADPKD mouse model with less polyuria than tolvaptan. Healthy-volunteer Phase 1 dosing began on Oct 1, 2026; no patient data exist yet.

Why it is noted here: cAMP is a shared driver in ARPKD models too, and cAMP-lowering drugs (V2 blockers, octreotide) slowed kidney and liver cysts in the PCK rat. Open question for the community: would long-form PDE4 activation help cholangiocyte cysts without worsening fibrosis? A 2025 J Clin Invest study (Kim et al., PMID 41196648) found that a selective inhibitor of long PDE4D isoforms reduced bile-duct-ligation liver fibrosis in mice and halted established fibrosis in a diet model, acting on stellate cells and Kupffer cells (not cholangiocytes). That is the opposite direction from activating these enzymes. ⚠️ Limits: no ARPKD, liver or pediatric data; the company's numerical results for its clinical compound are not public.

Noted (Tier 4, company report, ADPKD only): PYC-003, a drug designed to raise PC1 protein, reports early human urinary PC1 data ahead of ASN Kidney Week on Oct 22 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

BiotechDispatch, Oct 8, 2026. In seven patients with PKD1 mutations, urinary PC1 rose 46% from baseline 14 days after a single dose (three at 1.2 mg/kg, four at 2.4 mg/kg; pooled). Kidney volume was measured in 18 patients at 3 months against a historical benchmark of 925 untreated patients; the article does not report that result. Multiple-dose data are expected in 2027, with regulator talks about a registrational Phase 2/3. Why it is noted: a urinary target-engagement marker and an accelerated-approval route, neither yet matched for ARPKD. A protein-raising drug assumes a working allele, which ARPKD lacks (our inference). ⚠️ Limits: small, single-dose, company-reported.

Noted (Tier 4, company report): CSL pays $355 million upfront for lixudebart, an anti-claudin-1 antibody, in kidney and liver fibrotic disease including primary sclerosing cholangitis 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

CSL and Alentis release, Oct 5, 2026. Lixudebart (formerly ALE.F02) targets exposed claudin-1, described as a driver of inflammatory and fibrotic signaling. Lead indication: a rare autoimmune kidney disease (Phase 2); FSGS and primary sclerosing cholangitis are also planned. In a 41-patient Phase 1b trial in advanced liver fibrosis, liver function improved at six weeks (company statement; disease cause not given). Why it is noted: a late-stage antifibrotic that includes a bile duct disease, and claudin-1 is a tight-junction protein. ⚠️ Limits: no ARPKD or PKHD1 data; autoimmune, adult disease.

Noted (Tier 4, adjacent): Japan approves sodium phenylbutyrate for PFIC types 1 and 2 — raising working bile salt pump at the cell membrane 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Wire report, Oct 7, 2026, quoting Hisamitsu Hayashi (University of Tokyo) (link; primary studies not read). Approval came in August 2026 on small studies: six PFIC2 patients with histology improved in three, four still on treatment with their own livers after 6.5 to 8 years, and three PFIC1 patients with itch relief that returned when the drug stopped; no controlled comparison. Symptom-focused drugs (ileal bile acid transporter inhibitors) do not stop fibrosis. Why it is noted: a protein-trafficking correction approach in a pediatric cholestatic disease, comparable in logic to the CFTR-corrector thread. ⚠️ Limits: not an ARPKD treatment signal.

Noted (Tier 4, preclinical): a long-acting FXR agonist depot reduces fibrosis in two mouse models — no biliary safety readouts 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Sultana A, Bybee G, Pathania AS, et al. Biomolecules 16(10):1443, Oct 3, 2026. DOI: 10.3390/biom16101443 (open access; full text read). A stearyl-ester prodrug of vonafexor, formulated as a monthly intramuscular depot, reduced fibrosis markers in carbon tetrachloride-injured mice and in hepatitis B-transgenic mice (small groups). ⚠️ Limits: no bile acid, ALP or bile duct readouts; no FXR-knockout control; no in-vivo comparison with the parent drug; a hepatocyte toxin model, not a biliary one. Whether sustained-release FXR agonism is safe in cholangiopathy is untested.

Noted (Tier 5, conceptual): CAR T cells aimed at uPAR cleared senescent cells and reduced established liver fibrosis in mice — not tested in biliary or ARPKD fibrosis 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Amor C, et al. Nature 583:127–132, 2020. DOI: 10.1038/s41586-020-2403-9. uPAR-directed CAR T cells removed senescent cells and improved liver function in toxin and NASH-diet mouse models, with cytokine-release-like toxicity at high doses; a 2025 follow-up reported persistence up to 12 months in mice. Why it is noted: shows the idea of clearing fibrogenic cells by engineered immune cells. ⚠️ Limits: uPAR has not been measured in ARPKD liver; the approach needs conditioning chemotherapy and is a research concept only, not an option for children.

Noted (Tier 3, sponsor-funded, adult MASH): resmetirom's effect did not differ significantly across five common MASH risk genotypes — within the study's limited power 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

Chalasani NP, Taub R, Mogg R, Labriola D, Camacho RC, Zhang H, Anstee QM. J Hepatol, journal pre-proof (received Sept 23, 2025; accepted Aug 30, 2026; a sponsor release dates publication Oct 5, 2026). DOI: 10.1016/j.jhep.2026.08.037. ✅ Full text read (pre-proof). Funded by Madrigal; five of the seven authors are Madrigal employees. In 738 genotyped participants of the MAESTRO-NASH phase 3 trial (non-cirrhotic MASH, fibrosis stages F1B to F3), the effect on MASH resolution, fibrosis improvement and MRI fat reduction did not differ significantly by PNPLA3, HSD17B13, TM6SF2, MTARC1 or MBOAT7 risk genotype, or by a combined genetic risk score. For example, fibrosis improvement across PNPLA3 low, intermediate and high risk was 34.2%, 29.5% and 31.4% with resmetirom versus 14.1%, 13.7% and 15.0% with placebo (both doses pooled). The authors also note that people with higher PNPLA3 genetic risk had fewer metabolic risk factors at baseline, suggesting that higher genetic risk lowers the metabolic burden needed to reach the same fibrosis stage.

Why it is noted: a worked example of asking whether common modifier genes change a treatment's effect, the question the PKD genetics fellowship raises for ARPKD, and of a threshold-style idea that genetic risk lowers the second hit needed. ⚠️ Limits: exploratory analysis with nominal p-values; the authors say it can rule out only relatively large interactions and cannot rule out effects in rarer genotypes; SERPINA1 was not analyzed; no clinical outcomes; adult MASH, not a biliary or ciliopathy disease. ARPKD trials will be far smaller, so a similar analysis there would be underpowered (our inference).

Also seen (headline-level, abstracts not read): preclinical antifibrotic reports and a hormone-signaling review 📌 NOTED · Oct 2026

(1) Sun et al., Food Funct 2026: phenethyl isothiocyanate, a plant compound, reduced carbon tetrachloride fibrosis in mice by inducing ferroptosis in activated stellate cells. Connection: an earlier mouse study found ferroptosis promotes cyst growth in ADPKD, so the same mechanism could cut opposite ways in cysts and fibrosis (our inference); no dietary inference follows. (2) Mohammad et al. (PDF, 2026): the diabetes drug sitagliptin reduced fibrosis in bile-duct-ligated rats through anti-inflammatory and Nrf2 effects. Connection: a repurposing report in a cholestatic fibrosis model; small studies of this kind are common in that model. (3) Bushra et al., ACS Nano Med: an exosome-bile-salt-vesicle oxygen nanobubble tested in multilineage liver spheroids. Connection: extracellular-vesicle delivery and spheroid fibrosis models. (4) Yanum et al., Biochem Pharmacol 2026: review of prolactin receptor signaling in liver disease. Connection: another hormonal axis alongside estrogen's role in cystic liver disease. All are animal or cell studies; none involves ARPKD.

Open Questions

Open Questions for the Research Community

These questions came out of this month's reading and are not answered in the sources we found. If your work addresses any of them — preliminary data, an unpublished observation, or a paper we missed — a brief reply is all we ask.

GENETICS & VARIANTS
Q1. Which PKHD1 variants cause liver-predominant disease? Cohorts so far (Burgmaier's zones, Zhao, Lapunzina-Soler, and Bhuva's nine congenital hepatic fibrosis cases) have not been pooled into one table of variant, position and phenotype. Do missense variants in the C-terminal ectodomain (Zone 3, AA 2625–4074) cluster in liver-predominant cases? A 2025 series of prenatally diagnosed Caroli-type cases found three of four missense variants in this region, but the numbers are tiny. An older French cohort (Denamur 2010) lists missense variants in the same region (W2736G, I2957T, R3240K) among children who died perinatally, so the region is not liver-only. Small postnatal Caroli-syndrome series (Giacobbe 2022) point both ways.
Q2. Does an interior-exon missense variant of uncertain significance, such as c.10926G>A (p.Met3642Ile), change splicing? Precedents exist (R760H through an enhancer motif; a last-nucleotide missense with exon 40 skipping). Would a minigene assay or urine-cell RNA testing settle it?
Q3. Do modifier genes beyond PKHD1 explain the wide variation in ARPKD severity, including liver involvement? Does the UK PKD genetics fellowship's dataset include ARPKD patients? Candidates raised in the older literature, from animal work, include HNF1B, KIF12 and interactions with PKD1 and PKD2; human evidence is lacking.
DELIVERY & THERAPY
Q4. How can cholangiocytes be reached? Five clinical AAV capsids transduced them poorly in perfused human livers. Has anyone screened capsids or tried retrograde bile-duct delivery for cholangiocytes?
Q5. Is there a primary, citable report of AAV-associated liver failure in children with pre-existing cholangiopathy, and does it apply to congenital hepatic fibrosis?
Q6. Would activating long-form PDE4 (LoAc® compounds) reduce cyst growth in ARPKD cholangiocytes without worsening stellate-cell fibrosis? Published PDE4D data point the other way for fibrosis.
BIOMARKERS & TRIALS
Q7. What liver endpoint could regulators accept for ARPKD-CHF: a stiffness trend, platelet count and spleen size, a blood marker, or an imaging tracer? What are the pediatric reference ranges, and how should values from different elastography methods be compared?
Q8. Is PKHD1 detectable in healthy children's cholangiocytes in the pediatric liver atlas, and do adult-derived findings (for example the OLR1+ macrophages) hold at a pediatric baseline?
MODELS & CELLS
Q9. Can urine-derived cells from children with ARPKD-CHF yield cholangiocyte or assembloid models, and which labs are set up to receive samples with proper consent?
